Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians II, 3-15)
2 Corinthians II, 3 — 'ἀφ’ ὧν' — 'aph hon' — from those. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following 'ho' indicates here that the apostle would find a cause for joy, rather than they themselves attempting to make him rejoice.

2 Corinthians II, 4 — 'ἐκ γὰρ πολλῆς θλίψεως καὶ συνοχῆς καρδίας' — 'ek gar polles thlipseos kai synokhes kardias' — for out of much affliction and anguish of heart. That is, by reason of. 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' - 'eis hymas' — in you; toward you. That is, regarding you.

2 Corinthians II, 5 — 'ἀπὸ μέρους' — 'apo merous' — in part; partially; partly. How. Set expression.

2 Corinthians II, 6 — 'αὕτη ἡ ὑπὸ τῶν πλειόνων' — 'aute he hypo ton pleionon' — that by the many. The preposition 'hypo' here is with the genitive case and indicates here that the punishment was executed by others, that is, factually the passive voice of being punished by many.

2 Corinthians II, 8 — 'κυρῶσαι εἰς αὐτὸν ἀγάπην' — 'kyrosai eis auton agapen' — confirm love toward him!. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the directionality of love (toward whom?) as the direction of the efforts and actions of the faithful.

2 Corinthians II, 9 — 'εἰς τοῦτο γὰρ καὶ ἔγραψα' — 'eis touto gar kai egrapsa' — for this [actually] I wrote. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the purpose of the action. 'εἰ εἰς πάντα ὑπήκοοί ἐστε' - 'ei eis panta hypekooi este' — whether in all things you are obedient. The preposition 'eis' indicates here directionality toward something of the obedience of faith.

2 Corinthians II, 10 — 'ἐν προσώπῳ Χριστοῦ' — 'en prosopo CHristou' — in the Person of Christ. That is, from Christ, in the name of Christ, as Christ Himself would do. Apostolic forgiveness is executed also by Christ, if it is pleasing to Him. Here is a connection with the commandment of Christ in the Gospels; also Paul emphasizes that he acted precisely according to Christ's will. Therefore indeed, that person whom Paul forgave here, Christ indeed forgave as well.

2 Corinthians II, 11 — 'ἵνα μὴ πλεονεκτηθῶμεν ὑπὸ τοῦ σατανᾶ' — 'hina me pleonektethomen hypo tou satana' — lest we should be outwitted by Satan. The preposition 'hypo' here is with the genitive case and indicates here the passive voice be outwitted.

2 Corinthians II, 12 — 'εἰς τὴν Τρῳάδα' — 'eis ten Troada' — into Troas. Where to. 'εἰς τὸ εὐαγγέλιον τοῦ Χριστοῦ' - 'eis to euangelion tou CHristou' — for the Gospel of Christ. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the purpose of the action. 'ἀνεῳγμένης ἐν κυρίῳ' - 'aneogmenes en kyrio' — opened in the Lord. That is, by Whom and how.

2 Corinthians II, 13 — 'εἰς Μακεδονίαν' — 'eis Makedonian' — into Macedonia. Where to.

2 Corinthians II, 14 — 'ἐν τῷ Χριστῷ' — 'en to CHristo' — in Christ. In Whom. 'ἐν παντὶ τόπῳ' - 'en panti topo' - in every place; everywhere. Where.

2 Corinthians II, 15 — 'ἐν τοῖς σῳζομένοις καὶ ἐν τοῖς ἀπολλυμένοις' — 'en tois sozomenois kai en tois apollymenois' — in those that are saved, and in those that perish. The middle-passive state of the participles here indicates that both groups of people participate to a great extent both in salvation and in perishing, that is, the latter are consequences of precisely the actions of those people.

A passage that is considered directly connected with 1 Corinthians. There Paul taught to break off communion with a brother in Christ who committed a public sin of fornication. The Corinthians obeyed Paul, but that brother manifested sincere and deep repentance, accepted the penance with sorrow, converted, and suffered much due to the absence of communion with Christians. Therefore here Paul writes about manifesting love to such a repentant sinner and forgiving him. Paul with pain issued the decision then, and now manifests mercy and asks to forgive.

An important point here — according to God's design, Church punishments and penance must not be hard-hearted under any conditions. The faithful cannot under any conditions be cruel. It can be very difficult to distinguish cruelty from justice, the struggle against evil from the desire for revenge, and the like, and Satan, knowing this, tries very hard to make use of these difficulties. Paul warns about this, and teaches to be merciful, rather than cruel.

It must be said that discipline among the first Christians then was very high, and with the passage of time it softened. The Church tries to be open to different people, since the Lord commands us to try to find those who perished.

A door opened — a figurative expression meaning the absence of obvious obstacles, accessibility.

Savor of knowledge — a person is capable of smelling an odour at least initially even without wishing to. Therefore of all the senses Paul chooses precisely smell when he speaks of how the preaching of faith by the apostles, like an odour, cannot simply be ignored or unnoticed.
