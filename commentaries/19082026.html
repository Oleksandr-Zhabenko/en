Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians VI, 11-16)
2 Corinthians VI, 12 — 'ἐν ἡμῖν' — 'en hemin' — in us. In whom, where. 'στενοχωρεῖσθε δὲ ἐν τοῖς σπλάγχνοις ὑμῶν' - 'stenokhoreisthe de en tois splankhnois hymon' — you are restricted in your own sympathies (compassions). Where. Compassion expands a person's boundaries, for the person who sympathises receives another person, people, or creatures into the boundaries of their feelings, expanding them and extending them to that person, people, or creatures. At the same time, unbalanced, unwise impulses can also cause discomfort, a sense of limitation and helplessness, but here it is not about them, but only about high compassion close to God's mercy. Here the apostle says that the apostles have empathy, and often sympathy for the faithful, whereas the faithful often lack it, which is characterized here as straitenedness / restriction.

2 Corinthians VI, 15 — 'συμφώνησις' — 'symphonesis' — harmony, agreement, concord (hence the word symphony).

2 Corinthians VI, 16 — 'ἐν αὐτοῖς' — 'en autois' — in them; among them. Where, among whom. That is, God will become a Neighbour to people. The Old Testament quotation is composed of two — Leviticus XXVI, 11-12, Ezekiel XXXVII, 26-27.

Just before this, Paul wrote about apostolic labour. And here he continues, saying that the hearts of the apostles are open and filled with love for the faithful, but the faithful sometimes lack such openness and mutual love in return. They ought to grow in this as children grow. At that time, calling apostles fathers and themselves their children was not widespread (this is a later Church tradition regarding clergy). In verse 13, therefore, the translation as unto my children can be both a comparison and a direct statement that the Corinthians are the spiritual children of the Apostle Paul.

Further, Paul says that the faithful should guard against sins and idolatry, since having been cleansed in baptism and accepted as children of God, they ought to live holily, and God is to dwell in them. See also:

(Mark I, 23-28)
Mark I, 23 — 'ἐν τῇ συναγωγῇ αὐτῶν' — 'en te synagoge auton' — in their synagogue. Where. 'ἄνθρωπος ἐν πνεύματι ἀκαθάρτῳ' - 'anthropos en pneumati akatharto' — a man in an unclean spirit. In whom. That is, possessed by an unclean spirit. See details of the meaning at the link:
https://share.gemini.google.com/901c8daff8ce

Mark I, 25, 26 — 'ἐξ αὐτοῦ' — 'ex autou' — out of him. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates from where that spirit was to come out.

Mark I, 28 — 'πανταχοῦ εἰς ὅλην τὴν περίχωρον τῆς Γαλιλαίας' — 'pantakhou eis holen ten perikhoron tes Galilaias' — everywhere throughout all the region surrounding Galilee. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the spread of information. Where to, where.

Mark I, 24 — literally what to us and to thee, Jesus?.. (that is, what have we to do with thee?, what communion is there between us?. The demon does not want to have anything in common with God, does not even want to abide in the same place together. An important point regarding demons and evil spirits — they want to be like God, but without God; they want to displace Him, which is impossible). In a parallel passage in Luke (Luke IV, 33-37), the demon asks: Are you come to destroy us?.

An important point is that the demon might not have known that the Son of God was before him, but felt God's power in Him. Nevertheless, it is often understood that the demon's words speak of knowing that this is the Messiah, the Christ promised by God. Demons know the prophecies, know about the Trinity, and can feel much spiritually. But they do not know the hearts of people, do not know the future, and the like; that is, they are limited as angels in their powers (although exceeding humans in them). If indeed the demon knew that this was the Messiah, he might have thought that this was the Son of God, God, but might not have known that for certain (in other words, he could guess, but not be sure).

It is considered that Christ forbade him to speak so that people's faith would not be built on demonic disclosures — for what is revealed by demons (the evil one) is not revealed by God; it is not Revelation in the proper sense, and although it may contain elements of truth, just as half-truths or deception contain elements of truth, overall it misleads.
