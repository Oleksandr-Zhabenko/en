Liturgy:
(Colossians I, 1-2, 7-11)
Colossians I, 2 — 'ἐν Κολοσσαῖς' — 'en Kolossais' — at Colosse. 'ἐν Χριστῷ' - 'en CHristo' — in Christ. 'ἀπὸ θεοῦ' - 'apo theou' — from God. The preposition 'apo' emphasizes that grace and peace are God's gift to people.

Colossians I, 7 — 'ἀπὸ Ἐπαφρᾶ' — 'apo Epaphra' — from Epaphras. The preposition 'apo' indicates a transmission from Epaphras to others, pointing to his ministry.

Colossians I, 8 — 'ἐν πνεύματι' — 'en pneumati' — in the Spirit. In Whom.

Colossians I, 9 — 'ἀφ’ ἧς ἡμέρας' — 'aph hes hemeras' — since the day / from the day. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following 'he' indicates the starting point of time. 'ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ' - 'en pase sophia' — in all wisdom / with all wisdom. Both translation options are possible.

Colossians I, 10 — 'εἰς πᾶσαν ἀρεσκείαν' — 'eis pasan areskeian' — unto all pleasing. Here the preposition 'eis' emphasizes development in pleasing. 'ἐν παντὶ ἔργῳ' - 'en panti ergo' — in every good work.

Colossians I, 11 — 'ἐν πάσῃ δυνάμει' — 'en pase dynamei' — strengthened with all might / in all power. Both translation options are possible. 'εἰς πᾶσαν ὑπομονὴν' - 'eis pasan hypomonen' — unto all patience / for all endurance. Both translation options are possible.

The beginning of the readings from the Epistle of the Apostle Paul to the Colossians. In theme it resembles the Epistle to the Ephesians, therefore it is often thought that they were written at approximately the same time, all the more so since Colosse and Ephesus were geographically quite close to each other. It is believed that the occasion for writing was a certain heresy that began to spread among the Colossians. See regarding the writing and themes in particular at the link:
https://biblehub.com/colossians/

The opening of the Epistle contains greetings and wishes.
Then immediate mention is made of the occasion for writing the Epistle — the account of Epaphras, Paul's co-worker, one of the 70 apostles.
And then it speaks of the goal of writing — that the Colossians might be established in the faith and live spiritually according to God's grace.
Paul speaks of endurance and long-suffering which are built upon hope, as well as growth in the knowledge of God. All this together is meant to serve to the end that the Colossians will live holily, so that the King of their life truly is Christ, Whose glorification follows further.
When Paul speaks of long-suffering, he does not imply that one must suffer much and long for the sake of faith, but rather implies that one must be steadfast in good, not growing weary or murmuring, but growing in love and the knowledge of God's will. In other words, to be long-suffering, one must always preserve hope, as well as love and faith (preserving and remembering hope, being inspired by it, having faith, and growing through love).

(Luke VIII, 1-3)
Luke VIII, 1 — 'ἐν τῷ καθεξῆς' — 'en to kathexes' — afterward; on the next day. Set expression. When.

Luke VIII, 2 — 'ἀπὸ πνευμάτων πονηρῶν καὶ ἀσθενειῶν' — 'apo pneumaton poneron kai astheneion' — from evil spirits and infirmities. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that the deliverance was full, complete. 'ἀφ’ ἧς' - 'aph hes' — out of whom / from whom. The preposition 'apo' (also in its form before 'he') in both instances emphasizes that the exit of the evil spirits was complete, final, and the healing was thorough. I would also mention that Saint Mary Magdalene is my beloved female saint (not counting the Mother of God).

Luke VIII, 3 — 'ἕτεραι πολλαί, αἵτινες διηκόνουν αὐτοῖς ἐκ τῶν ὑπαρχόντων αὐταῖς' — 'heterai pollai, haitines diekonoun autois ek ton hyparkhonton autais' — many others, which ministered unto them of their substance. The preposition 'ek' indicates here that the sacrificial ministry of those women was complete, sincere, and they were entirely self-dedicated.

The reading speaks of women ministering to Jesus, accompanying Him and the apostles, and assisting them with their means (property). In particular, three are mentioned by name; all of them subsequently became Holy Myrrh-bearers, and among them is my beloved female saint — Co-Apostolic Mary Magdalene. There have been attempts to identify this name with the forgiven sinful woman at the end of the preceding chapter, but there is no real confirmation for this, whereas there are weighty objections. Notably, in the forgiveness of the repentant sinful woman there is not even a hint of unclean spirits (and had they been present, the Lord would have delivered the woman from them alongside such an instructive example of forgiveness and love; furthermore, it would be strange if spirits continued to remain in the woman even after her forgiveness by Jesus). It would also be strange if a wealthy woman (Mary Magdalene was such, at least prosperous, so that she could assist for a prolonged period a community of men such as was the company of Jesus and His apostles) were both demon-possessed and a publicly known sinner (this is a modest argument, yet quite realistic, for in those times it would have been difficult for a demon-possessed and publicly notorious sinful woman to remain truly prosperous among the Jews, or at least demon-possession and public sinfulness would have greatly hindered her from being truly prosperous unless attached to some ruler). In view of this latter consideration, it is entirely possible that those seven demons did not manifest themselves overly noticeably or openly, so that the woman suffered from them predominantly inwardly, whilst it manifested little outwardly.

On the whole, the named women (and those unnamed) subsequently became Myrrh-bearers. Among them are also two of my beloved saints — Martha and Mary, the sisters of Lazarus.

I would also note that many people remark that women did not display enmity against Jesus. That is, the Incarnate Son of God was worthy in the eyes of all women of His time.
