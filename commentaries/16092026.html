Liturgy:
(Galatians VI, 2-10)
Galatians VI, 4 — 'εἰς ἑαυτόν' — 'eis heauton' — in himself; for themselves. 'εἰς τὸν ἕτερον' - 'eis ton heteron' — in another; for another. In both cases it speaks of a person becoming independent of the opinion of those around them.

Galatians VI, 6 — 'ἐν πᾶσιν ἀγαθοῖς' — 'en pasin agathois' — in all good things; by all good things. Both translation options are possible, although the second appears more appropriate.

Galatians VI, 8 — 'εἰς τὴν σάρκα' — 'eis ten sarka' — to the flesh; for the flesh. Where to and for what. 'ἐκ τῆς σαρκός' - 'ek tes sarkos' — of the flesh. 'εἰς τὸ πνεῦμα' - 'eis to pneuma' — to the Spirit / spirit; for the Spirit / spirit. Where to and for what or for Whom. Various translation options are possible, since one can do good for a component of one's own nature, or one can please the Holy Spirit, God. 'ἐκ τοῦ πνεύματος' - 'ek tou pneumatos' — of the spirit / Spirit. Must be translated logically according to the previous phrase. Then the first option means that a person receives eternal life through the spirit as a component of their nature, whilst the second — that the True Source is the Holy Spirit. The preposition 'ek' in both cases indicates origin here.

Paul's encouragement and exhortation are directly connected with what Paul spoke about immediately before this, particularly with spiritual life. See about that at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15092026.html

In general, it can be said that here Paul looks at man (a human being generally speaking) from the standpoint of his relationship with God and other people, rather than from the standpoint of society. Therefore he does not speak of the significance of spiritual life for society, but speaks of the personal, of the significance for each person individually. Yes, spiritual life is of great importance for society, but of even greater importance for each person in particular. This can be asserted because each person inherits eternity personally.
Factually, it refers to what is called in modern language personal responsibility of each person before God. Yes, people are saved together and can also perish together, but Paul does not speak of this here; rather, he speaks of each person being personally responsible before God for themselves, for their deeds, actions (or inaction), thoughts, and words.
In view of this, Paul encourages doing good and caring for one another.

(Mark VII, 14-24)
Mark VII, 15 — 'εἰς αὐτόν' — 'eis auton' — into him (into a human being). Where to. 'τὰ ἐκ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου' - 'ta ek tou anthropou' — the things which come out of him. The preposition 'ek' paired with the preceding 'eis' here indicates from where those things originate which are mentioned further as the cause of defiling a person.

Mark VII, 17 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκον' — 'eis ton oikon' — into the house. Where to. 'ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄχλου' - 'apo tou okhlou' — from the people / crowd. The preposition 'apo' indicates that Christ here left the crowd completely.

Mark VII, 18 — 'εἰ机制' — 'eis ton anthropon' — into the man (human being). Where to.

Mark VII, 19 — 'εἰς τὴν καρδίαν' — 'eis ten kardian' — into the heart. Where to. 'εἰς τὴν κοιλίαν' - 'eis ten koilian' — into the belly. Where to. 'εἰς τὸν ἀφεδρῶνα' - 'eis ton aphedrona' — into the draught / toilet. Where to.

Mark VII, 20 — 'ἐκ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου' — 'ek tou anthropou' — out of the man (human being). The preposition 'ek' emphasizes that what comes next is indeed essential now.

Mark VII, 21 — 'ἐκ τῆς καρδίας' — 'ek tes kardias' — out of the heart. The preposition 'ek' emphasizes that what comes next is indeed essential now.

Mark VII, 24 — 'εἰς τὰ ὅρια Τύρου' — 'eis ta horia Tyrou' — into the borders / coasts of Tyre. Where to. 'εἰς οἰκίαν' - 'eis oikian' — into an house. Where to.

Food in itself cannot be an evil unless connected with sin. And human sins, like virtues, are always connected with the heart of a human being. Therefore the heart itself is of decisive importance in whether a person will be defiled or clean. Therefore Christ's faith is focused on purity of heart, rather than purity of food. At the same time it must be remembered that a person does something in connection with the heart; the heart participates in one way or another in what a person does.
