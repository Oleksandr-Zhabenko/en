Liturgy:
(Galatians II, 11-16)
Galatians II, 11 — 'εἰς Ἀντιόχειαν' — 'eis Antiokheian' — to Antioch. Where to.

Galatians II, 12 — 'ἀπὸ Ἰακώβου' — 'apo Iakobou' — from James. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that these were not close followers of James, but they had departed at that moment from James himself, who held a position according to the decision of the Apostolic Council in Jerusalem. 'ἐκ περιτομῆς' - 'ek peritomes' — of the circumcision. The preposition 'ek' may indicate that those people attached great, here excessively essential importance to circumcision as an act of the ceremonial Law of Moses (and thereby exaggerated its significance for Christians).

Galatians II, 14 — 'οὐκ ὀρθοποδοῦσιν πρὸς τὴν ἀλήθειαν τοῦ εὐαγγελίου' — 'ouk orthopodousin pros ten aletheian tou euangeliou' — walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel; do not act righteously with the truth of the Gospel; do not act correctly for the advantage of the truth of the Gospel. Various translation options are possible here, differing significantly in meaning. I think the last option, although appearing the least common, sheds the most light on the essence of the conflict and accusation. Apostles are called to act so that the Gospel, Christ's truth, gains ever greater advantage in the world. Peter, Barnabas, and certain others unnamed by name here took a step back in this situation from the truth of freedom in Christ to Old Testament ceremonial piety. Whether these were their sincere doubts — most likely not, since the Apostolic Council in Jerusalem, described in Acts XV, clearly testifies that all the apostles agreed that one ought not to act thus. Galatians II, 15 — 'ἐξ ἐθνῶν' — 'ex ethnon' — of the Gentiles. The preposition 'ek' here indicates that the difference between Jews and Gentiles was essential regarding the knowledge of God prior to that time.

Galatians II, 16 — 'ἐξ ἔργων' — 'ex ergon' — by the works / out of works. That is, that righteousness is a consequence of works, and not of God's grace. (Mark V, 24-34)
Mark V, 25 — 'ἐν ῥύσει αἵματος' — 'en rhysei haimatos' — in an issue of blood. That is, having a discharge of blood.

Mark V, 26 — 'παθοῦσα ὑπὸ πολλῶν ἰατρῶν' — 'pathousa hypo pollon iatron' — many physicians had laboured over her; suffered many things of many physicians. It is difficult to render nicely the passive construction with 'hypo' and the genitive case, but the passive state of the woman is emphasized, her suffering, particularly from the physicians themselves and their treatment. 'δαπανήσασα τὰ παρ’ ἑαυτῆς πάντα' - 'dapanesasa ta par heautes panta' — and had spent all that she had. That is, she gave her last personal funds and property for treatment, becoming completely impoverished. The preposition 'para' here is with the genitive case. The same construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, also regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ. For details see the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/02082025.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/Pryjmennyky.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/28052026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/16082026.html
Here it indicates that her sincere desire and faith that she would be healed were a gift of the Holy Spirit, although before being healed by Christ, she had spent everything, and also indicates that the Incarnate Son of God Himself healed her according to her faith. 'μᾶλλον εἰς τὸ χεῖρον' — 'mallon eis to kheiron' — rather grew worse; rather into the worse. Where to. That is, that her condition before meeting Christ, and despite the fact that her sincerity and faith were from the Holy Spirit, rather grew worse from the physicians' treatment.

Mark V, 27 — 'ἐν τῷ ὄχλῳ' — 'en to okhlo' — in the press / crowd. Where, how.

Mark V, 29 — 'ἀπὸ τῆς μάστιγος' — 'apo tes mastigos' — of that plague / affliction. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that she was healed completely, entirely.

Mark V, 30 — 'ἐν ἑαυτῷ' — 'en heauto' — in Himself. Where. That is, inwardly as Human Being. Here it refers to the human nature, which learned of the healing at the moment of the healing itself, but not earlier. God healed the woman in Christ even before Christ's human nature learned of it, but God healed through the touch to the God-man Christ, through the touch to the garment behind Christ's body. An important passage for icon veneration — the garment here acts in the same way as a wonder-working icon. Overall we believe that Christ could be depicted, touched, and the like, because He possesses a human nature, but this nature does not exist by itself, but because it is the human nature of God, the Only-begotten Son of the Father, Jesus Christ, and God Himself in His Hypostasis grants it existence. That is, one can touch Christ precisely because… He is both God and Human Being. 'ἐξ αὐτοῦ' - 'ex autou' — out of Him. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates here from where the virtue went out, and Who is its Source. 'ἐν τῷ ὄχλῳ' - 'en to okhloi' — in the press / crowd. That is, surrounded closely by people around.

Mark V, 34 — 'ὕπαγε εἰς εἰρήνην' — 'hypage eis eirenen' — go in(to) peace. Where to. That is, the Lord wishes her to inherit the peace of the Kingdom of God, in addition to her healing. 'ἀπὸ τῆς μάστιγός σου' - 'apo tes mastigos sou' — of your plague / affliction. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that the healing is complete, utter.

Mark provides more details.
It is understood that Jesus' question was asked in order to reveal the woman's faith and praise her as an example for others.
Also here we see an example of how a holy object, by the action of God's power, God's grace, can significantly contribute through faith to healing, and more generally — to the realization of expected and requested good.
It must also be said that ritual female uncleanness did not defile the Lord Jesus, but on the contrary — such was her faith and God's will, to heal that woman.

(Galatians II, 21-III, 7)
Galatians III, 2, 5 — 'ἀφ’ ὑμῶν' — 'aph hymon' — of you. The preposition 'apo' in its form before 'hy' indicates here an emphasis on the answer itself, rather than on anything else (only the answer to the question is needed). 'ἐξ ἔργων' - 'ex ergon' — by the works / out of works. 'ἐξ ἀκοῆς πίστεως' - 'ex akoes pisteos' — by the hearing of faith. That is, from the heard testimony of faith, from the preaching of faith. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates here what served to receive the Holy Spirit in reality.

Galatians III, 5 — 'ἐν ὑμῖν' — 'en hymin' — among you / in you. In whom.

Galatians III, 6 — 'εἰς δικαιοσύνην' — 'eis dikaiosynen' — for righteousness. That is, to what it led him.

(Mark VI, 1-7)
Mark VI, 1 — 'εἰς τὴν πατρίδα αὐτοῦ' — 'eis ten patrida autou' — into His own country. Where to.

Mark VI, 2 — 'ἐν τῇ συναγωγῇ' — 'en te synagoge' — in the synagogue. Where.

Mark VI, 3 — 'ἐν αὐτῷ' — 'en auto' — at Him. That is, on account of Him. Christ Himself in no way tempted them, as indicated by the middle or passive voice of the verb used here regarding offense/temptation.

Mark VI, 4 — 'ἐν τῇ πατρίδι ἑαυτοῦ καὶ ἐν τοῖς συγγενεῦσιν αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ αὐτοῦ' — 'en te patridi heautou kai en tois syngeneusin autou kai en te oikia autou' — in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house. Where. Mark specifies that he is without honour only in his own native land, among his own relatives, and in his own house (in the parallel passage in Matthew XIII, 53-58, 2 of these 3 are named). One can think that Mark again provides more details in this phrase. On the other hand, examples from the Old Testament are omitted, since it is considered that the addressees of Mark's Gospel were Roman Christians, former Gentiles, who had not grown up on Old Testament examples (unlike the Jews, to whom Matthew's Gospel was primarily addressed). See also:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/21072025.html

Verse 7 speaks of the sending of the 12 apostles to preach during the earthly life of Jesus Christ. This preaching was to teach them how they ought to act in the future (a kind of practical training during studies).

Of Venerable Martyr Macarius, Archimandrite of Kaniv, Ovruch, Pereyaslav, Wonderworker:
(Ephesians VI, 10-17; John XV, 17-XVI, 2)
Ephesians VI, 10 — 'ἐνδυναμοῦσθε ἐν κυρίῳ καὶ ἐν τῷ κράτει τῆς ἰσχύος αὐτοῦ' — 'endynamousthe en kyrio kai en to kratei tes iskhyos autou' — be strong in the Lord, and in the power of His might; be strengthened by the Lord and by the power of His might. Both translation options are possible; the first indicates that a person becomes a participant in the Lord's power and partaker of the Lord Himself, whilst the second — that the Lord Himself and the power of His might strengthen the faithful. For more on translation nuances see the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

Ephesians VI, 12 — 'ἐν τοῖς ἐπουρανίοις' — 'en tois epouraniois' — in high places / in the heavens. Here, unlike Ephesians I, 3 and III, 10, the translation in super-heavenly places is incorrect, since there are no demons beside Christ in the glory of the Father. See more at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/10102024.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/16102024.html
At the same time, it can indeed refer here to the atmospheric space, the visible sky.

Ephesians VI, 13 — 'ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ πονηρᾷ' — 'en te hemera te ponera' — in the evil day. Used here is a variant of the word bad (in the end, by results), evil, that is, at a time when difficulties surround.

Ephesians VI, 14 — 'ἐν ἀληθείᾳ' — 'en aletheia' — in truth; by truth. Rather the second translation option in meaning, as the first appears incomplete. See also at the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

Ephesians VI, 15 — 'ἐν ἑτοιμασίᾳ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου τῆς εἰρήνης' — 'en hetoimasia tou euangeliou tes eirenes' — in readiness of the Gospel of peace; in preparation of the Gospel of peace; with the readiness of the Gospel of peace. Various translation options are possible, signifying being built upon Christ's Gospel and abiding constantly in it, living such a life as when Christ's peace ought to come right now to spread further in people's lives.

Ephesians VI, 16 — 'ἐν πᾶσιν' — 'en pasin' — above all / in all things. That is, the shield of faith must be in all things, faith must be in all things. 'ἐν ᾧ' - 'en ho' — wherewith; in which. In meaning rather the first variant (wherewith).
