Liturgy:
(Colossians II, 20-III, 3)
Colossians II, 20 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν στοιχείων' — 'apo ton stoikheion' — from the rudiments / elements. The preposition 'apo' emphasizes liberation from the elements of the world through dying with Christ. 'ἐν κόσμῳ' - 'en kosmo' — in the world. Points to living in the world.

Colossians II, 22 — 'εἰς φθορὰν τῇ ἀποχρήσει' — 'eis phthoran te apokhresei' — which all are to perish with the using / unto destruction by use. An unusual construction with the dative case, which rather replaces the instrumental — by using (it is known that Koine Greek lacked a distinct instrumental case, having only four main cases, with the dative fulfilling instrumental functions). That is, in sense — if one uses that, it leads to corruption and destruction, for it is sin. Here the recognition of and adherence to religious prescriptions outside Christ is called that which leads to corruption and destruction (deterioration). If something is intended by God and is good, its use according to God's will leads not to destruction or deterioration, but to manifestations of the nature of the created thing and the revealing of its value; whereas here, on the contrary, it concerns sin — which does not reveal value, but leads to corruption.

Colossians II, 23 — 'ἐν ἐθελοθρησκίᾳ' — 'en ethelothreskia' — in will worship / in self-imposed devotion; in voluntary (religious) service. In what. Why does such service have the appearance of wisdom? Because it resembles genuine and true service. 'ἐν τιμῇ' - 'en time' — in any honour / in honour. Rather the first option, since it refers to a false height of service.

Colossians III, 1 — 'ἐν δεξιᾷ τοῦ θεοῦ' — 'en dexia tou theou' — on the right hand of God. Set expression. That is, in the highest glory and honour, alongside the Father.

Colossians III, 3 — 'ἐν τῷ θεῷ' — 'en to theo' — with God / in God. Emphasizes that this life is in God, holy.

Thinking on heavenly and exalted things in Christ is called to elevate the mind, to reconstruct it after the image of God's mind, when the sins of the world do not surround and encompass the mind, but it ascends above them upwards unto God, unto the purity and clarity prepared by God. This is possible also in a miraculous manner, through divine manifestations and revelations, but predominantly this occurs in synergy with God's will and grace in all things, particularly in cooperation with God regarding one's own salvation and that of other people.

(Luke IX, 23-27)
Luke IX, 26 — 'ἐν τῇ δόξῃ αὐτοῦ καὶ τοῦ πατρὸς καὶ τῶν ἁγίων ἀγγέλων' — 'en te doxe autou kai tou patros kai ton hagion angelon' — in His own glory, and in His Father's, and of the holy angels. The glory of the holy angels — their multitude and host, their service to Christ as God.
