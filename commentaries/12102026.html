Liturgy:
(Philippians II, 12-16)
Philippians II, 12 — 'ἐν τῇ παρουσίᾳ μου' — 'en te parousia mou' — in my presence. The word is polysemous, but here through direct juxtaposition and contrast only this translation option applies. 'ἐν τῇ ἀπουσίᾳ μου' - 'en te apousia mou' — in my absence. Analogous to the preceding.

Philippians II, 13 — 'θεὸς γάρ ἐστιν ὁ ἐνεργῶν ἐν ὑμῖν καὶ τὸ θέλειν καὶ τὸ ἐνεργεῖν ὑπὲρ τῆς εὐδοκίας' — 'theos gar estin ho energon en hymin kai to thelein kai to energein hyper tes eudokias' — literally for it is God which works in you both to will and to do of His good pleasure. The faithful synergise with God in fulfilling God's will and action within themselves and other creatures, but since God leads them to it first, grants them strength and bestows this gift, it can indeed be said that it is He Who works it. See more on this (mostly in Ukrainian) at the links:

Philippians II, 15 — 'ἐν οἷς' — 'en hois' — among whom. 'ἐν κόσμῳ' - 'en kosmo' — in the world. Where.

Philippians II, 16 — 'εἰς καύχημα' — 'eis kaukhema' — that I may rejoice / for praise. The preposition 'eis' here indicates the goal of the action. 'εἰς ἡμέραν' - 'eis hemeran' — in the day. That is, the full praise of the apostle will be realised only on the Day of the Second Coming of Christ. 'εἰς κενὸν' - 'eis kenon' — in vain; for naught. Set expression.

Paul again commends the Philippians for their virtues, testifying that those virtues are from God, bearing witness to the significance of faithful Christians for the world (parallel with Matthew X, 16, XII, 39, also with Matthew V, 14-16 and other passages of Scripture). He also expresses personal joy that his own apostolic labour among them was fruitful unto salvation (expressing it in the form of a metaphor of running, an athletic contest).

(Luke VI, 24-30)
Luke VI, 29, 30 — 'εἰς τὴν σιαγόνα' — 'eis ten siagona' — unto the cheek. 'ἀπὸ τοῦ αἴροντός' - 'apo tou airontos' — to take away. The preposition 'apo' underlines to take away completely, far off.
