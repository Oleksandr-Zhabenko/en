Liturgy:
(Ephesians II, 14-22)
Ephesians II, 15 — 'ἐν τῇ σαρκὶ αὐτοῦ' — 'en te sarki autou' — in His flesh; by His flesh. Both translation options are possible: the first points to the infirmities of human nature as participating in the work of human salvation, whilst the second indicates that those very infirmities, by being assumed by the Son of God, were healed. 'ἐν δόγμασιν' - 'en dogmasin' — in ordinances / in decrees. 'ἐν αὐτῷ εἰς ἕνα καινὸν ἄνθρωπον' - 'en auto eis ena kainon anthropon' — in Himself into one new human being; by Himself into one new human being. Both translation options are possible; both underline that in salvation the Son of God creates a renewed humanity — His own human nature — and through it renews other people.

Ephesians II, 16 — 'ἐν ἑνὶ σώματι' — 'en heni somati' — in one body; in a single body. Refers to the Church as the Body of Christ. 'ἐν αὐτῷ' - 'en auto' — thereby / by it; in it. Both translation options are possible; both underline the significance of the Cross of the Lord as the instrument of salvation.

Ephesians II, 18 — 'ἐν ἑνὶ πνεύματι' — 'en heni pneumati' — in One Spirit; in a Single Spirit; by One Spirit; by a Single Spirit. All translation options are possible: the first two point to the Holy Spirit as the One Who accompanies unto the Father, whereas the other two indicate that the Father leads unto Himself by the Holy Spirit.

Ephesians II, 21, 22 — 'ἐν ᾧ' — 'en ho' — in Whom. 'εἰς ναὸν ἅγιον ἐν κυρίῳ' - 'eis naon hagion en kyrio' — unto an holy temple in the Lord / into a holy temple in the Lord. The preposition 'eis' indicates movement and development — where to.

Ephesians II, 22 — 'εἰς κατοικητήριον τοῦ θεοῦ ἐν πνεύματι' — 'eis katoiketerion tou theou en pneumati' — for an habitation of God through the Spirit / into a dwelling place of God in the Spirit (by the Spirit).

In these verses (14-18 ), Christ is called our Peace. First and foremost, Paul testifies that Christ brought salvation to all people — not only to Israelites according to the flesh — and by His Sufferings, Death, and Resurrection reconciled humanity with God.

more is written regarding suffering and redemption in Christ, whilst here it speaks of the peace that people received in Christ, Who humbly became human and descended even into Hades to save humanity.
Christ suffered not only for Israel, but for all people, because crucifixion was not a penalty under the Law — Christ voluntarily accepted for the salvation of humanity such sufferings — crucifixion — as were not prescribed by the Law of Moses as punishment for sins, but which were used by the gentiles. Christ did this in order to save everyone.

(Luke VIII, 41-56)
Luke VIII, 41 — 'παρὰ τοὺς πόδας' — 'para tous podas' — at the feet / closer to the feet. The preposition 'para' here takes the accusative case. In sense, the second option applies: that is, Jairus sought to fall as close as possible to the feet of Jesus so that He would surely pay attention to his entreaty. 'εἰς τὸν οἶκον' - 'eis ton oikon' — into his house. Where to.

Luke VIII, 42 — 'ἐν δὲ τῷ ὑπάγειν' — 'en de to hypagein' — but as He went. That is, while walking.

Luke VIII, 43 — 'ἐν ῥύσει αἵματος' — 'en rhysei haimatos' — having an issue of blood / in a flow of blood. That is, her illness was a condition in which she remained. 'ἀπὸ ἐτῶν δώδεκα' - 'apo eton dodeka' — twelve years. The preposition 'apo' indicates from what point in the past this had been the case — an established usage of it. 'ἀπ’ οὐδενὸς' - 'ap oudenos' — of any / from no one. She could not obtain healing or cure from anyone to whom she had previously turned. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates that what interested her was specifically healing, and also that the source of healing is not the physician himself, but God.

Luke VIII, 46 — 'ἀπ’ ἐμοῦ' — 'ap emou' — out of Me / from Me. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel underlines that power, according to Jesus' words, went forth beyond His body, evidently in the direction of the woman for her healing.

Luke VIII, 48 — 'εἰς εἰρήνην' — 'eis eirenen' — in peace / into peace. Literally — peace as a destination and direction of movement. The woman consistently manifested right and blessed conduct, becoming an example of how to seek peace in God.

Luke VIII, 49 — 'παρὰ τοῦ ἀρχισυναγώγου' — 'para tou arkhisynagogou' — from beside the ruler of the synagogue. The preposition 'para' with the genitive case. Such a construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, as well as Eternal Trinity's Council, God's design of salvation of people, regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ.
