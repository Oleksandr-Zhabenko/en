(Luke VIII, 5-15)
Luke VIII, 5, 12 — 'ἐν τῷ σπείρειν' — 'en to speirein' — as he sowed; during the sowing. When. 'παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν' - 'para ten odon' — to the proximity of the road; alongside the road. The preposition 'para' here takes the accusative case and indicates that the seed fell in proximity to the road, in the direction towards it.

Luke VIII, 7 — 'ἐν μέσῳ' — 'en meso' — among; in the midst. Where. Set expression.

Luke VIII, 8 — 'εἰς τὴν γῆν τὴν ἀγαθήν' — 'eis ten gen ten agathen' — into good soil (ground). (Good in the outcome, by results, in a broad sense). Where to.

Luke VIII, 10 — 'ἐν παραβολαῖς' — 'en parabolais' — in parables; by parables. Both translation options are possible, with the first emphasizing that the truth takes the form of parables, whilst the second points out that the parable as such is suitable to teach well.

Luke VIII, 12 — 'ἀπὸ τῆς καρδίας αὐτῶν' — 'apo tes kardias auton' — away out of their hearts / from their heart. The preposition 'apo' here emphasizes that it takes away completely, far off, so that nothing remains.

Luke VIII, 13 — 'ἐν καιρῷ' — 'en kairo' - for a time; in time of. Set expression. When.

Luke VIII, 14 — 'εἰς τὰς ἀκάνθας' — 'eis tas akanthas' — among thorns; into the thorns. Where to. 'ὑπὸ μεριμνῶν καὶ πλούτου καὶ ἡδονῶν τοῦ βίου πορευόμενοι συνπνίγοντα' - 'hypo merimnon kai ploutou kai hedonon tou biou poreuomenoi synpnigonta' — are choked with (by) cares and riches and pleasures of this life, going forth. The preposition 'hypo' here takes the genitive case and indicates that which causes that choking in people, the weakening of spiritual life and efforts — cares, riches, life's desires; indirectly by context here it also indicates that these latter often act as obstacles, empty thorns that wound, yet do not nourish the soul.

Luke VIII, 15 — 'ἐν τῇ καλῇ γῇ' — 'en te kale ge' — in the good ground / in the noble, good soil. 'ἐν καρδίᾳ καλῇ καὶ ἀγαθῇ' - 'en kardia kale kai agathe' — in an beautiful and good heart / in a noble, excellent heart, good in its outcome, on the whole. Here the combination of good in different degrees of height and perfection indicates that a person first cleaves strongly with the heart to the Word of God, and then perseveres in the good even at a time when there is no longer that same enthusiasm, vigour, energy, zest, and inspiration. One must come to love the Lord strongly, and then preserve this love not necessarily in that same elevated and lofty state, but in a good one. 'ἐν ὑπομονῇ' - 'en hypomone' - with patience; in endurance. A continuation of the preceding thought — patience is necessary in the time of 'ἀγαθῇ'.

The famous Parable of the Sower with explanations by Jesus Himself. I would note here that in Luke the text of the parable itself is somewhat abridged, yet it is noticeable that He understands this parable as a description of the process of maturing and bearing fruit. Not all successfully complete this process to the end, although all were, if only in small measure, included in it, all being called to that maturing in the Lord. The description shows what common obstacles exist to a successful completion and the bringing forth of abundant fruit. Such an understanding is indicated, in particular, by the words of Luke VIII, 14-15, where we find the words 'τελεσφοροῦσιν' — 'telesphorousin' — (not) bringing fruit to perfection / reaching maturity, and 'ὑπομονῇ' - 'hypomone' — patient endurance, which requires time and constancy.
