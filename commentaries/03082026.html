Liturgy:
(1 Corinthians XV, 12-19)
1 Corinthians XV, 12 — 'ἐγήγερται ἐκ νεκρῶν' — 'egegertai ek nekron' — was raised from the dead. The preposition 'ek' here signifies from where the transition of Jesus occurred — from the dead to the living. 'ἐν ὑμῖν' - 'en hymin' — in you; among you. Where.

1 Corinthians XV, 17 — 'ἐν ταῖς ἁμαρτίαις' — 'en tais hamartiais' — in sins. In what.

1 Corinthians XV, 18, 19 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ' — 'en CHristo' — in Christ. In Whom. See regarding the translation nuances a fine dialogue with the Google Gemini Flash-3.6 at the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

1 Corinthians XV, 19 — 'ἐν τῇ ζωῇ ταύτῃ' — 'en te zoe taute' — in this life. That is, in the present state, which is susceptible to sins, suffering, diseases, corruption, and death.

On the basis of the truth and reality of the Resurrection of Jesus Christ, Paul confirms the truth of the doctrine of the general resurrection of the dead. A rare example of logical syllogisms in Scripture. It must be said that many Corinthians were educated, knew philosophy and logic, so Paul reasons as a philosopher or logician here. In particular, he uses the logical law If B follows from A, then not A follows from not B and the law of double negation: not (not C) is equal to C. Here A is the dead do not rise, B is Christ is not risen, and C pertains simultaneously to two different statements — Christ is risen and the dead rise. That is, Paul proves a theological theorem which means that such a phenomenon as the resurrection of the dead exists, an example and confirmation of which is the Resurrection of Jesus Christ. However, it must be understood that purely logically only its existence is proven (if a person believes in the resurrection of Jesus Christ), but not its universality nor its necessity. About the universality of the resurrection of all the dead and about its necessity we learn from faith, from God's Revelation, and not from logic.

Further Paul continues to use the same laws of logic already for another question — the salvation of people and forgiveness of sins (an analogous reasoning, with the difference that Paul goes from the denial of the Resurrection of Christ, and then shows that these reasonings are false because Christ is risen).

Verse 19 shows that faith in the resurrection of the dead and eternal life is an essential and inseparable component of faith in Christ, that is, Christianity.

(Matthew XXI, 18-22)
Matthew XXI, 18 — 'εἰς τὴν πόλιν' — 'eis ten polin' — into the city; to the city. Where to.

Matthew XXI, 19 — 'ἐν αὐτῇ' — 'en aute' — in it; on it. 'ἐκ σοῦ' — 'ek sou' — from you. The preposition 'ek' here indicates origin. 'εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα' - 'eis ton aiona' — forever; unto the age. Set expression.

Matthew XXI, 21 — 'εἰς τὴν θάλασσαν' — 'eis ten thalassan' — into the sea; to the sea. The preposition 'eis' here indicates movement.

Matthew XXI, 22 — 'ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ' — 'en te proseukhe' — in (elevated, high, spiritual) prayer.
