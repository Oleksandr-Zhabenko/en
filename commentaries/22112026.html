Liturgy:
(Ephesians IV, 1-6)
Ephesians IV, 1 — 'ὁ δέσμιος ἐν κυρίῳ ἀξίως περιπατῆσαι τῆς κλήσεως ἧς ἐκλήθητε' — 'ho desmios en kyrio axios peripatesai tes leseos hes eklethete' - a prisoner in the Lord, that you walk worthily of the calling; a prisoner, that in the Lord you walk worthily of the calling. Generally speaking, this is another case where different variants of translation are possible, where the words in the Lord may relate both to Paul's imprisonment and to his request to the readers of the letter. Most likely it is a deliberate play on words, which presupposes the correctness and simultaneity of both meanings.

Ephesians IV, 2 — 'ἐν ἀγάπῃ' — 'en agape' - in love; by love. Both variants of translation are possible; the first indicates that love must be in everything in general, and the second — that it is precisely love that is very important.

Ephesians IV 3 — 'ἐν τῷ συνδέσμῳ τῆς εἰρήνης' — 'en to syndesmo tes eirenes' - in the bond of peace; by the bond of peace; in the union of peace; by the union of peace. Different variants of translation are possible, which together convey more deeply the thought of peace as that which unites a person's life in Christ and makes it whole.

Ephesians IV, 4 — 'ἐν μιᾷ ἐλπίδι τῆς κλήσεως ὑμῶν' — 'en mia elpidi tes kleseos hymon' - in one hope of your calling; by one hope of your calling. Most likely the first, since the calling takes place not so much by hope or for its sake, which would make it more subjective, but by the Lord through this hope, which is more certain.

Ephesians IV, 6 — 'ἐν πᾶσιν' — 'en pasin' - in everything; in all. By meaning, the second variant of translation should be preferred.

The Apostle Paul calls us to behave in accordance with the calling to be part of the Body of the Son of God, the Church, and to live accordingly.
First of all he calls to unity and peace, to growth in God, in one faith.
The important verses 4-6 testify that Baptism is one (this has even entered the Creed), that is, it is not repeated.
One faith means that the Lord revealed to the faithful one faith, and not several different ones, and one faith is a consequence of the Unity of God's Revelation, and also of its eternity and immutability. All this follows from God's properties — for God's Revelation has God Himself as its Source.

(Luke X, 25-37)
Luke X, 26 — 'ἐν τῷ νόμῳ' — 'en to nomo' - in the Law. Where.

Luke X, 27 — 'ἐξ ὅλης τῆς καρδίας' — 'ex holes tes kardias' - out of the whole heart; from the whole heart. The preposition 'ek' in its form before the following 'ho' indicates the origin of love for God — it must be sincere, heartfelt, and express a person's heart. 'ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ ψυχῇ' - 'en hole te psykhe' - in the whole soul; (by) with the whole soul. 'ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ ἰσχύϊ' - 'en hole te iskhyi' - in the whole strength; (by) with the whole strength. 'ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ διανοίᾳ' - 'en hole te dianoia' - in the whole mind; (by) with the whole mind. Here everywhere both variants of translation are possible, with the second being mostly the widespread one. The first variants indicate the substantive filling of the soul, strength and mind — that is, with what they must be filled, and the second — more generally and fully — indicates the soul, strength and mind as such, entirely. A comparison with Matthew XXII, 36-39 shows the following (I quote:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/07042026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15042025.html
Matthew XXII, 37 — 'ἀγαπήσεις κύριον τὸν θεόν σου ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ καρδίᾳ σου καὶ ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ ψυχῇ σου καὶ ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ διανοίᾳ σου' — 'agapeseis kyrion ton theon sou en hole te kardia sou kai en hole te psykhe sou kai en hole te dianoia sou' - you shall love the Lord your God in the whole of your heart, and in the whole of your soul, and in the whole of your mind (deep reflection, understanding); you shall love the Lord your God with (by) your whole heart, and with (by) your whole soul, and with (by) your whole understanding (deep reflection, mind). A comparison with the parallel passages shows their use of different prepositions, which is significant. A comparison with what is written at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/28012026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/05022025.html
shows that the first variant is better in meaning, since it says where love must be, that it must fill the whole person, the whole human being, with all the consequences of this fullness.
A comparison with the Gospel according to Luke shows that both variants of prepositions are combined there:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/10112024.html
Moreover, the lawyer first says 'ex holes tes kardias', and then uses the preposition 'en'. Christ's answer shows that this is also correct. The use of 'ex' shows where love begins and originates, and also that it must be entirely sincere.
In general one can say that 'en' in this place is everywhere better translated as in, whereas 'ex' — as from or out of. The first emphasises the filling with love, its depth and substance, and the second — sincerity, origin and freedom, ease, naturalness.
A comparison with Mark XII, 30-33 (I quote:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/28012026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/05022025.html
Mark XII, 30 — 'ἐξ ὅλης τῆς καρδίας σου καὶ ἐξ ὅλης τῆς ψυχῆς σου καὶ ἐξ ὅλης τῆς διανοίας σου καὶ ἐξ ὅλης τῆς ἰσχύος σου' — 'ex holes tes kardias sou kai ex holes tes psykhes sou kai ex holes tes dianoias sou kai ex holes tes iskhyos sou' - from (out of) your whole heart, and from (out of) your whole soul, and from (out of) your whole understanding (deep reflection, mind), and from (out of) your whole strength. The preposition 'ek' in its form before the following 'ho' indicates where that love must come from, and also emphasises that this is essential. To a certain extent: You have a heart — let love for God originate from all of it! You have a soul — let love for God originate from all of it! You have reflection, understanding, mind — let love for God originate from all of them! You have strength — let love for God originate from all of it!. It is important to emphasise that 'en' is not used, that is, with what?, but 'ek' ('ex'), that is, from where?. This indicates that one cannot compel to love or use for love, but must give freedom to the heart, soul, mind and strength so that they love God by it. Let the children come to Me, says the Lord about this.
Mark XII, 33 — 'τὸ ἀγαπᾶν αὐτὸν ἐξ ὅλης τῆς καρδίας καὶ ἐξ ὅλης τῆς συνέσεως καὶ ἐξ ὅλης τῆς ἰσχύος' — 'to agapan auton ex holes tes kardias kai ex holes tes syneseos kai ex holes tes iskhyos' - to love Him from (out of) the whole heart, and from (out of) the whole understanding, and from (out of) the whole strength. See above. It is noticeable that the lawyer understands everything named as human powers, whereas in Christ there is more subjecthood, each named part exists more, more fully.
All this shows the deep meaning of all the variants and their interconnection.

Luke X, 30 — 'ἀπὸ Ἱερουσαλὴμ εἰς Ἱερειχώ' — 'apo Hierousalem eis Hiereikho' - from Jerusalem into Jericho; having left Jerusalem, into Jericho.

Luke X, 31 — 'ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ' — 'en to hodo' - in the road; along the road. Both variants of translation are possible.

Luke X, 34 — 'εἰς πανδοκίον' — 'eis pandokion' - into an inn. Where to.

Luke X, 35 — 'ἐν τῷ ἐπανέρχεσθαί' — 'en to epanerkhesthai' - in returning. That is, on returning, during the return.

Luke X, 36 — 'εἰς τοὺς λῃστάς' — 'eis tous lestas' - into robbers. That is, into the hands of robbers.

To love is first of all to care, to look after, to value, as all this applies to oneself.
Neighbour (Ukrainian ближній) — literally close one (the Ukrainian сусід, for example, is in English also neighbour) — is a person who is close in one respect or another. The Jews considered as neighbours either co-religionists and fellow citizens, Jews, or those who greatly favoured and helped them in some way. In the parable, however, we see that the one who became a neighbour was a Samaritan, hostile to the Jews (the Jews and Samaritans were in enmity), who was moved to compassion (a more literal sense of the words had mercy here). The sense is that people are able to become close (not only in the personal sense, but in the social. Social closeness, unlike — which can also be combined with — personal closeness in family, friendly and marital relationships) and are not merely able, but called.
Go and do likewise, and you will live — that is, you will be able to inherit eternal life. And in becoming close, one must love the other person as oneself, not only as regards what kind of love this is, but also as regards its strength (equally strongly).
This is often forgotten. The ability to become and to be close lies at the basis of love.
The lawyer's question is literally translated as And what must I do to inherit eternal life?. Do means that constancy is needed, and not something one-off.
