Liturgy:
(1 Corinthians IV, 9-16)
1 Corinthians IV, 10, 15 — ‘μωροί’ — ‘moroi’ — foolish — from ‘μωρός’ — ‘moros’. 'ἐν (γὰρ) Χριστῷ' — 'en (gar) CHristo' — (for) in Christ; (for) by Christ. Rather the first translation option, as both wisdom and mentorship are God's gifts; Christ grants them when a person can be or actually is in a certain unity with Him or can move toward that. See also a fine dialogue with Google Gemini Flash-3.6 about the translation nuances:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

1 Corinthians IV, 13 — ‘παρακαλοῦμεν’ — ‘parakaloumen’ — we pray, we beseech (God) (here; in general the word is polysemous).

1 Corinthians IV, 16 — ‘παρακαλῶ’ — ‘parakalo’ — I beseech (here; in general the word is polysemous).

The reading from the Apostle is about apostolic labor. The apostles labour as trailblazers, laying the path, and then it is much easier for the faithful to follow them. And as trailblazers, it is always harder for the apostles. But the Lord strengthens them.

(Matthew XVII, 14-23)
Matthew XVII, 15 — 'πολλάκις γὰρ πίπτει εἰς τὸ πῦρ καὶ πολλάκις εἰς τὸ ὕδωρ' — 'pollakis gar piptei eis to pyr kai pollakis eis to hydor' — for often he falls into the fire, and often into the water. Where to.

Matthew XVII, 18 — 'ἀπὸ τῆς ὥρας ἐκείνης' — 'apo tes horas ekeines' — from that hour. The preposition 'apo' indicates here the starting point of time regarding an important change that occurred with the boy.

Verse 21 is absent from some ancient manuscripts of the Gospel.

Matthew XVII, 22 — 'ἐν τῇ Γαλιλαίᾳ' — 'en te Galilaia' — in Galilee. Where. 'μέλλει ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου παραδίδοσθαι εἰς χεῖρας ἀνθρώπων' - 'mellei ho hyios tou anthropou paradidosthai eis kheiras anthropon' — the Son of Man is about to (that is, so it will happen according to the logic of events and circumstances) be betrayed into the hands of men. Where to. That is, Christ as God and man allows this, while simultaneously knowing that it will be precisely so. See more about this at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/renewal-of-Peter.html

The father lacked faith, but in despair turned to Jesus. And such an appeal, being sincere and heartfelt, the Lord did not reject, but also rebuked the father. Often in life we receive good from God, but together we also receive a rebuke. One should not neglect either, for God in His supreme wisdom knows better than we do. I will dwell here on the rebuke itself. Unbelieving is called so because he did not believe, and perverse — because he was in sins. When there is no faith, perversion easily sets in; people easily succumb to these or those sins. And this is a warning for everyone, even for those who are successful in labour for the sake of salvation. For we often receive God's mercy, though under strictness we would not receive it. Therefore Christ's mercy gives hope to everyone, calls everyone to conversion and repentance.
The father's words show that he has weakness in faith, that is, a mixture of faith with unbelief, but through turning heartfeltly with love for his son to Jesus, through humility and other virtues — he receives what he asks for (factually, Jesus Himself manifests the desire to grant him faith through healing).
Here also the Lord reveals the special significance of prayer (and some manuscripts add fasting here) — it can be a spiritual weapon against the evil one, better and more effective than any other. This is another encouragement to prayer and fasting.
The boy's condition resembles epilepsy in its symptoms through its attacks. But it must be understood that demonic possession and illness (even mental) are not the same thing; they require different treatment. In the first case, it is necessary to cast out the evil spirit, and in the second — God's healing (unfortunately, modern medicine cannot fully and successfully treat epilepsy, nor does it have truly effective means to cast out evil spirits, nor does it engage in the latter).

Afterward Jesus reveals what awaits Him in Jerusalem, but the disciples did not understand, because they held to their own ideas about the Messiah — King of Israel.
