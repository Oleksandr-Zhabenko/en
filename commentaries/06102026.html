Liturgy:
(Philippians I, 8-14)
Philippians I, 8 — 'ἐν σπλάγχνοις' - 'en splankhnois' - in the internal organs; in the heart; within; in the deep seat of affection. All translation options are possible, as profound feelings are meant.

Philippians I, 9 — 'ἐν ἐπιγνώσει καὶ πάσῃ αἰσθήσει' - 'en epignosei kai pase aisthesei' - in knowledge and all discernment; by knowledge and all perception. Both translation variants are possible. The first points to knowledge as the setting against which love flourishes well, whilst the second points to the fact that true knowledge of God, of Christ, leads to, causes, and brings about love.

Philippians I, 10 — 'εἰς τὸ δοκιμάζειν' - 'eis to dokimazein' - that you may test; that you may experience; that you may approve after testing. Somewhat polysemous words that are nevertheless entirely clear from the context. 'εἰς ἡμέραν Χριστοῦ' - 'eis hemeran CHristou' - for the day of Christ. The preposition 'eis' here points to readiness to stand before Christ in His glory.

Philippians I, 11 — 'εἰς δόξαν καὶ ἔπαινον θεοῦ' - 'eis doxan kai epainon theou' - unto the glory and praise of God; for the glory and praise of God. The preposition 'eis' here indicates purpose. For more on glory and praise in the Biblical sense, see the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Beauty.html

Philippians I, 12 — 'εἰς προκοπὴν τοῦ εὐαγγελίου' - 'eis prokopen tou euangeliou' - for the successful advancement of the Gospel; for the progress of the Gospel. Here as well, the preposition 'eis' indicates purpose. It is about the spreading and acceptance of the Gospel for the salvation and sanctification of people.

Philippians I, 13 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ' - 'en CHristo' - in Christ. 'ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ πραιτωρίῳ' - 'en holo to praitorio' - throughout the whole praetorium (that is, the palace in Jerusalem seized by the Romans, or in the quarters of the praetorians, the Roman guards, in Rome).

Philippians I, 14 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' - 'en kyrio' - in the Lord. It is about brothers in the Lord. 'περισσοτερως τολμαν αφοβως' - 'perissoteros tolman aphobos' - exceedingly (extraordinarily, beyond measure) boldly (intrepidly and) fearlessly. These words bear witness to several things:
1. Paul did not expect such inspiration to preach among the faithful as a result of his imprisonment for Christ;
2. Paul might have thought of a more temperate way of preaching for the faithful, but they on the contrary displayed zeal and daring.
One could say from the entire description (today's and what follows) in a word (or two) that the preaching was fervent, passionate.
The example of boldness and intrepidity of Paul himself as an apostle inspired others to similar efforts, particularly the situation with Paul's imprisonment. Which he writes about. In Church history, there have been many such cases, in particular, such mentions are very frequent in descriptions of the sufferings of holy martyrs for Christ.
There is also an important lesson here for everyone — one needs to preach with faith, also boldly, but wisdom, which Paul speaks of implicitly, is also needed — one must display sound judgment in everything.

(Luke V, 12-16)
Luke V, 12 — 'ἐν μιᾷ τῶν πόλεων' — 'en mia ton poleon' - in a certain city. Where.

Luke V, 13 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'ap autou' - from him. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel points to complete deliverance from the disease.

Luke V, 14 — 'εἰς μαρτύριον' — 'eis martyrion' - for a testimony; unto a testimony. The preposition 'eis' here indicates purpose.

Luke V, 15 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν ἀσθενειῶν' — 'apo ton astheneion' - from the infirmities. The preposition 'apo' points to complete deliverance from diseases.

Luke V, 16 — 'ἐν ταῖς ἐρήμοις' — 'en tais eremois' - into a solitary place. Where to.
