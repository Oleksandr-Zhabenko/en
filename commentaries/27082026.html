Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians X, 7-18)
2 Corinthians X, 8 — 'εἰς οἰκοδομήν' — 'eis oikodomen' — for edification; for house-building. It refers to the apostles' participation in God's economy/dispensation, in the gradual revealing of the depth and power of God's salvation in the Church. 'εἰς καθαίρεσιν' - 'eis kathairesin' — for destruction; for tearing down. The preposition 'eis' indicates the purpose of the action here.

2 Corinthians X, 12 — 'ἀλλὰ αὐτοὶ ἐν ἑαυτοῖς ἑαυτοὺς μετροῦντες καὶ συνκρίνοντες ἑαυτοὺς ἑαυτοῖς οὐ συνιᾶσιν' — 'alla autoi en heautois heautous metrountes kai synkrinontes heautous heautois ou syniasin' — but they measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise. It is worth dwelling on this further. Here it refers to the situation where people determine for themselves what constitutes the purpose of their life, and then measure their achievements against their own definitions. Paul emphasizes that such a position allows choosing quite an arbitrary goal of life, and this is unwise. Someone might say that there are people who claim one should compare oneself only with one's previous self — evaluating progress. But the position of these people differs from the one mentioned by Paul — they compare themselves on the basis of certain criteria that are to some extent independent of themselves (hence, more objective and often originating ultimately from God's commandments). But these people are also in danger of getting lost (if they have not already done so).

2 Corinthians X, 13, 15 — 'εἰς τὰ ἄμετρα' — 'eis ta ametra' — beyond our measure; immeasurably. Here it should be translated according to meaning in this way. 'ἐμέρισεν ἡμῖν' - 'emerisen hemin' — distributed to us; apportioned to us.

2 Corinthians X, 14 — 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' — 'eis hymas' — unto you. 'ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ' - 'en to euangelio' — in the Gospel; through the Gospel. Both translation options are possible here, though they represent distinct nuances.

2 Corinthians X, 15 — 'ἐν ἀλλοτρίοις' — 'en allotriois' — in other men's labors; among strangers. 'ἐν ὑμῖν' - 'en hymin' — among you. 'ἡμῶν εἰς περισσείαν' - 'hemon eis perisseian' — by us abundantly; unto abundance.

2 Corinthians X, 16 — 'εἰς τὰ ὑπερέκεινα' — 'eis ta hyperekeina' — in the regions beyond. 'ἐν ἀλλοτρίῳ' - 'en allotrio' — in another's sphere; in another man's line. Various options are possible. 'εἰς τὰ ἕτοιμα' - 'eis ta hetoima' — made ready to our hand. Set expression meaning to be ready / prepared.

2 Corinthians X, 17 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord; by the Lord. Both translation options are possible, representing quite different senses. The first implies unity and abiding in the Lord — in the Lord as part of God's world, as part of God's design, as participants in God's will, God's grace, etc. See more about this meaning at the link:
https://share.gemini.google/901c8daff8ce
The second option means boasting by the Lord Himself. But the original text allows both options, which can mutually complement each other in meaning here.

Overall, this reading serves as a vivid example of the active semantic use of prepositions to express complex thoughts.

(Mark III, 28-35)
Mark III, 29 — 'ὃς δ’ ἂν βλασφημήσῃ εἰς τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, οὐκ ἔχει ἄφεσιν εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα' — 'hos d an blasphemese eis to pneuma to hagion ouk ekhei aphesin eis ton aiona' — but he that shall blaspheme against the Holy Spirit has never forgiveness (complete release/remission) for ever. These are polysemous words, particularly because the word 'aphesin' is polysemous. A widespread Patristic interpretation is the idea of conscious and embittered resistance to the truth, which makes conversion and repentance impossible until the time of change, until it is abandoned — that is, as long as one remains unrepentant. I think it is important here to take into account the nuances of the meaning of 'aphesin' — it is not merely about forgiveness, but about complete release and liberation. And there is its own logic here — such a sin will also be forgiven, but a person having turned away from the Holy Spirit will no longer reach that fullness of His that would have been achievable without this sin. Here Christ in such an understanding speaks as the Omniscient. This does not mean that God will limit such a person, but it means that among all such people there will be none who will reach the highest peaks of spiritual life. That is, the fact that such a person will not attain the possible heights of the Spirit is called an eternal misstep / eternal sin (also a variant translation of the words for sin). And hence the conclusion that one must be very cautious when something can potentially be from the Holy Spirit. It is always better to acknowledge one's ignorance (and this will almost always be honest in such cases) than hastily to disrespect the Holy Spirit. It is also worth understanding that repeated disrespect for the Holy Spirit consistently decreases the fullness of the possibility to attain more gifts of the Holy Spirit. Honouring the Holy Spirit truly, a person will reach spiritual heights, for He will lead them to that, to God, to the entire Most Holy Trinity.
