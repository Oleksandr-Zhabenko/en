Liturgy:
(2 Thessalonians II, 1-12)
2 Thessalonians II, 2 — 'εἰς τὸ μὴ ταχέως σαλευθῆναι ὑμᾶς ἀπὸ τοῦ νοὸς' — 'eis to me takheos saleuthenai hymas apo tou noos' — that you be not soon shaken from your mind (way of thinking; worldview). The preposition 'eis' here indicates the goal of the action, whilst the preposition 'apo' indicates an apparent (false) cause: the Apostle means that although someone thinks it reasonable to consider that the Kingdom of God has already arrived (taking this as reason/mind), in reality it is not so, for it has not yet arrived before the Second Coming of Jesus Christ.

2 Thessalonians II, 4 — 'εἰς τὸν ναὸν τοῦ θεοῦ' — 'eis ton naon tou theou' — into the temple of God; into the sanctuary of God. That is, where to?, rather than where?, he sits down. With regard to the Jerusalem Temple, another word is almost always used in the Gospels. It may also denote a figurative sitting, that is, the spreading of a worldview, information, preaching, rumours, and so forth within the temple that will welcome the Antichrist.

2 Thessalonians II, 6 — 'εἰς τὸ ἀποκαλυφθῆναι αὐτὸν ἐν τῷ αὐτοῦ καιρῷ' — 'eis to apokalyphthenai auton en to autou kairo' — that he might be revealed (uncovered; a word sharing the same root as 'revelation') in his time. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action here. It should be added that the restraining may be linked to time — that is, restrains so that he is revealed [no earlier than] in his own time. Moreover, the restraining is rather directed at circumstances (which hinder his appearance) than at the person of the Antichrist himself (who will appear only at the end of the world). Otherwise, one would have to think that the Antichrist already exists and is simply awaiting his manifestation.

2 Thessalonians II, 7 — 'ἐκ μέσου' — 'ek mesou' — out of the way / from the midst. Set expression. In view of what is written at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/10072026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/18072025.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/11102025.html
these can be polysemantic words.

2 Thessalonians II, 9 — 'ἐν πάσῃ δυνάμει καὶ σημείοις καὶ τέρασιν ψεύδους' — 'en pase dynamei kai semeiois kai terasin pseudous' — in all power and signs and lying wonders; by all power and signs and lying wonders. Both translation options are possible and complement each other. It signifies that his appearance will be accompanied by wonders and signs, and that his appearance itself will take place as something miraculous in general, though in truth it will be a delusion.

2 Thessalonians II, 10 — 'ἐν πάσῃ ἀπάτῃ ἀδικίας' — 'en pase apate adikias' — with all deceivableness of unrighteousness / in all deception of unrighteousness; by all deception of unrighteousness. All translation options are possible. 'εἰς τὸ σωθῆναι' - 'eis to sothenai' — that they might be saved. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action here.

2 Thessalonians II, 11 — 'εἰς τὸ πιστεῦσαι αὐτοὺς τῷ ψεύδει' — 'eis to pisteusai autous to pseudei' — that they should believe a lie / for their believing the lie. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action here. As I have written previously, the Apostle Paul at times, following Old Testament usage, does not distinguish between God's permission (that which God does not desire, yet permits) and God's will (that which God desires). This is an example of precisely such a lack of distinction: where God will permit the Antichrist to people is meant (God does not want him to become such, yet will permit it), Paul writes God shall send the Antichrist to people.

Overall, this is an enigmatic passage that is frequently understood in different ways. The majority hold that the Antichrist here is an entirely real person who will appear before the Second Coming of Jesus Christ. Moreover, this passage is one of the clearest indications of the reality (rather than symbolism or allegory) of the Antichrist.
At the same time, in the Gospels, little attention is devoted to the theme of his appearance; there are only indirect mentions and references to Old Testament prophecies, which complicates understanding. One may think this is so because speculations on the subject of the Antichrist are often unfounded and ultimately intensify despondency in many people.
There are also differing opinions as to what is meant by who or what restrains the appearance of the Antichrist. For instance, there is the view that it is the Holy Spirit, or another that it is the former Roman (legal) state (one might say today — strong international law and state authorities), and there are other opinions as well.
The entire logic of the Antichrist's formation and actions proceeds from the fact that the devil would wish for his coming as swiftly as possible, whilst something from God restrains the fulfilment of this desire.
It is also frequently understood that human repentance delays the time of the end of the world, whereas human thoughtlessness and unrepented sins bring it closer. God knows the exact time and all circumstances as Omniscient, yet from the human perspective, events appear in precisely this way.
Nevertheless, it is also important that the Antichrist should not be at the centre of the faithful's attention, since the faithful belong to God. Yet neglecting what is known about him is likewise wrong, for this has been revealed by God to humanity.

(Luke XII, 48-59)
Luke XII, 48 — 'παρ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'par autou' — from beside him. The preposition 'para' in its form before a following vowel takes the genitive case here. Such a construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, the Eternal Council of the Most Holy Trinity, God's design, and the human nature of Jesus Christ. See the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/Pryjmennyky.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/vystup-2025-hypo-genitive-Romans-XIII_1.pdf
One may think that since God's gifts are given to a person through the Son of God and the Holy Spirit, the Lord likewise expects from those to whom much is given conformity to Christ, godlikeness, and the fruits of the Holy Spirit.

Luke XII, 51 — 'ἐν τῇ γῇ' — 'en te ge' — on earth / in the earth. Where? Refers to an established, permanent peace.

Luke XII, 52 — 'ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν' — 'apo tou nyn' — from henceforth / from now on. Set expression; the preposition 'apo' indicates from what moment in time the action takes place. 'ἐν ἑνὶ οἴκῳ' - 'en heni oiko' — in one house. Where.

Luke XII, 57 — 'ἀφ’ ἑαυτῶν' — 'aph heauton' — of yourselves / from yourselves. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following 'he' indicates that the conclusions drawn ought to be more objective. One may also think that judging from yourselves means that people will not know the exact time and circumstances of the end of the world, yet will hold various opinions on the subject. The Lord encourages thinking, yet does not encourage trusting various ideas. Be ready!, yet you do not know.

Luke XII, 58 — 'ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ' — 'en te odo' — in the way / on the road. 'ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ' - 'ap autou' — from him. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates that deliverance from the adversary must be complete and total. 'εἰς φυλακήν' - 'eis phylaken' — into prison. Where to. Underlines that the faithful will not desire this, yet may become victims.

Overall, this passage speaks primarily of readiness for God's judgment and divine visitation, as well as difficulties in relationships with people on account of faith, when one must guard it despite one's surroundings, even one's nearest relatives.
It also speaks of disagreement, disputes, and conflicts, and of the importance of reconciliation and peace as such.
Not peace, but division — signifies the extraordinary effectiveness of Gospel truths in their acceptance and embodiment in life by people — literally, they are so active (compare with chemically active reagents), possessing so much energy, action, and power, that people, uniting with Christ, cannot fail to activate a great number of processes — both manifest and hidden — around them and even afar. Therefore, life according to the Gospel is not passive, but active; not tranquil, but dynamic and mobile; not sluggish, but intense, and so forth. And this state is best described by the element of fire, whence the comparison arises.
Yet just as in nature various substances can be tightly bound together, and people may face a need to separate, transform, and process them, so too with the Gospel — its manifestations are exceedingly energetic and active, for otherwise certain people could not be saved.
The Lord Jesus Christ also speaks of how people generally devote too little attention to faith, to understanding divine truths, and to the knowledge of truth, focusing instead far more on earthly applications of their knowledge. And this is wrong.
He also teaches that reconciliation must be active — we must strive and care for peace, rather than waiting for the actions of others.
