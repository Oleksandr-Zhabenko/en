Liturgy:
Sunday before the Exaltation:
(Galatians VI, 11-18)
Galatians VI, 12 — 'εὐπροσωπῆσαι ἐν σαρκί' — 'euprosopesai en sarki' — to make a fair show in the flesh; to look well in the flesh. Both translation options are possible. That is, fundamentally attaching primary importance to the flesh. Paul is not speaking against bodily beauty (since circumcision is not put on display), but against the exclusive significance that his opponents attached to ritual circumcision, and in general — to rules for the flesh, while neglecting the spiritual, an example of which is the cross of Christ.

Galatians VI, 13 — 'ἐν τῇ ὑμετέρᾳ σαρκί' — 'en te hymetera sarki' — in your flesh; by your flesh. That is, to make your flesh the ground of their glory and boasting.

Galatians VI, 14 — 'ἐν τῷ σταυρῷ' — 'en to stauro' — in the cross; by the cross; through the cross. Various translation options are possible; the first indicates Paul's abiding in the action of the Lord's cross, in redemption and salvation, whilst the second — that the cross of Christ itself is the ground for glorying for the faithful, that is, it reveals their dignity, worth, and value in the eyes of God (because the cross is a direct testimony of God's love, of how the Lord values the salvation of people), being the reason why it is worth being Christians. The third is more soft in articulation.

Galatians VI, 17 — 'ἐν τῷ σώματί' — 'en to somati' — in the body. Here it is not flesh, but body. Often these are synonyms, but the distinction may also signify that suffering for Christ's sake, as well as His wounds, retain their significance after the Resurrection. That is, sufferings and wounds for Christ will possess glory even after the general resurrection. Hence the special veneration of the wounds of martyrs.

Verse 15 — by a new creature / new creation is meant the renewal of human being through faith in Christ, that is, how a person is genuinely transformed through Christ in the Holy Spirit. This transformation is significantly more important than the ceremonial component of the Law of Moses.

Paul once again speaks of the absence of any necessity to observe all the ceremonial precepts of the Law of Moses after Christ, particularly circumcision. Separately, I will note that the words of Galatians VI, 12 lest they should suffer persecution for the cross of Christ (which in those times was in the eyes of unbelievers the worst punishment) may testify that there were Christians who, through circumcision and the observance of the ceremonial Law of Moses, attempted to conform themselves completely to the Jews, and thus avoid being persecuted by them and by the Romans. Paul, on the contrary, confesses the very faith of Christ in the power and significance of the Lord's cross.

Of the Consecration (Dedication) of the Temple of the Resurrection of Christ in Jerusalem:
(Hebrews III, 1-4)
Hebrews III, 2 — 'ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ οἴκῳ αὐτοῦ' — 'en holo to oiko autou' — in all his house. Where.

Hebrews III, 3 — 'πλείονος γὰρ οὗτος δόξης παρὰ Μωϋσῆν ἠξίωται' — 'pleionos gar houtos doxes para Moysen exiotai' — for this Man was counted worthy of more glory than Moses; for He is worthy of greater glory alongside Moses. The preposition 'para' here is used with the accusative case and indicates a comparison with Moses (when juxtaposed, that is, compared). Here it speaks of the human nature of Christ, of His glorification in the Resurrection and Ascension as Head of the Church, as Creator, Saviour, and Builder. It must be added that here Paul refers to an Old Testament passage where Moses is called God's faithful servant in all His house, since through him the Lord revealed the entire Old Testament Law. Also in the Law there is a prophecy about a Prophet like unto Moses, Who must be obeyed, and He is Jesus Christ. A faithful servant is not the Builder.

Hebrews III, 4 — 'πᾶς γὰρ οἶκος κατασκευάζεται ὑπό τινος' — 'pas gar oikos kataskeuazetai hypo tinos' — for every house is builded by someone. The preposition 'hypo' here is used with the genitive case and indicates the middle or passive voice of the verb is builded / built. Here Christ as the Builder (verse 3) is indirectly named God.
