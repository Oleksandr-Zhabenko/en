Liturgy:
(Colossians III, 17-IV, 1)
Colossians III, 17 — 'ἐν λόγῳ' — 'en logo' — in word; by word. 'ἐν ἔργῳ' - 'en ergo' — in deed; by deed. Both translation options are possible. 'ἐν ὀνόματι' - 'en onomati' — in the name; by the name. Both translation options are possible; the first indicates that the faithful are to act as those who belong to Christ, that is, according to God's will, whereas the second indicates that this gives strength and inspiration to do so.

Colossians III, 18 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. Here it indicates that such is the conduct of those who are faithful in the Lord.

Colossians III, 20 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — unto the Lord / in the Lord. Here it speaks of how the Son of God expressed His good pleasure (this is well pleasing unto the Lord), setting an example of obedience to the Father, and giving us an example to work for His good pleasure. When children are obedient to good parents, they are like Christ.

Colossians III, 22 — 'ἐν ὀφθαλμοδουλίαις' — 'en ophthalmodouliais' — with eyeservice; as menpleasers. In sense all translation options are similar. 'ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι' - 'anthropareskoi' — menpleasers / one filled with a desire to please people. 'ἐν ἁπλότητι καρδίας' - 'en haploteti kardias' — in singleness of heart; with sincerity of heart. Both translation options are possible, together showing the general character of service.

Colossians III, 23 — 'ἐκ ψυχῆς' — 'ek psykhes' — heartily / from the soul. That is, completely sincerely, as if it were not someone else's will, but your own. See more on this at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Cup.html
Here one can also see a parallel with the chief commandments of love, one of which is phrased with such words. See:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/07042026.html

Colossians III, 24 — 'ἀπὸ κυρίου' — 'apo kyriou' — from the Lord. The preposition 'apo' indicates that this will be a gift of God.

Colossians IV, 1 — 'ἐν οὐρανῷ' — 'en ourano' — in heaven. Where.

(Luke IX, 44-50)
Luke IX, 44 — 'εἰς τὰ ὦτα' — 'eis ta ota' — into your ears. A typical figurative idiom meaning Listen!. 'εἰς χεῖρας ἀνθρώπων' - 'eis kheiras anthropon' — into the hands of men. Where He will be delivered.

Luke IX, 45 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτῶν' — 'ap auton' — from them. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates here that this was completely, entirely hidden from the disciples, incomprehensible to them.

Luke IX, 46 — 'ἐν αὐτοῖς' — 'en autois' — among them; in them. Where, among whom.

Luke IX, 47 — 'παρ’ ἑαυτῷ' — 'par heauto' — in the vicinity of Himself / near Himself. Here the preposition 'para' in its form before a following vowel takes the dative case. Indicates Christ's proximity to people.

Luke IX, 48 — 'ἐν πᾶσιν ὑμῖν' — 'en pasin hymin' — among you all / in you all. Where, among whom. I wrote with many details and important points on the topic of humility and greatness at the link and others cited there:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/30072026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/07082025.html

Jesus reveals to the disciples His future Sufferings, Death, and Resurrection, but they did not understand and were afraid to ask Him. An example of how the Lord sometimes does what will become necessary for people later, even if they may not understand it at the time; nevertheless, this does not stop the Lord. It is also important to say that misunderstanding can be a stage in development, for if people always understood everything, they would live completely differently. The apostles remembered their misunderstanding, as well as the words of Jesus, and recalled them after His Resurrection and believed. Had they understood, they would have been prepared for the events awaiting Jesus and would have reacted differently, but the Lord allows them to know their own weakness. It is vital to see that understanding is valuable, but even when it is absent, faith is still required.

The conclusion of the reading touches upon the topic of people who do not walk with Jesus' disciples, that is, in modern terms, the unchurched. An active topic for many, which I wrote about at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Hyper.html
To quote:
The Lord says that he that is not against us is for us, he that gathers not with me scatters, he that is not against us is on our part and similar passages (Luke IX, 49-50, Mark IX, 38-41, Matthew XII, 30 and others). Speaking of Himself, generally speaking, the thought follows that one cannot be neutral regarding Christ Himself. This thought is well known: we can never be in the middle, for example, between God and the devil, or between the Kingdom of God and hell; there is no intermediate, middle state in reality. There is a temporary indefinite state — the present time, while things are not yet finalized, but even so it is not final. Regarding Christ we can in no way be in the middle, neutral, as to being with Him or against Him, for Christ or against Him. If the apostles are together with Christ, the Church together with Christ in this context, then these words apply to them. It is impossible to be neutral regarding the Church in such a sense. Christ is in the center of the universe, and everything has its meaning and purpose in relation to Him, and nothing has meaning without Him. Everything else that is not directly with Christ in this context is not at the center of the universe, and regarding it we can be neutral, taking an intermediate position.
When speaking of relationships between the Church and the surrounding world outside the Church, the apostles enter into these words of Christ as His representatives: essentially these words apply to them, they stand at the center of the universe, and so one cannot be neutral toward them; ultimately, as one cannot serve two masters, a person will decide, love one and serve that one — God or mammon. In such a context a person (or people, or society) cannot be in the middle.
On the other hand, regarding relationships between Churches that, for example, mutually recognize Sacraments or need broader communion, they cannot view one another or themselves in the context of we are in the center of the universe, because by virtue of these very words they are not in such a context relative to one another at the center of the universe.
I shall dwell on the word He that is not against you is FOR you (Luke IX, 50). For — the Greek hyper (ὑπέρ), which with the genitive comes from over, above, and can also mean the cause, for what. That is, these words can also be understood as (a field for cooperation, preaching, activity) for you, or further: your actions are precisely meant to bring them to the knowledge of the truth, you are sent to preach to those who are not against you.
I would add that these same words can be understood yet another way — he that is not against you is one in whom I act beyond that which I act in you (beyond not as more, but as an overflow, similar to the well-known crumbs). And still another way — he that is not against you can often act 'in your name', that is, their good will serve to spread the faith, and their number to the massfulness of faith. In all cases we see the diversity of God's providence and care for the faithful and all others.
