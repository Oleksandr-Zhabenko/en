Liturgy:
(1 Corinthians IV, 1-5)
1 Corinthians IV, 2 — 'ἐν τοῖς οἰκονόμοις' — 'en tois oikonomois' — in stewards.

1 Corinthians IV, 3 — 'εἰς ἐλάχιστόν' — 'eis elakhiston' — a very small thing. That is, it becomes least significant, losing value down to almost nothing. 'ὑφ’ ὑμῶν' - 'hyph hymon' — by you. 'ὑπὸ ἀνθρωπίνης ἡμέρας' - 'hypo anthropines hemeras' — by man's judgment (literally: human day) (that is, by human standards, since trial/judgment was held by day). The preposition 'hypo' in both places is used with the genitive case and indicates the passive voice to be judged; to be examined (for determining value, innocence, or truth); to be evaluated. A polysemous word in the passive voice indicates that the Apostle Paul generally yields all such things to God.

1 Corinthians IV, 4 — 'ἐν τούτῳ' — 'en touto' — hereby; in this. Both translation options are possible, although they represent distinct senses.

1 Corinthians IV, 5 — 'ἀπὸ τοῦ θεοῦ' — 'apo tou theou' — from God. The preposition 'apo' emphasizes that this praise will be a gift of God, and also shows how vibrantly God will rejoice in people for what He will praise, much as a sincere, open, and benevolent person openly and distinctly responds to the good in friends.
