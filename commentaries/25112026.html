Liturgy:
(1 Timothy I, 18-20; II, 8-15)
1 Timothy I, 18 — 'ἐν αὐταῖς' — 'en autais' — by them / in them. Meant here is according to them.

1 Timothy II, 8 — 'ἐν παντὶ τόπῳ' — 'en panti topo' — every where / in every place. By sense — the second option.

1 Timothy II, 9 — 'ἐν καταστολῇ' — 'en katastole' — in modest apparel / in attire. Here only this translation option applies by sense. 'ἐν πλέγμασιν' - 'en plegmasin' — with broided hair / in the braiding of hair; by the braiding of hair. By sense, the second option is better, since it is clearer.

1 Timothy II, 11 — 'ἐν ἡσυχίᾳ μανθανέτω ἐν πάσῃ ὑποταγῇ' — 'en hesykhia manthaneto en pase hypotage' — let the woman learn in silence [quietness] with all subjection / in quietness let her learn in full submission; by quietness let her learn in full submission. Various translation options are possible; moreover, it is in fact unclear who is meant to be quiet here — the woman or the man teaching her, for it may refer to the man needing to be quiet, calm, and gentle, thereby also showing love, whilst the woman honours her husband. One way or another, it concerns the manifestation of love rather than restricting someone. One might further say that if it refers to the woman, the husband must likewise possess quietness and gentleness, for otherwise there will truly be no quietness if the husband (or the wife) is not so.

1 Timothy II, 12 — 'ἐν ἡσυχίᾳ' — 'en hesykhia' — in silence / in quietness; in stillness. Here the thought is further unfolded that quietness serves to manifest love rather than being a means of restriction. It is wrong to understand the Apostle's words as a means of imposing restrictions; rather, they are instruction on how mutual love between spouses is manifested — in gentleness and freedom from wrath. For wrath for the sake of teaching helps little, and can cause great harm.

1 Timothy II, 14 — 'ἐν παραβάσει' — 'en parabasei' — in the transgression.

1 Timothy II, 15 — 'ἐν πίστει καὶ ἀγάπῃ καὶ ἁγιασμῷ μετὰ σωφροσύνης' — 'en pistei kai agape kai hagiasmo meta sophrosynes' — in faith and charity [love] and holiness with sobriety. In what.

The Apostle Paul writes authoritatively, as a leader.
He excommunicated from the Church heretics who blasphemed God, and next gives instructions concerning women, that they are not to preach during the service. He also wrote about this in another epistle. I have written an explanation (how this may be understood from his words and the faith of the Church) at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/04082023.html
Regarding the need in prayer not to be wrathful and to forgive, this is found in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew V-VI), as well as in other passages of the Gospel.

(Luke XV, 1-10)
Luke XV, 4 — 'ἐξ ὑμῶν' — 'ex hymon' — of you / from among you. 'ἐξ αὐτῶν' - 'ex auton' — of them / from among them. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates here selection from a group. 'ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ' - 'en te eremo' — in the wilderness. Where.

Luke XV, 6 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκον' — 'eis ton oikon' — home / into the house. Where to.

Luke XV, 7 — 'ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ' — 'en to ourano' — in the heaven. Where.

Jesus teaches the purpose of His Coming — to seek and to save sinners (men and women alike), calling them to repentance, giving them the necessary strength for it, and doing whatever else is needed toward that end.
He uses parables because this was widespread at that time, accessible to understanding in simplicity yet also holding a deeper meaning, possessing great depth and vividness, and prompting one to agree at least in a certain sense.
Regarding the parallel passage to the first parable in the reading — about the lost and found sheep (Matthew XVIII, 12-14) — see the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/01062026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/05062023.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15072023.html
Overall, the parables are deeply eloquent, yet they also figuratively show what the Lord does for human salvation — for example, the lit lamp is an image both of divine teaching, and of the grace of the Holy Spirit which calls to repentance and salvation, and of the illumination of mind and heart by the light of Christ's truth, and so forth.
Moreover, the image of the Good Shepherd is a symbol of Christ Himself, owing to which it also entered iconographic and liturgical traditions (there are well-known icons of this kind, and in the vestments of clergy there are important elements that specifically symbolise Christ as Shepherd).

Of Hieromartyrs Clement, Bishop of Rome, and Peter, Archbishop of Alexandria:
(Philippians III, 20-IV, 3; Matthew V, 14-19)
Philippians III, 20 — 'ἐν οὐρανοῖς' — 'en ouranois' — in heavens / in the heavens. Where. Heavens is here in the plural. It brings to mind Christ's words that in the Father's house there are many mansions, which He goes to prepare. 'ἐξ οὗ' - 'ex hou' — from whence / from which; whence. The preposition 'ek' in its form before the following 'hou' indicates here from where we await Christ.

In verse Philippians III, 21, the Apostle speaks of the conformity of the faithful to the human nature of Christ after their transformation by divine power. This also concerns the resurrection and the incorruptibility of the body thereafter. The resurrected and/or transformed faithful will be bodily similar in state to the state of the body of the Risen Christ. See more on this topic in my book Loving Enemies: Why the Creator Is Perfect, and We Can Be as Well.

Philippians IV, 1 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. In Whom, in unity with Whom, by Whose power.

Philippians IV, 2 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. Analogous to the previous, yet concerning thoughts (being of the same mind).

Philippians IV, 3 — 'αἵτινες ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ συνήθλησάν μοι μετὰ καὶ Κλήμεντος καὶ τῶν λοιπῶν συνεργῶν μου, ὧν τὰ ὀνόματα ἐν βίβλῳ ζωῆς' — 'haitines en to euangelio synethlesan moi meta kai Klementos kai ton loipon synergon mou, hon ta onomata en biblo zoes' — those women which laboured with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and with other my fellowlabourers, whose names are in the book of life / to those (women, maidens [here in the feminine gender]) who in the Gospel (in the preaching of the good news) contended (zealously laboured) (to assist) me, together with Clement [whose memory is celebrated today] and my other co-workers, whose names are in the Book of Life. In what; where. Clement is mentioned by name, indicating that he was a co-worker of Paul and a preacher of the Gospel. The feminine gender also speaks of the women who laboured in the proclamation of the Gospel alongside the apostles; in particular, well-known holy women may be meant here.

Philippians IV, 4 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord; by the Lord. Both translation options are possible: the first underlines that the Lord is the source and strength of joy, whilst the second indicates that the faithful are called to be comforted (made joyful) by the Lord Jesus Christ Himself.
