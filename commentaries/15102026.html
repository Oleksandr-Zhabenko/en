Liturgy:
(Philippians III, 1-8)
Philippians III, 1 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord; by the Lord. Both translation options are possible: the first underlines that joy is a gift of God's grace, whilst the second expresses the wish to rejoice in the Lord Himself.

Philippians III, 3, 4 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Jesus Christ; through Jesus Christ. Similar to the preceding, both translation options are possible. 'ἐν σαρκὶ' - 'en sarki' — in the flesh; by the flesh. Rather the first translation option applies, that is, it refers to confidence in the flesh, meaning in something closely and directly connected with the material.

Philippians III, 5 — 'ἐκ γένους Ἰσραήλ' — 'ek genous Israel' — of the stock of Israel. 'Ἑβραῖος ἐξ Ἑβραίων' - 'Hebraios ex Hebraion' — an Hebrew of the Hebrews. Here the preposition 'ek' indicates origin.

Philippians III, 6 — 'ἐν νόμῳ' — 'en nomo' — in the Law. Where.

The Apostle Paul commands us to rejoice in the Lord, as well as to keep the pure faith of Christ from the temptation of returning from Christ to the rituals of the Old Testament, about which he writes extensively in various epistles.
He adduces his own example as a very pious and zealous believer whose observance of all ritual prescriptions did not save him (that is, Paul) from persecuting the Church of Christ in his blindness. Therefore, for others too, the observance of the ritual component of the Law does not save (just as it did not save Paul).

At the end of the reading, Paul says that in Christ there is everything needed, whereas if someone proposes to him something instead of Christ (anti-Christ), he counts all that as loss—the more decisively, the further it is from Christ. He chooses a sharp expression to underline the madness of those who depart from Christ for the sake of something else.
