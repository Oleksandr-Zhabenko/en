Liturgy:
(1 Corinthians XV, 58-XVI, 3)
1 Corinthians XV, 58 — 'ἐν τῷ ἔργῳ τοῦ κυρίου' — 'en to ergo tou kyriou' — in the work of the Lord; by the work of the Lord. Both translation options are possible. 'ἐν κυρίῳ' - 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. In Whom.

1 Corinthians XVI, 1 — 'εἰς τοὺς ἁγίους' — 'eis tous hagious' — for / concerning (the collection for) the saints. Here the preposition 'eis' indicates the direction of action.

1 Corinthians XVI, 2 — upon the first day of the week — that is, on every Lord's Day, Sunday (according to the Jewish calendar). The Lord's Day is the day of the Lord when the New Testament Church especially honoured the Lord, primarily through the communal celebration of the Eucharist during the Liturgy (compare with Acts XX, 7). It should be noted that the text contains a complex linguistic construction, yet it is used in precisely this sense. 'παρ’ ἑαυτῷ' — 'par heauto' — in the vicinity of oneself; near oneself. The preposition 'para' in its form before a following vowel here takes the dative case and signifies near. Here it signifies personal property prepared in a special way to be set aside for charitable aid.

1 Corinthians XVI, 3 — 'εἰς Ἱερουσαλήμ' — 'eis Hierousalem' — unto Jerusalem. Where to.

The Apostle Paul teaches us to care for our neighbours, especially for brothers and sisters in Christ, and moreover to make certain forms of care weekly (and, speaking generally, periodic yet constant), so as not to complicate these matters, but to render them simple.

(Luke V, 17-26)
Luke V, 17 — 'ἐν μιᾷ τῶν ἡμερῶν' — 'en mia ton hemeron' — in one of the days. When. 'ἐκ πάσης κώμης' - 'ek pases komes' — out of every town (village). From where. 'εἰς τὸ ἰᾶσθαι' - 'eis to iasthai' — to heal them. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal here.

Luke V, 19 — 'εἰς τὸ μέσον ἔμπροσθεν' — 'eis to meson emprosthen' — into the midst before (the face). Where to. Here is a combination of two strong words indicating that Christ had to respond to these actions. Into the midst — that is, as close as possible and perceptibly to Christ; 'emprosthen' — literally before the face, that is, in such a manner as to be definitively noticed and heard. Sometimes those who pray with a strong desire for the Lord to answer their prayer attempt to draw as close to God as possible. Here the men did this twice. If you pray for someone, it is worth laying out twice as much effort and zeal, yet such efforts receive special commendation and reward from God. In the Orthodox tradition, this doubling is noticeable particularly in the litanies, where one of them calls out: Again and again in peace let us pray to the Lord!.

Luke V, 22 — 'ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν' — 'en tais kardiais hymon' — in your hearts. Where.

Luke V, 24, 25 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκόν' — 'eis ton oikon' — into his house. Where to.
