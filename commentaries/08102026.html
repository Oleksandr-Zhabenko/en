Liturgy:
(Philippians I, 20-27)
Philippians I, 20 — 'ἐν οὐδενὶ αἰσχυνθήσομαι, ἀλλ’ ἐν πάσῃ παρρησίᾳ' — 'en oudeni aiskhynthesomai, all en pase parresia' — in nothing I shall be ashamed, but with all boldness / in full confidence. Various translation options are possible due to the polysemy of the word parresia. 'ἐν τῷ σώματί μου' — 'en to somati mou' — in my body. Here it refers to human service in the body — and the apostle expresses faith that his devotion to Christ and persistent labour will lead to the glorification of Christ in all his life.
The Apostle Paul says that by his example he inspired others boldly to preach Christ, and this will serve Paul himself unto salvation through the prayers of the faithful for him and by the action of Jesus Christ Himself and the Holy Spirit.
Here also an important thought is that if a person acts in the Holy Spirit, properly, then even if someone else attempts to use this for their own interests with a different purpose, this will not nullify the correctness and holiness of the actions of that first person, nor reduce them to nothing, but the correctness and holiness will be preserved by the Holy Spirit Himself.
Here everything depends on the Holy Spirit — whether He truly leads the person to those actions or not. If He leads, then such actions are holy and proper, as was just said above. But if actions look good, yet it is not the Holy Spirit leading to them Himself, then such seemingly good actions are most likely not truly good in reality, and that first person needs to act with great caution, discernment, and wisdom with a view to their possible use by someone else for another purpose — all to avoid becoming simply a victim of their own heedlessness, shortsightedness, and naivety. And main of all — one must seek what is truly God's will.
In the case of Paul's preaching, he testifies that it is the Holy Spirit acting through Him, and therefore he rejoices.

Philippians I, 21 — 'τὸ ζῆν χριστὸς καὶ τὸ ἀποθανεῖν κέρδος' — 'to zen CHristos kai to apothanein kerdos' — literally for to me living is Christ, and dying [the state close to death, approaching death] is gain [advantage]. Here is a famous and profound passage; I shall dwell on it in somewhat greater detail. The Apostle Paul implies that he lives for Christ, striving to please Christ specifically, therefore he can also call his life Christ in the sense that the meaning, center, and content of his life is Christ Himself (and this is great happiness). And when Paul is in difficult circumstances where he might die, then this both brings him closer to the reward in Christ, to eternal life, and also — in contrast to people far from God, from Christ — the fact that death does not separate from Christ, but enables one also to unite with Him, is an advantage, a value, a gain, an acquisition. Of which Paul writes further right here. See also in more detail at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/renewal-of-Peter.html
Here one can see what constitutes happiness at the summits of God's design. Human happiness is to be in such unity with Christ that life itself becomes the abiding of Christ in you and of you in Christ. The Apostle John the Theologian writes more about this, particularly regarding the Eucharist, perichoresis, etc. See the links:

Philippians I, 22 — 'τὸ ζῆν ἐν σαρκί' — 'to zen en sarki' — to live in the flesh. That is, the present life with its infirmities.

Philippians I, 23 — 'ἐκ τῶν δύο' — 'ek ton duo' — from these two; by the two. The preposition 'ek' indicates here the source of action. 'εἰς τὸ ἀναλῦσαι' - 'eis to analysai' — to depart; in order to depart (here figuratively — to die).

Philippians I, 25 — 'εἰς τὴν ὑμῶν' — 'eis ten hymon' - unto you. The preposition 'eis' indicates movement towards, and in sense also direction, address.

Philippians I, 26 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Christ Jesus; through Christ Jesus. Both translation options are possible; the first underlines the general connection of joy with Christ, whilst the second indicates that Christ is the Cause and Source of joy. 'ἐν ἐμοὶ' - 'en emoi' — in me. In sense also through me.

Philippians I, 27 — 'ἐν ἑνὶ πνεύματι' — 'en heni pneumati' — in one Spirit. Points to the Holy Spirit, Who both unites and grants strength.

Paul himself does not decide here which is better for him, but he realises that both are good. And that which will be realised is that he will continue to live, and has at least the intention to come eventually to the Philippians. Judging by everything, Paul is confident because he received some assurance from God (concerning his life), perhaps a revelation about it, or perhaps certain events clearly indicate God's deliverance from dangers.
And further he says that he wishes to commend the Philippians, and also that his praise, as well as his life and arrival, the fulfilment of God's assurance, will be for the Philippians an even greater boast and joy.

Today's reading concludes with an encouragement and inducement to live in accordance with and defend the faith of Christ, the Gospel faith.

(Luke VI, 12-19)
Luke VI, 12 — 'ἐν ταῖς ἡμέραις' — 'en tais hemerais' — in those days. Set expression. 'εἰς τὸ ὄρος' - 'eis to oros' — into a mountain; onto the mountain. Where to. 'ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ' - 'en te proseukhe' — in high, spiritualised prayer.

Luke VI, 13 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτῶν' — 'ap auton' — of them. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates that Jesus separated the twelve from all the others.

Luke VI, 17 — 'ἀπὸ πάσης' — 'apo pases' — from all. 'ἀπὸ τῶν νόσων' - 'apo ton noson' — from their diseases. The preposition 'apo' emphasizes that people completely left the surroundings, and also completely got rid of their diseases — a full, complete healing. The very word healing [ztsilennya] shares a root with the word whole [tsilyi].

Luke VI, 18 — 'ἀπὸ πνευμάτων ἀκαθάρτων' — 'apo pneumaton akatharton' — from unclean spirits. See just above.

Luke VI, 19 — 'παρ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'par autou' — from beside Him. The preposition 'para' here takes the genitive case. Such a construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father in the Godhead, regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ, regarding the Eternal Council of the Most Holy Trinity, and regarding the entire Divine design. I have written previously that this parallel is essential and mysterious — here it underlines the fullness of the Holy Spirit in Christ according to His human nature — through His human nature people received Divine gifts and healings. He is truly the promised Anointed One, the Messiah, the Christ. See also the links:
