Liturgy:
(Galatians III, 15-22)
Galatians III, 17 — 'ὑπὸ τοῦ θεοῦ' — 'hypo tou theou' — by God. The preposition 'hypo' with the genitive case indicates here the passive voice of the participle confirmed (by Whom). 'εἰς τὸ καταργῆσαι' - 'eis to katargesai' — that it should make of none effect; to annul. That is (since a negation stands here), it does not lead to that.

Galatians III, 18, 21 — 'ἐκ νόμου' — 'ek nomou' — of the Law. 'ἐξ ἐπαγγελίας' - 'ex epangelias' — by promise. The preposition 'ek' in its various forms here emphasizes the source from which the inheritance comes, by virtue of what.

Galatians III, 19 — 'ἐν χειρί' — 'en kheiri' — in the hand; by the hand. Rather the second translation option, as it is significantly clearer. A concise factual explanation of the meaning of the Lord granting the Law to Moses. Further it is said that Moses is called a mediator here, but also not only Moses, but prophets, etc., that is, in general the people who received the Old Testament Divine Revelations.

Galatians III, 22 — 'ὑπὸ ἁμαρτίαν' — 'hypo hamartian' — under sin. Here 'hypo' is used with the accusative case, carrying the meaning of locking the lock, that is, the Law of Moses was given to reveal sin clearly and to show that people need salvation from it in God. 'ἡ ἐπαγγελία ἐκ πίστεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ' - 'he epangelia ek pisteos Iesou CHristou' — the promise by faith of Jesus Christ; the promise by faith, of Jesus Christ.
