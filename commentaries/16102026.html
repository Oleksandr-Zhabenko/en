Liturgy:
(Philippians III, 8-19)
Philippians III, 9 — 'ἐν αὐτῷ' — 'en auto' — in Him. 'ἐκ νόμου' - 'ek nomou' — of the Law / from the Law. 'τὴν ἐκ θεοῦ δικαιοσύνην ἐπὶ τῇ πίστει' - 'ten ek theou dikaiosynen epi te pistei' — the righteousness which is of God (based on the) by the faith. The preposition 'ek' here indicates origin.

Philippians III, 11 — 'εἰς τὴν ἐξανάστασιν τὴν ἐκ νεκρῶν' — 'eis ten exanastasin ten ek nekron' — unto the resurrection of the dead. That is, it speaks of attaining the resurrection. The preposition 'ek' indicates here from where the transition occurs — from death unto life.

Philippians III, 12 — 'ὑπὸ Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ' — 'hypo CHristou Iesou' — by Jesus Christ. Here the preposition 'hypo' with the genitive case indicates the passive voice I am apprehended (by Whom).

Philippians III, 14 — 'εἰς τὸ βραβεῖον' — 'eis to brabeion' — for the prize / unto the prize. Both translation options are possible, though the first is clearer and simpler. 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' - 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Jesus Christ.

Philippians III, 16 — 'εἰς ὃ' — 'eis ho' — whereunto / unto that which. The preposition 'eis' indicates here drawing near to and attaining.

Philippians III, 19 — 'ἐν τῇ αἰσχύνῃ' — 'en te aiskhyne' — in their shame.

At the beginning of the reading, Paul says that in Christ there is everything needed, whereas if someone proposes to him something instead of Christ (anti-Christ), he counts all that as loss—the more decisively, the further it is from Christ. He chooses a sharp expression to underline the madness of those who depart from Christ for the sake of something else.

Speaking of perfection in Philippians III, 13, 15, the apostle implies that perfection consists in attaining God's goal in Christ. The apostle pursues this goal, exerting effort to attain it, thus being an example of constant spiritual growth.

(Luke VII, 31-35)
Luke VII, 32 — 'ἐν ἀγορᾷ' — 'en agora' — in the marketplace. Where. In the place of gathering for transacting business and trade.

Luke VII, 35 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν τέκνων' — 'apo ton teknon' — of all (its) her children. Here the preposition 'apo' emphasizes that wisdom has received a testimony that can be provided separately from the state of wisdom's children (that is, her own children for wisdom, and not those who were just spoken of), that is, it underlines the impartiality of Jesus' words, their objectivity.
