Liturgy:
(Colossians II, 1-7)
Colossians II, 1 — 'τῶν ἐν Λαοδικίᾳ' — 'ton en Laodikia' — them at Laodicea. Where. 'τὸ πρόσωπόν μου ἐν σαρκί' - 'to prosopon mou en sarki' — my face in the flesh. That is, they have not seen Paul in the body. The use of flesh here may indicate Paul's age, pointing to the infirmity of the body that might come with age. See also:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/13112024.html
One may think that the apostle refers to his bodily infirmities. I contend for you in my infirmities, I contend also for those who have not seen me, — this nuance may be emphasized.

Colossians II, 2 — 'ἐν ἀγάπῃ' — 'en agape' — in love; by love. Both translation options are possible, with the first emphasizing that love encompasses other virtues, and the second that love itself as the bond of perfection unites people as well. 'εἰς πᾶν πλοῦτος τῆς πληροφορίας τῆς συνέσεως' - 'eis pan ploutos tes plerophorias tes syneseos' — unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding. Very eloquent and lofty words. 'εἰς ἐπίγνωσιν' - 'eis epignosin' — to the acknowledgement / for the knowledge. In both instances the preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action.

Colossians II, 3 — 'ἐν ᾧ' — 'en ho' — in Whom; by Whom. The first option is preferable, as it underlines that in coming to know Christ, one discovers the treasures of wisdom, whereas the second implies that the Father Himself hid these treasures of knowledge by Christ, which raises additional questions, since in reality Christ precisely revealed and manifested the Father to people, as well as Himself.

Colossians II, 4 — 'ἐν πιθανολογίᾳ' — 'en pithanologia' — in enticing words; by persuasive speech. Both translation options are possible. The first option indicates that these false, beguiling words are granted significance by deceitful people (who may even be sincerely convinced of their justice), whereas the second option indicates that they cynically use them for deception as a means.

Colossians II, 5 — 'τὸ στερέωμα τῆς εἰς Χριστὸν πίστεως ὑμῶν' — 'to stereoma tes eis CHriston pisteos hymon' — the stedfastness of your faith in Christ. The preposition 'eis' underlines that faith in Christ is also, and must be, a movement towards Him; He is the Goal and Final Destination of this movement. A typical preposition for faith in Christ, used similarly in other places of the New Testament.

Colossians II, 6, 7 — 'ἐν αὐτῷ' — 'en auto' — in Him; by Him. Both translation options are possible, though the first is easier to understand, whilst the second has parallels with Acts XVII, 28. See the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/26092026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/03102025.html

Colossians II, 7 — 'ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ' — 'en eukharistia' — in thanksgiving; by thanksgiving. Both translation options are possible; the first underlines that thanksgiving often includes other virtues, whilst the second points out that thanksgiving itself is a wealth and is capable of overflowing a person with joy.

The Apostle Paul says that although he is absent in the flesh, he supports the faith of the Colossians, as well as the Laodiceans, including those who have never seen Paul personally.
What is important is that in Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom. This means that all wisdom, everything that cannot easily be acquired from experience or simple knowledge, everything essential and substantial must be sought in Christ. Whereas that which lies on the surface, which is easily seen and obvious, a human being can see and come to know by virtue of their natural capacities. This latter also occurs with the participation of Christ, yet implicitly, so that they themselves may not know it until God reveals it, if only at the Last Judgment.
Indeed, all truly deep knowledge, cognition, and wisdom are in Christ, though they may be as if hidden, requiring revelation; and this revelation is impossible unless God grants it and people synergise with God in this. But when such revelation occurs, it means God reveals it, and people synergise with God. And this is directly connected with the Incarnate Son of God.

(Luke IX, 12-18)
Luke IX, 12 — 'εἰς τὰς κύκλῳ κώμας' — 'eis tas kyklo komas' — into the towns round about. Where to. 'ἐν ἐρήμῳ τόπῳ' - 'en eremo topo' — in a desert place. Where.

Luke IX, 13 — 'εἰς πάντα τὸν λαὸν τοῦτον' — 'eis panta ton laon touton' — for all this people. Here the preposition 'eis' indicates for whom the food is intended.

Luke IX, 16 — 'εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν' — 'eis ton ouranon' — up to the sky; into the heaven. Where to.

Luke IX, 18 — 'ἐν τῷ εἶναι' — 'en to einai' — as He was; whilst. In sense, the second translation option is more suited to standard rendering.

The miracle of the feeding of the 5,000 people, as well as the question of Who Jesus is.
