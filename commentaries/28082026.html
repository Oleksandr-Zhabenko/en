Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians XI, 5-21)
2 Corinthians XI, 6 — 'ἐν παντὶ φανερώσαντες ἐν πᾶσιν εἰς ὑμᾶς' — 'en panti phanerosantes en pasin eis hymas' — made manifest in all things among you for your sake. The preposition 'eis' indicates the purpose here.

2 Corinthians XI, 9 — 'ἀπὸ Μακεδονίας' — 'apo Makedonias' — from Macedonia. The preposition 'apo' emphasizes that they left Macedonia. 'ἐν παντί' - 'en panti' — in all things.

2 Corinthians XI, 10 — 'εἰς ἐμὲ ἐν τοῖς κλίμασιν τῆς Ἀχαΐας' — 'eis eme en tois klimasin tes Akhaias' — unto me in the regions of Achaia. For whom and where.

2 Corinthians XI, 12, 21 — 'ἐν ᾧ καυχῶνται' — 'en ho kaukhontai' — wherein they boast; in what they glory. Both translation options are possible. See also about the nuances of translating such constructions at the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

2 Corinthians XI, 13 — 'εἰς ἀποστόλους' — 'eis apostolous' — into apostles. The ostentatious transformation is emphasized.

2 Corinthians XI, 14 — 'εἰς ἄγγελον' — 'eis angelon' — into an angel. The transformation is emphasized.

2 Corinthians XI, 17, 21 — 'ἐν ἀφροσύνῃ' — 'en aphrosyne' — foolishly; in foolishness. 'ἐν ταύτῃ' - 'en taute' — in this same; by this same.

2 Corinthians XI, 20 — 'εἰς πρόσωπον' — 'eis prosopon' — in the face.

2 Corinthians XI, 21 — 'ἐν ᾧ' — 'en ho' — wherein; in what. Both translation options are possible. 'ἐν ἀφροσύνῃ' - 'en aphrosyne' — in lack of sense; foolishly; unwisely. How. The apostle does not encourage speaking about oneself and one's achievements. Here he as it were enters into a contest over who is greater, which Christ also teaches us to avoid, but he does it for the salvation of the disciples. See more at the links:

(Mark IV, 1-9)
Mark IV, 1 — 'εἰς πλοῖον' — 'eis ploion' — into a ship. Where to. 'ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ' - 'en te thalasse' — in the sea. Where.

Mark IV, 2 — 'ἐν παραβολαῖς πολλά' — 'en parabolais polla' — many things in parables; by many parables. Both translation options are possible. 'ἐν τῇ διδαχῇ αὐτοῦ' - 'en te didakhe autou' — in His doctrine.

Mark IV, 4 — 'ἐν τῷ σπείρειν' — 'en to speirein' — as he sowed; during the sowing. When. 'παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν' - 'para ten hodon' — to the proximity with the road. The preposition 'para' here is with the accusative case and indicates that the seed fell nearer to the road.

Mark IV, 7 — 'εἰς τὰς ἀκάνθας' — 'eis tas akanthas' — among thorns. Where to.

Mark IV, 8 — 'εἰς τὴν γῆν τὴν καλήν' — 'eis ten gen ten kalen' — into good ground; into very good ground. Where to. 'εἰς τριάκοντα καὶ εἰς ἑξήκοντα καὶ εἰς ἑκατόν' - 'eis triakonta kai eis hexekonta kai eis hekaton' — thirtyfold, and sixtyfold, and an hundredfold. How many fold.

(1 Corinthians II, 6-9)
1 Corinthians II, 6 — 'ἐν τοῖς τελείοις' — 'en tois teleiois' — among them that are perfect; among the mature.

1 Corinthians II, 7 — 'ἐν μυστηρίῳ' — 'en mysterio' — in a mystery. 'εἰς δόξαν ἡμῶν' - 'eis doxan hemon' — unto our glory; for our glory. Here 'eis' indicates the goal toward which movement takes place according to God's design.

1 Corinthians II, 9 — 'ἀνέβη' — 'anebe' — [neither] entered [into the heart of man].

Paul previously said that the apostles' preaching is not built on the ability to speak eloquently, but on the truth, the action of the Holy Spirit, accompanied by various miracles and signs.
Here further he continues, saying that in order to know such preaching, in order to see wisdom in it, one needs to be mature, to a certain extent perfect (not in an absolute sense as something exceptional or extraordinary, but in the sense of mature, having reached a certain depth of life, this is connected with knowing and living according to the so-called meaning and goal of life), for example, knowing well the testimony of one's conscience.
Then he says that the apostles' wisdom is from God — the Lord designed to save people from before the ages. The Fathers explain in this way that the Son of God would have to become incarnate in any case, because this is important for the divinisation of people, so that people might become children of God by grace.
And further Paul speaks precisely about divinisation and Jesus Christ, although he does not name it directly. This is visible from the indicated word 'ἀνέβη'. That is, in joyful, solemn, and magnificent states one can figuratively say that the heart of human being ascends, and here Paul says that human being has never attained such a state as the Kingdom of God will be, such an elevation from God.

(Matthew XXII, 15-22)
Matthew XXII, 15 — 'ὅπως αὐτὸν παγιδεύσωσιν ἐν λόγῳ' — 'hopos auton pagideusosin en logo' — how they might entangle Him in His talk; so that they might entangle Him in talk. All translation options are possible.

Matthew XXII, 16 — 'ἐν ἀληθείᾳ' — 'en aletheia' — in truth; truly. That is, how. 'εἰς πρόσωπον ἀνθρώπων' - 'eis prosopon anthropon' — into the face of people. That is, you do not try to fit into social perceptions.

The deceit and feigned righteousness of those who asked became manifest in their very question. If Christ is a Teacher from God (in fact, is God Himself and Lord), then why did they want to test Him? Ought they not rather to obey? Taxes — it refers to a poll tax on the person or property, rather than certain fees for using something. In Antiquity people perceived money as belonging to the ruler. Also, the concept of human rights in the modern sense did not exist, although the Roman state was effectively the first constitutional state in a sense close to modern understanding. If a person paid such a tax for themselves, they witnessed their loyalty to the authority in the person of Caesar, effectively showing their citizenship by it. And the Lord says to render to the state and society what is due, and also what is due — to God. Order is important. First one needs to determine what belongs to whom, and then render it. This means that it is always a subject for investigation (to a certain extent), but at the same time Jesus' answer shows that a correct answer to it always exists, and one needs to choose and fulfill it. Simultaneously this clearly shows that the existence of authority as an institution corresponds to God's will.
