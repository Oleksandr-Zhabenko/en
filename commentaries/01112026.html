(Luke XVI, 19-31)
Luke XVI, 21 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν πιπτόντων ἀπὸ τῆς τραπέζης τοῦ πλουσίου' — 'apo ton piptonton apo tes trapezes tou plousiou' — from the crumbs falling from the rich man's table. The preposition 'apo' indicates that these crumbs were tiny in comparison with the rich man's feast, and also that in such a case, had they actually been given to Lazarus, it would have been a gift from the rich man to him.

Luke XVI, 22 — 'ὑπὸ τῶν ἀγγέλων' — 'hypo ton angelon' — by the angels. The preposition 'hypo' here takes the genitive case, indicating the passive voice of the verb was carried, as well as who carried Lazarus's soul to Abraham's bosom. 'εἰς τὸν κόλπον Ἀβραάμ' - 'eis ton kolpon Abraam' — into Abraham's bosom. Where to.

Luke XVI, 23 — 'ἐν τῷ ᾅδῃ' — 'en to hade' — in hell / in Hades. Where. 'ἐν βασάνοις' - 'en basanois' — being in torments. In what state. 'ἀπὸ μακρόθεν' - 'apo makrothen' — afar off. Set expression. 'ἐν τοῖς κόλποις αὐτοῦ' - 'en tois kolpois autou' — in his bosom. Where.

Luke XVI, 24 — 'ἐν τῇ φλογὶ ταύτῃ' — 'en te phlogi taute' — in this flame. In what state.

Luke XVI, 25 — 'ἐν τῇ ζωῇ σου' — 'en te zoe sou' — in thy lifetime. When.

Luke XVI, 26 — 'ἐν πᾶσι τούτοις' — 'en pasi toutois' — in all these things. That is, in addition to all that.

Luke XVI, 27 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκον τοῦ πατρός μου' — 'eis ton oikon tou patros mou' — to my father's house. Where to.

Luke XVI, 28 — 'εἰς τὸν τόπον τοῦτον τῆς βασάνου' — 'eis ton topon touton tes basanou' — into this place of torment. Where to.

Luke XVI, 30 — 'ἐάν τις ἀπὸ νεκρῶν πορευθῇ πρὸς αὐτούς' — 'ean tis apo nekron poreuthe pros autous' — if one went unto them from the dead. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that the brothers considered themselves completely unattached to the world of the dead, regarding themselves as alive — and here an appearance of a dead man! It also underlines that such an appearance implies the deceased person has entirely left the world of the dead, that is, risen.

Luke XVI, 31 — 'τις ἐκ νεκρῶν ἀναστῇ' — 'tis ek nekron anaste' — one rose from the dead. The preposition 'ek' indicates from where the transition took place — from the dead to the living — and here too a visible and clear parallel with the Resurrection of Christ Himself is present. That is, if they did not accept the preparation for Christ in the form of the Law and the Prophets, then they will not accept Christ Himself either. If they do not seek spiritual life, righteousness, and holiness, even miracles will not help.
