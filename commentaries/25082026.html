Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians VIII, 16-IX, 5)
2 Corinthians VIII, 16 — 'ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ Τίτου' — 'en te kardia Titou' — in the heart of Titus. That is, that God granted Titus such earnest care in his heart. Where.

2 Corinthians VIII, 18 — 'ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ' — 'en to euangelio' — in the Gospel. That is, they praise the brother both for a life according to the Gospel and praise him according to the Gospel. Two meanings.

2 Corinthians VIII, 19 — 'ὑπὸ τῶν ἐκκλησιῶν' — 'hypo ton ekklesion' — by the Churches. The preposition 'hypo' with the genitive case indicates here the passive voice of the participle chosen. 'προθυμίαν' - 'prothymian' — readiness to act, willingness.

2 Corinthians VIII, 20 — 'ἐν τῇ ἁδρότητι' — 'en te hadroteti' — in this abundance; by this abundance. Both options are possible, the exact meaning of which is not completely clear, but the general thought is obvious. 'ὑφ’ ἡμῶν' - 'hyph hemon' — by us. The preposition 'hypo' in its form before a following 'he' here is used with the genitive case and indicates by whom the ministry is performed, pointing to the passive voice.

2 Corinthians VIII, 22, 23, IX, 5 — 'ἐν πολλοῖς' — 'en pollois' — in many things; in many matters. In what. 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' - 'eis hymas' — toward you; for you.

2 Corinthians VIII, 24 — 'εἰς αὐτοὺς' — 'eis autous' — to them. 'εἰς πρόσωπον' - 'eis prosopon' — before the face; before. Set expression.

2 Corinthians IX, 1 — 'εἰς τοὺς ἁγίους' — 'eis tous hagious' — to the saints; for the saints. Here it refers to helping with material gifts for the faithful, particularly for supporting the activity of the Church overall. Both translation options are possible.

2 Corinthians IX, 2 — 'ἀπὸ πέρυσι' — 'apo perysi' — since last year. Set expression. The preposition 'apo' indicates here the starting point of time regarding an important change.

2 Corinthians IX, 3 — 'ἐν τῷ μέρει' — 'en to merei' - in this behalf; in this case. Set expression.

2 Corinthians IX, 4 — 'ἐν τῇ ὑποστάσει ταύτῃ' — 'en te hypostasei taute' — in this confidence; in this matter. Here an expression is used that is only generally understood.

Paul speaks of Titus and other helpers, praises them and also the Corinthians for the care shown and labor for the glory of God. He also prepares the Corinthians for later generosity.

(Mark III, 13-19)
Mark III, 13 — 'εἰς τὸ ὄρος' — 'eis to oros' — into a mountain; onto a mountain. Where to.

Mark III, 19 — 'εἰς οἶκον' — 'eis oikon' — into an house. Where to.

The names of the apostles in different Gospels differ somewhat, since some of the apostles had two names. Sons of thunder occurs only in Mark. It is considered that it refers to their zeal, disposition, character, and also a certain prophecy about their gifts.

Apostle Titus of the Seventy, Bishop of Crete:
(Titus I, 1-4; II, 15-III, 3, 12-13, 15; Matthew V, 14-19)
Titus I, 3 — 'ἐν κηρύγματι' — 'en kerygmati' — in preaching; through preaching. Both translation options are possible; the first emphasizes that it refers to Christ as the Word, and the second — to God's words, that is, to the content and meaning of the preaching.

Titus I, 4 — 'ἀπὸ θεοῦ πατρὸς καὶ Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ τοῦ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν' — 'apo theou patros kai CHristou Iesou tou soteros hemon' — from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ our Saviour. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that grace and peace are God's gifts to people.

Titus III, 3 — 'ἐν κακίᾳ καὶ φθόνῳ' — 'en kakia kai phthono' — in malice and envy; by malice and envy. Both translation options are possible; the first indicates that malice and envy (rather referring to thoughts, but followed also by deeds) were the usual state of both Saul, who became the Apostle Paul, and perhaps of Titus himself, about which is not generally known; while the second indicates that people tried to live by spreading precisely malice and envy.

Titus III, 12 — 'εἰς Νικόπολιν' — 'eis Nikopolin' — to Nicopolis. Where to.

Titus III, 15 — 'ἐν πίστει' — 'en pistei' — in the faith. That is, whoever shares the common faith in Christ and loves.
