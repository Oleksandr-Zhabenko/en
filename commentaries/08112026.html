Liturgy:
(Ephesians II, 4-10)
Ephesians II, 4 — 'ἐν ἐλέει' — 'en eleei' — in mercy; by mercy. Both translation options are possible, and both complement each other: the first emphasizes that mercy is the general character of God, He is Merciful; whilst the second points out that God abundantly manifests various mercies.

Ephesians II, 6 — 'ἐν τοῖς ἐπουρανίοις ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en tois epouraniois en CHristo Iesou' — in above-heavenly places in Christ Jesus. See regarding above-heavenly places in Christ at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/10102024.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/16102024.html

Ephesians II, 7 — 'ἐν τοῖς αἰῶσιν τοῖς ἐπερχομένοις' — 'en tois aiosin tois eperkhomenois' — in the ages to come. That is, in the future. When. 'ἐν χρηστότητι' - 'en khrestoteti' — in kindness; by kindness. Analogous to the previous verse 4, both translation options are possible, complementing each other. 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' - 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Christ Jesus. In Whom.

Ephesians II, 8 — 'ἐξ ὑμῶν' — 'ex hymon' — of yourselves / from yourselves. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following 'hy' indicates here the source of origin, and the negation shows that salvation and grace originate from God.

Ephesians II, 9 — 'ἐξ ἔργων' — 'ex ergon' — of works / from works. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates here substantial grounds, answering the question on what basis?.

Ephesians II, 10 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Jesus Christ; by Jesus Christ. Both translation options are possible, although the first means that the Most Holy Trinity creates the human being, with the Son being the Pattern, Model, Example, Goal, and Power of creation and creature; whilst the second emphasizes the action of the Father, accomplished by the Son of God as the Immediate Source. Indeed, the Holy Spirit is also implied implicitly. Overall — the Creator is the entire Trinity, with each Person, Hypostasis participating in Her own distinct manner. 'ἐν αὐτοῖς' - 'en autois' — in them.

A general, profound theological reading in which Paul writes what God's plan is regarding the world, how salvation occurs, what it testifies to, and unto what God calls humanity.
Individual parts of the reading echo others that we have already analyzed.

When it is said in verses 6-7 that God raised us up together with Christ and made us sit in glory to reveal Him, it refers primarily to the human nature of Jesus Christ, which is common to all people; therefore all people in Christ are saved, delivered, redeemed, resurrected, ascended, and unto all the riches of God's goodness are revealed.
Yet here it also concerns how through Christ people themselves, the hypostases of human nature, enter into this divine plan of salvation and the Kingdom of God.

(Luke VIII, 26-39)
Luke VIII, 26 — 'εἰς τὴν χώραν τῶν Γεργεσηνῶν' — 'eis ten khoran ton Gergesenon' — into the country of the Gadarenes (Gergesenes). Where to.

Luke VIII, 27 — 'ἐκ τῆς πόλεως' — 'ek tes poleos' — out of the city. The preposition 'ek' indicates from where the man was. 'ἐν οἰκίᾳ' - 'en oikia' — in a house. 'ἐν τοῖς μνήμασιν' - 'en tois mnemasin' — in the tombs. In both cases where.

Luke VIII, 29, 33 — 'ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου' — 'apo tou anthropou' — out of the man / from the man. The preposition 'apo' indicates that the unclean spirit was to depart completely, entirely, utterly. 'ὑπὸ τοῦ δαιμονίου' - 'hypo tou daimoniou' — by the demon. The preposition 'hypo' with the genitive case underlines here the state of dependency, captivity, possession; that is, it underlines the passive voice of the verb was driven and who drove him. 'εἰς τὰς ἐρήμους' - 'eis tas eremous' — into the wilderness. Where to.

Luke VIII, 30 — 'εἰς αὐτόν' — 'eis auton' — into him. Where to.

Luke VIII, 31 — 'εἰς τὴν ἄβυσσον' — 'eis ten abysson' — into the deep / into the abyss. Where to.

Luke VIII, 32 — 'ἐν τῷ ὄρει' — 'en to orei' — on the mountain. Set expression. 'εἰς ἐκείνους' - 'eis ekeinous' — into them. Emphasizes the desire of the unclean spirits and that they could possess the herd of swine only by God's permission. If you wish to know more, please write to me.

Luke VIII, 33 — 'εἰς τοὺς χοίρους' — 'eis tous khoirous' — into the swine. 'εἰς τὴν λίμνην' - 'eis ten limnen' — into the lake. Where to.

Luke VIII, 34 — 'εἰς τὴν πόλιν καὶ εἰς τοὺς ἀγρούς' — 'eis ten polin kai eis tous agrous' — in the city and in the country. Here the dynamic preposition 'eis' is used instead of 'en', since it concerns the phenomenon of spreading reports and information. The preposition 'eis' emphasizes the dissemination of the story.

Luke VIII, 35, 38 — 'ἀφ’ οὗ' — 'aph hou' — out of whom / from whom. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following 'hou' indicates that they departed completely, entirely, utterly. 'παρὰ τοὺς πόδας' - 'para tous podas' — to the proximity with the feet / willing to be close to the feet. The preposition 'para' with the accusative case indicates here the healed man's effort to draw near to the feet of Jesus, that is, humbly unto Himself.

Luke VIII, 37 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτῶν' — 'ap auton' — from them. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates that they besought Jesus to depart. 'εἰς πλοῖον' - 'eis ploion' — into the ship. Where to.

Luke VIII, 39 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκόν σου' — 'eis ton oikon sou' — to your own house / into your house. Where to.

The healing of the Gadarene demoniac. Luke emphasizes the miraculous nature in Jesus' actions, as scholars testify.
