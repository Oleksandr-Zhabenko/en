Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians IX, 12-X, 7)
2 Corinthians IX, 13 — 'εἰς τὸ εὐαγγελίον τοῦ Χριστοῦ' — 'eis to euangelion tou CHristou' — unto the Gospel of Christ. The preposition 'eis' indicates here that for the sake of which obedience is rendered — the accomplishment of the Gospel (Good News) of Jesus Christ concerning salvation and restoration. 'εἰς αὐτοὺς καὶ εἰς πάντας' - 'eis autous kai eis pantas' — unto them, and unto all people. The preposition 'eis' emphasizes the purpose of the activity.

2 Corinthians X, 1 — 'ἐν ὑμῖν' — 'en hymin' — among you; in your presence. 'θαρρῶ' - 'tharro' — (I) am bold (in a good sense), have confidence/zeal. 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' - 'eis hymas' — toward you; regarding you. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the directionality of action — regarding whom.

2 Corinthians X, 2 — 'θαρρῆσαι' — 'tharresai' — to be bold, confident, show zeal. 'τολμῆσαι' - 'tolmesai' — to dare, be courageous. The first of these two words refers more to a state of calm boldness or sustained confidence, whereas the second refers to a decisive transition or act of daring.

2 Corinthians X, 3 — 'ἐν σαρκί' — 'en sarki' — in flesh. 'στρατευόμεθα' - 'strateuometha' — we war, fight as soldiers. From this word also comes strategy — that is, Paul means a wise struggle built on reason rather than on emotions. The life of the apostles (and with them the faithful who are like them) is akin to a large-scale overcoming of evil. Further Paul says that the spiritual weapons of the faithful are mighty to the pulling down of strongholds (capable of overcoming great and widespread evil).

2 Corinthians X, 5 — 'εἰς τὴν ὑπακοήν' — 'eis ten hypakoen' — into the obedience; unto the obedience. Where to and for what purpose.

2 Corinthians X, 6 — 'ἐν ἑτοίμῳ' — 'en hetoimo' — in readiness. Set expression.

The Apostle Paul encourages helping needy faithful, whom he calls saints (for the sake of God's grace among them, while the faithful themselves are not necessarily personally saints). See more at the links:

(Mark III, 20-27)
Mark III, 21 — 'οἱ παρ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'hoi par autou' — those from beside Him; His kinsmen. That is, those who are close. The preposition 'para' in its form before a following vowel is used here with the genitive case. This construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, regarding the Most Holy Trinity, regarding all of God's design, and regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ. See more at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/Pryjmennyky.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/02082025.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/28082026.html
Here this indicates that it refers to close personal relationships, that is, His kinsmen.

Mark III, 22 — 'ἀπὸ Ἱεροσολύμων' — 'apo Hierosolymon' — from Jerusalem. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that they completely left Jerusalem (and perhaps a spiritual dimension is also implied).

Mark III, 23 — 'ἐν παραβολαῖς' — 'en parabolais' — in parables; by parables. Both translation options are possible; the first indicates the method of expressing thought and logic, the second that the teaching itself had the form of a parable.

Mark III, 27 — 'εἰς τὴν οἰκίαν τοῦ ἰσχυροῦ' — 'eis ten oikian tou iskhyrou' — into a strong man's house. Where to.

The Lord continues His ministry, heals, casts out demons, and here overcomes the objections of the Pharisees and scribes against miracles.
