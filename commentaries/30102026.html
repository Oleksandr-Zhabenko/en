Liturgy:
(Colossians IV, 10-18)
Colossians IV, 11 — 'ἐκ περιτομῆς' — 'ek peritomes' — who are of the circumcision. That is, originating from among the circumcised. 'εἰς τὴν βασιλείαν' - 'eis ten basileian' — unto the Kingdom / for the Kingdom. The preposition 'eis' here indicates the goal.

Colossians IV, 12 — 'ἐξ ὑμῶν' — 'ex hymon' — who is one of you. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates here selection from a group of people. 'ἐν ταῖς προσευχαῖς' - 'en tais proseukhais' — in high, inspired prayers; by high, inspired prayers. Both translation options are possible. 'ἐν παντὶ θελήματι τοῦ θεοῦ' - 'en panti thelemati tou theou' — in all the will of God; by all the will of God. Both translation options are possible, with the first indicating that the Lord wishes us to be filled with good, whilst the second points to what precisely is to be filled with, that is, what ought to be fully accomplished.

Colossians IV, 13, 15 — 'ἐν Λαοδικίᾳ' — 'en Laodikia' — in Laodicea. Where. 'ἐν Ἱεραπόλει' - 'en Hierapolei' — in Hierapolis. Where.

Colossians IV, 14 — 'Λουκᾶς ὁ ἰατρὸς ὁ ἀγαπητὸς' — 'Loukas ho iatros ho agapetos' — Luke, the beloved physician. Mention is made of the apostle and evangelist Luke, my beloved saint.

Colossians IV, 16 — 'παρ’ ὑμῖν' — 'par hymin' — in the vicinity of you / near you. The preposition 'para' in its form before a following vowel takes the dative case here and indicates that even the illiterate were meant to hear, that is, the reading allows the letter to be shared among the entire community of the faithful. 'ἐν τῇ Λαοδικέων ἐκκλησίᾳ' - 'en te Laodikoen ekklesia' — in the church of the Laodiceans. Where. 'ἐκ Λαοδικίας' - 'ek Laodikias' — from Laodicea. The preposition 'ek' here indicates the location from which that other letter comes. This verse clearly indicates the existence of a sister Epistle to the Laodiceans which has not been preserved.

Colossians IV, 17 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. Here — as a servant of the Lord and an executor of His will.

The Apostle Paul writes concluding greetings in the letter and speaks of his intentions and those of his co-workers, among whom Luke is mentioned, the beloved physician by profession and line of activity. It is considered that Luke is so named not only because he treated Paul and assisted him, but also on account of his devotion, for he did not abandon him even at a time when all others had previously left (thus, their friendship was tested).
