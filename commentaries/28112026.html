(Galatians III, 8-12)
Galatians III, 8 — 'ἐκ πίστεως δικαιοῖ τὰ ἔθνη' — 'ek pisteos dikaioi ta ethne' — would justify the the nations through faith / from faith justifies the nations; from faith justifies the Gentiles. Various translation options are possible. The preposition 'ek' indicates what will become the cause and source of justification. 'ἐν σοὶ πάντα τὰ ἔθνη' - 'en soi panta ta ethne' — in you shall all nations / in you all the nations; by you all the nations. Rather the first option, since Abraham was not the Redeemer and Saviour; rather, Jesus Christ is — the Offspring and at the same time the Root of Abraham, seeing Whom he was established in faith.

Galatians III, 9, 11, 12 — 'ἐκ πίστεως' — 'ek pisteos' — of faith / from faith. The preposition 'ek' indicates the significance of faith.

Galatians III, 10 — 'ἐξ ἔργων νόμου' — 'ex ergon nomou' — of the works of the Law / from works of the Law. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates what is essential in their faith and piety. 'ὑπὸ κατάραν' - 'hypo kataran' — under the curse / under a curse (here rather in time, that is, still under the operation of the curse, not yet redeemed). The preposition 'hypo' here takes the accusative case. 'ἐν τῷ βιβλίῳ τοῦ νόμου' - 'en to biblio tou nomou' — in the book of the Law. Where.

Galatians III, 11 — 'ἐν νόμῳ' — 'en nomo' — by the Law / in the Law. 'παρὰ τῷ θεῷ' - 'para to theo' — in the vicinity of God / near God. The preposition 'para' here takes the dative case, indicating that works of the Law do not save a person in God's presence/closeness — that is, the rites of the Law of Moses pale before God and do not of themselves save a person.

Galatians III, 12 — 'ἐν αὐτοῖς' — 'en autois' — in them; by them. Both translation options are possible.

(Luke IX, 57-62)
Luke IX, 57 — 'ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ' — 'en te hodo' — in the way / on the road. Where and when.

Luke IX, 61 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκόν' — 'eis ton oikon' — into the house. By sense — to those of my household.

Luke IX, 62 — 'εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω' — 'eis ta opiso' — backwards / into the things behind. That is, backwards.

For the parallel passage to verses 57-60 (Matthew VIII, 19-22), see the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/27062026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/05072025.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/01072023.html

Verses 61-62 are similar in meaning to the preceding ones: Christ states that one must possess decisiveness, irreversibility, and finality in the decision to follow Christ. Otherwise, a person is not steadfast in their striving, and therefore is not reliable with respect to entering the Kingdom of God in the Gospel sense, of which I have written previously several times — being able already in this life to live consciously and fulfil God's will.
There is an apparent parallel with 1 (3) Kings XIX, 20, where Elisha asked, before becoming a disciple and later successor to Elijah, to bid farewell to his father. Elijah permitted it, whereas Jesus is Greater than Elijah. The calling of Christians to follow Christ is even weightier than the calling to become a disciple of the great prophet Elijah.
