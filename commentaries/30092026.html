Ephesians V, 26 — 'ἐν ῥήματι' — 'en rhemati' — in the word; in the matter; with a word; by a matter. A polysemous word and overall phrase, but it certainly does not refer solely to words, but to the essence of the matter.

The preposition 'eis' here indicates who those people will become — a single whole. These important words are a Mystery of God — for instance, one can pose the question as to why specifically one flesh. Just above in the verse it spoke of shall be joined (that is, an image of bodily union), and further it speaks of flesh. One can recall the dialogue with the Sadducees, where Christ said that after the Resurrection they do not marry. I think a person feels the body almost always, but not everything a person feels has a bodily origin and source. The bodily, however, is often that which can be most felt, tested, fixed, touched (let us recall Thomas the Apostle). That is, the unity of two in Marriage is not absolute, but is so essential that it cannot be neglected or arbitrarily disposed of.

Ephesians V, 32 — 'εἰς Χριστόν' — 'eis CHriston' — concerning Christ; for Christ. 'εἰς τὴν ἐκκλησίαν' - 'eis ten ekklesian' — concerning the church; for the church. In sense, the second option in both cases.

Paul writes about love among Christians. It includes humility, a true vision of oneself. And right here he writes about the life of married spouses. Marriage in the Lord is a Mystery, a Sacrament, carrying a profound meaning, and Paul here likens it to the union of Christ and the Church. Also right here is a continuation regarding mutual humility. Many people pay attention to the fact that a wife ought to be humble regarding her husband. But not everyone pays attention to the fact that a husband, if he is like Christ, must give himself sacrificially for the sake of his wife, just as Christ gave Himself up for the salvation of the Church unto crucifixion and death on the cross. The husband is thus called to no less humility than the wife by this comparison. That is, one must understand that Paul speaks of the mutual humility of spouses, of mutual consent, of harmony and mutual understanding. (Luke IV, 1-15)
Luke IV, 1 — 'ἀπὸ τοῦ Ἰορδάνου' — 'apo tou Iordanou' — from Jordan. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that Jesus completely left the lands near the Jordan. 'ἐν τῷ πνεύματι' - 'en to pneumati' — in the Spirit / by the Spirit. In Whom. 'ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ' - 'en te eremo' — in the wilderness. Where.

Luke IV, 2 — 'ὑπὸ τοῦ διαβόλου' — 'hypo tou diabolou' — by the devil. The preposition 'hypo' here is with the genitive case. I explained in detail one of the depths of this passage at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/10012026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/11012025.html
Matthew IV, 1 — 'εἰς τὴν ἔρημον' — 'eis ten eremon' — into the wilderness. 'ὑπὸ τοῦ πνεύματος πειρασθῆναι ὑπὸ τοῦ διαβόλου' - 'hypo tou pneumatos peirasthenai hypo tou diabolou' - by the Spirit, to be tested by the devil. The preposition 'hypo' is used twice with the genitive case, in both cases indicates the passive voice and through whom the events occur here in the text. This use of similar forms expressively emphasises the thought that one and the same events can be desired both by God and by the devil — God desires them as All-knowing, knowing that the consequences will be good, and the devil — in vain hope to tempt. This is important to understand, since often people think only dichotomically — that the desires of the devil are always opposed to the desires of God. Such an incorrect thought of people contradicts the foundations of faith, since then the person thinks that the devil supposedly knows all God's desires in order to oppose them, although actually for knowledge of God's desires, His holy will union with God's grace is needed, and the devil does not seek it. This reading today brightly testifies about another thing. This also means that the evil one often does not know God's will in order to directly oppose it, but opposes it in general, in particular regarding God's commandments known also to him.
Luke IV, 2 — 'ἐν ταῖς ἡμέραις ἐκείναις' — 'en tais hemerais ekeinais' — in those days. When.

Luke IV, 5 — 'ἐν στιγμῇ χρόνου' — 'en stigme khronou' — in a moment of time. How, when.

Luke IV, 9 — 'εἰς Ἱερουσαλήμ' — 'eis Hierousalem' — to Jerusalem. Where to.

Luke IV, 13 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'ap autou' — from Him. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel here emphasizes that the devil departed entirely from Jesus.

Luke IV, 14 — 'ἐν τῇ δυνάμει τοῦ πνεύματος εἰς τὴν Γαλιλαίαν' — 'en te dynamei tou pneumatos eis ten Galilaian' — in the power of the Spirit into Galilee.

Luke IV, 15 — 'ἐν ταῖς συναγωγαῖς' — 'en tais synagogais' — in their synagogues. Where. 'ὑπὸ πάντων' - 'hypo panton' — of all / by all. The preposition 'hypo' with the genitive case indicates a passive voice here and that people praised Jesus, rather than He was praising Himself.
