Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians II, 14-III, 3)
2 Corinthians II, 14 — 'ἐν τῷ Χριστῷ' — 'en to CHristo' — in Christ. In Whom. 'ἐν παντὶ τόπῳ' - 'en panti topo' — in every place; everywhere. Where.

2 Corinthians II, 15 — 'ἐν τοῖς σῳζομένοις καὶ ἐν τοῖς ἀπολλυμένοις' — 'en tois sozomenois kai en tois apollymenois' — in those who are being saved and in those who are perishing. The middle-passive voice of the participles here indicates that both groups of people participate to a great extent both in salvation and in perishing, meaning the latter are consequences of precisely those people's actions.

2 Corinthians II, 16 — 'ὀσμὴ ἐκ θανάτου εἰς θάνατον' — 'osme ek thanatou eis thanaton' — a fragrance from death to death. 'ὀσμὴ ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν' - 'osme ek zoes eis zoen' — a fragrance from life to life. Here the prepositional constructions are identical. In meaning, it is about how the ministry of the apostles and the life of the faithful are a kind of benchmark of who is being saved and who is perishing; the responsibility of the ministry of the faithful, and particularly of the apostles themselves, is also noted here.

2 Corinthians II, 17 — 'ὡς ἐξ εἰλικρινίας' — 'hos ex eilikrinias' — as out of sincerity (clarity, purity). 'ὡς ἐκ θεοῦ κατέναντι θεοῦ ἐν Χριστῷ' - 'hos ek theou katenanti theou en CHristo' — as from God before God (opposite God) in Christ. That is, as if proclaiming to God Himself. The apostle preaches as though he were preaching to God Himself — in such purity, sincerity, responsibility, attentiveness, diligence, humility, and the like. And unity with Christ enables him to preach in this way. All preachers of the Gospel are called to this. It must be said that teaching others properly and diligently is the most effective way for the teacher themselves to learn something, better even than practice. If a person wishes to learn something, a faithful person can be advised to tell God about it — of course, not as to someone who does not know, but rather as if at an examination before Him Who knows everything.

2 Corinthians III, 1 — 'ἐξ ὑμῶν' — 'ex hymon' - from you. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following 'hy' indicates here that it refers to letters that express something important from the faithful.

2 Corinthians III, 2 — 'ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ἡμῶν' — 'en tais kardiais hemon' — in our hearts. Where. 'γινωσκομένη καὶ ἀναγινωσκομένη ὑπὸ πάντων ἀνθρώπων' - 'ginoskomene kai anagignoskomene hypo panton anthropon' — known and read by all people. The preposition 'hypo' here is used with the genitive case and indicates the passive voice of the corresponding participles.

2 Corinthians III, 3 — 'ἐπιστολὴ Χριστοῦ διακονηθεῖσα ὑφ’ ἡμῶν' — 'epistole CHristou diakonetheisa hyph hemon' — a letter of Christ ministered by us. That is, obtained as a result of the ministry of the apostles. The preposition 'hypo' in its form before a following 'he' here is used with the genitive case and indicates the passive voice of the participle ministered. 'οὐκ ἐν πλαξὶν λιθίναις ἀλλ’ ἐν πλαξὶν καρδίαις σαρκίναις' - 'ouk en plaxin lithinais all en plaxin kardiais sarkinais' — not on tablets of stone, but on fleshy tablets of the heart. That is, the value of the Gospel lies not in its being written down somewhere as a text, but in its presence within the hearts of the faithful. Here is a direct link to the famous Old Testament prophecy of Jeremiah regarding the New Covenant.

Savor of knowledge — a person is capable of smelling an odour initially even without wishing to do so. Therefore, of all the senses, Paul chooses smell when he speaks of how the apostles' preaching of faith, like a fragrance, cannot simply be ignored or go unnoticed. Thus, whether a person wishes to smell an odour or not, if they perceive it at least once, so it is with the preaching of the apostles — if people accept it, it is unto salvation, and if they reject it, they move towards perishing. But they cannot remain indifferent or ignore it. Verse 15 of Chapter II also carries a deeper meaning — the distinguishing of the apostles among all people through Christ. That is, they are special in Christ among everyone — both those who will be saved and those who will perish; that is, among all people without exception (for every person will either be saved or perish; there is no third option, tertium non datum).

Next comes one of the most beautiful and poetic metaphors in Scripture — the image of Christ's letter of recommendation written on the fleshy tablets of the heart, not with ink, but by the Holy Spirit.

One can put it this way: when, in the faithful who are humane and pure in heart (or at least good-hearted), the Holy Spirit acts through Jesus Christ thanks to the work of the apostles, then they themselves become a confirmation of the truth of the words and preaching of the apostles through whom they received Christ's Good News.

There is also a meaning here regarding the Sacraments of Chrismation and Priesthood, for it is primarily through the Holy Spirit that they are performed, renewing a person from within by His gifts.
