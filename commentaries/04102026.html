Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians IX, 6-11)
2 Corinthians IX, 7 — 'ἐκ λύπης' - 'ek lypes' - out of pain; out of regret; out of sorrow; from pain; from regret; from sorrow. Many variants of translation are possible here, which also add vivid shades of meaning — one must give not because pain, regret or sorrow prompts to it, but out of compassion and mercy, love. 'ἐξ ἀνάγκης' - 'ex anankes' - out of necessity. The preposition 'ek' also, in its form before a following vowel, indicates here whence generosity and self-sacrifice must come — not being a burden, but genuine joy of giving and of becoming like God. True generosity here must be manifested from a complete desire to become like the All-generous God, Who gives even in abundance and without sorrow because of His generosity, quite sincerely.

2 Corinthians IX, 8 — 'περισσευσαι' - 'periesseusai' - to overfill. 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' - 'eis hymas' - into you; to you. The movement of God's grace towards the readers, the faithful, is emphasised. 'ἐν παντὶ πάντοτε πᾶσαν αὐτάρκειαν' - 'en panti pantote pasan autarkeian' - in every rank always all sufficiency. The thought is reinforced by repetitions of similar words. 'εἰς πᾶν ἔργον' - 'eis pan ergon ἀγαθόν' - for every good deed. That is, the purpose of all abundance (whether material or spiritual) is the possibility of doing good more and in every way.

2 Corinthians IX, 9 — 'εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα' - 'eis ton aiona' - for evermore; for ever. A set expression.

2 Corinthians IX, 10 — 'εἰς βρῶσιν' - 'eis brosin' - for food; for nourishment. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the purpose of bread for a human being.

2 Corinthians IX, 11 — 'ἐν παντὶ' - 'en panti' - in everything. 'εἰς πᾶσαν ἁπλότητα' - 'eis pasan haploteta' - for all simplicity; for all sincerity. That is, enrichment with God's grace leads to simplicity (the ability to fulfil God's will) and to sincerity of love and other virtues.

A human being is created such that they can be endowed with every grace. More about this is written at the links:
https://www.facebook.com/Oleksandr.S.Zhabenko/posts/
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/07102023.html
Here Paul writes that a person's generosity in kindness, in good gifts, in help leads to the Lord also endowing the person with His mercies and bounties. First of all this is manifested in graces.
Verse 8, the words from which are mentioned above, speaks of the fact that one must strive for self-sufficiency, but not in isolation, separation, detachment (although this may be necessary and needed in the case of a bad influence, in order to remove or avoid it), but through mutual participation and help. By this a person can become more like God, One in the Holy Trinity.
Verse 11 also emphasises the purifying effect of generosity and giving — a person who gives generously and willingly learns simplicity, sincerity, and this greatly contributes to and leads a person to purity of heart. Therefore all these concepts are even often mentioned together, and one often also implies the presence of the other (others). A simple and sincere person often also has greater purity.

The quotations are from various places in the Old Testament, in particular from the psalms and the prophets.

Paul here almost everywhere, when speaking about you (about the readers, listeners, addressees), speaks about the spiritual life. One can think so, that the Lord gives the spiritual to people who truly are generous, who give willingly, from a sincere heart, generously.

(Luke V, 1-11)
Luke V, 1, 2 — 'ἐν τῷ τὸν ὄχλον ἐπικεῖσθαι αὐτῷ καὶ ἀκούειν τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ' - 'en to ton okhlon epiekisthai auto kai akouein ton logon tou theou' - in that the crowd pressed towards Him and listened to the Word of God. When. 'παρὰ τὴν λίμνην' - 'para ten limnen' - to proximity with the lake. The preposition 'para' here with the accusative indicates proximity and approach to the lake, that is, Jesus deliberately approached the lake, then sat in a boat and preached from the boat. Here one can see a parallel with the preaching of the prophet Jonah (and Simon Peter was also the son of another Jonah), who during the storm acknowledged on the ship that it was because of him. Later, when Christ was forgiving Peter's denial, this could have reminded Peter again of these events and of his following Christ.

Luke V, 2 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτῶν' - 'ap auton' - from them. The preposition 'apo' emphasises that the fishermen were at a distance, the boats stood left alone.

Luke V, 3 — 'ἐμβὰς δὲ εἰς ἓν τῶν πλοίων' - 'embas de eis hen ton ploion' - and He entered into one boat. Where to. 'ἀπὸ τῆς γῆς' - 'apo tes ges' - from the land. That is, further away. 'ἐν τῷ πλοίῳ' - 'en to ploio' - in the boat. Where.

Luke V, 4 — 'εἰς τὸ βάθος' - 'eis to bathos' - into the depths. Where to. 'εἰς ἄγραν' - 'eis agran' - for catching. Here the preposition 'eis' indicates the purpose of the action — in order to catch fish.

Luke V, 7 — 'ἐν τῷ ἑτέρῳ πλοίῳ' - 'en to hetero ploio' - in another boat.

Luke V, 8 — 'ἀπ’ ἐμοῦ' - 'ap emou' - from me. That is, leave me completely.

Luke V, 10 — 'ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν' - 'apo tou nyn' - from now; from now on; henceforth. A set expression.

The story of the second calling by Jesus of the closest disciples. The first is written about in the Gospel according to John I. After that the Apostles continued to be fishermen, but were also disciples of Jesus.
Here, after the miraculous catch of fish, after the miracle that Christ performed, they changed their attitude to all of that and began to regard discipleship to Jesus as the main occupation in their life (nevertheless, they continued to be fishermen right up to Pentecost after the Ascension of Jesus).
The miracle is multivalent, in particular it teaches humility, obedience to God, trust in Him, and shows the disciples what the Lord is calling them to (to apostolic labour).
