Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians V, 1-10)
2 Corinthians V, 1 — 'οἰκοδομὴν ἐκ θεοῦ' — 'oikodomen ek theou' — an edifice from God. The preposition 'ek' indicates origin. That is, God will build the Kingdom of God and in particular the resurrected bodies according to the richness of His design. 'ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς' - 'en tois ouranois' — in the heavens.

2 Corinthians V, 2 — 'καὶ γὰρ ἐν τούτῳ' — 'kai gar en touto' — (and) for in this. 'ἐξ οὐρανοῦ' - 'ex ouranou' — from heaven. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel indicates origin here, from where that desired habitation comes.

2 Corinthians V, 3 — 'οὐ γυμνοὶ' — 'ou gymnoi' — not naked / not dressed merely in undergarments, underwear. The apostle figuratively compares the resurrected bodies of those who will not enter the Kingdom of God to undergarments, that is, here — basic clothing that is not truly an adornment and is not intended to be final, ultimate, and the only one present. Union with God as if clothes a person in another garment — better and intended by God.

2 Corinthians V, 4 — 'ἐν τῷ σκήνει' — 'en to skenei' — in this tabernacle. 'ὑπὸ τῆς ζωῆς' - 'hypo tes zoes' — by life / (swallowed up) by life (that is, eternal, from God). The preposition 'hypo' here takes the genitive case, indicating the passive voice was swallowed up and pointing to the fact that life will swallow up what is mortal. See the parallel with Elijah's sacrifice at the link below. It refers to the capacity of the frail body to decay, break down, deteriorate — all of these will be as if swallowed up by the life that will fill the bodies. Perhaps both possible thoughts are combined here: a person who suffered for Christ will be filled with life that energetically swallows up destruction and wounds, thus the glory of martyrs will be greater. Here one can also see a parallel with Communion — the faithful consume the Body and Blood of Christ, while in the resurrection Life will eat up their wounds and corruption. A kind of divine justice!

2 Corinthians V, 5 — 'εἰς αὐτὸ τοῦτο' — 'eis auto touto' — for the selfsame thing / for this very purpose. The preposition 'eis' here indicates the goal, the final target as a point of movement.

2 Corinthians V, 6 — 'ἐν τῷ σώματι' — 'en to somati' — in the body. That is, having the present body in its current state. 'ἐκδημοῦμεν ἀπὸ τοῦ κυρίου' - 'ekdemoumen apo tou kyriou' — we are absent from the Lord. The preposition 'apo' here (compare with the next) means that our bodies now are completely, entirely unlike God. Paul implies that our present bodies are not as they were intended by God, being like a second temporary building where people live awaiting the true building — from God. Crucially, the resurrected bodies and life in the Kingdom of God can go beyond the limits of spatial confinement, since it concerns God's action of energetic building, rather than merely matter in a altered state.

2 Corinthians V, 8 — 'ἐκδημῆσαι ἐκ τοῦ σώματος' — 'ekdemesai ek tou somatos' — to be absent from the body / to travel out from the body. The preposition 'ek' here indicates an exit from the body of the present age, but not its total abandonment. The point is not to get rid of the body, but for its mortal characteristics to be swallowed up by life from God. See also:
https://www.instagram.com/p/C8AN3CfikTS5Be1rGSSfudPIH8Aa-5L0JW7nCY0/

The Apostle Paul speaks figuratively of the body after the resurrection and the clothing in incorruption as a building from God. A very profound passage, at the same time exceptionally vivid.
The present life in the earthly body the apostle compares with life in the resurrected body (full life with God) as absence or travel — while we live in the present body, we are travelling, we are not at home regarding the resurrection in God. And this latter evokes a great desire in the faithful to be with God.
The very clothing in incorruption (in immediate proximity to the resurrection, yet following it) Paul describes here as life will eat, swallow, absorb what is mortal (like food or drink).
Here he speaks of the very same thing using a completely different image — through the earnest (that which is given in advance to establish trust, trusting relations, partially, incomplete) of the Spirit. On this topic one can also recall the sacrifice of the prophet Elijah, about which I wrote more at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Elias.html

(Luke VII, 1-10)
Luke VII, 1 — 'εἰς τὰς ἀκοὰς' — 'eis tas akoas' — into the audience / for the hearing. Indicates for what purpose Christ spoke. 'εἰς Καφαρναούμ' - 'eis Kapharnaoum' — into Capernaum. Where to.

Luke VII, 6 — 'ὑπὸ τὴν στέγην μου' — 'hypo ten stegen mou' — under my roof. Here the preposition 'hypo' takes the accusative case.

Luke VII, 8 — 'ὑπὸ ἐξουσίαν' — 'hypo exousian' — under authority. 'ὑπ’ ἐμαυτὸν' - 'hyp emauton' — under myself. Here the preposition 'hypo' takes the accusative case. I wrote earlier regarding the parallel passage in Matthew:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/21072024.html
The accusative with the preposition 'hypo' or its form before a vowel 'hyp' indicates subordination here, or more precisely in the context of the centurion's words — a certain functional alignment regardless of desires. To explain this latter assertion in more detail: the centurion is under authority — meaning he performs part of the functions of authority, in particular protection and control, while higher meanings of human existence, such as freedom, are not revealed here. Likewise regarding the centurion's soldiers — they carry out his orders, realizing to a certain extent his functions, while higher meanings of human existence are also not shown here. This is precisely the reason for the centurion's humility. Effectively, he implies that he recognizes the honor of receiving Jesus into his home, and recognizes that his life does not pass within the higher meanings of human existence. And Jesus in his eyes is the One Who came for their sake.

Luke VII, 9 — 'ἐν τῷ Ἰσραὴλ' — 'en to Israel' — in Israel. Where.

Luke VII, 10 — 'εἰς τὸν οἶκον' — 'eis ton oikon' — to the house / into the house. Where to.
