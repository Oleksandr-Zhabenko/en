(2 Corinthians XI, 1-6)
2 Corinthians XI, 3 — 'ἐν τῇ πανουργίᾳ' — 'en te panourgia' — in subtilty / by craftiness; in cunning; skilfully. All translation options are possible. 'ἀπὸ τῆς ἁπλότητος' - 'apo tes haplotetos' — from the simplicity. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that it concerns distancing oneself from Christ. 'εἰς Χριστόν' - 'eis CHriston' — into Christ / toward Christ; for Christ. Signifies that the mentioned simplicity leads unto Christ Himself, as well as that it is required for drawing closer to Christ.

2 Corinthians XI, 6 — 'ἐν παντὶ φανερώσαντες ἐν πᾶσιν εἰς ὑμᾶς' — 'en panti phanerosantes en pasin eis hymas' — thoroughly made manifest among you in all things / manifested everywhere in all things for you. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal here.

The Apostle Paul speaks figuratively of the faithful as the Bride of Christ, that is, the Church.
Yet whereas the Church remains with Christ forever, individual people can fall and depart, can even renounce Him and begin to act with hostility toward Christ. Betrothal precedes Marriage; it already requires faithfulness, yet serves as a kind of testing. Therefore, the life of the faithful in full unity with Christ (which is most characteristic of the age to come, of the Kingdom of God) may here be likened figuratively to the Marriage of Christ and the Church — in particular, we have the well-known image of the Kingdom of God as the Marriage Supper of Christ. Meanwhile, the path of the earthly Church in the present age can then be likened to the period of betrothal of the bride and groom.
He also warns against accepting another teaching, about some other Christ, whether about another person or claiming that Christ Himself was not like that. This remains pertinent in various eras, including today.
The Apostle states that the Gospel and the Christian faith, although they may be formulated in less than the finest words (in particular by specific individuals and groups), are in their depths priceless, true, right, saving, glorious, wonderful, joyful, and so on.
And the apostles, including Paul himself, partake in that fullness of life in Christ, in the Holy Spirit, in truth.

(Luke IX, 1-6)
Luke IX, 3 — 'εἰς τὴν ὁδόν' — 'eis ten hodon' — for your journey / into the way. The preposition 'eis' indicates here where one should not take additional material provisions (which also signifies here for what purpose — for travelling). These words of Christ properly orient the apostles for their work, yet they also hold meaning for observers — whoever might become interested in the apostles' activity for one purpose or another. For instance, the prohibition against taking material goods for the road is meant to reveal that the apostles do not act for enrichment (as other itinerant preachers did); the prohibition against greeting — so that people would not think the apostles were seeking casual acquaintances, needing them for their mission, or that the God Whom they serve is merely a god of luck and coincidence (fortune). That is, all these precepts are directed, among other things, toward dignified service, as well as ensuring that onlookers perceive the apostles' activity as called to serious and good interests, including societal ones. In this way more people will respond, and it also creates safer conditions for apostolic labour (to use modern phrasing).

Luke IX, 4 — 'εἰς ἣν ἂν οἰκίαν' — 'eis hen an oikian' — into whatsoever house. Where to.

Luke IX, 5 — 'ἀπὸ τῆς πόλεως ἐκείνης' — 'apo tes poleos ekeines' — out of that city / from that city. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that it concerns a complete cessation of relations with that city. The apostles (the faithful) are called to warn those people. 'ἀπὸ τῶν ποδῶν ὑμῶν' - 'apo ton podon hymon' — from your feet. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that shaking the dust off one's feet must be done completely, entirely, utterly; and also, if compared with the commentaries on Matthew X, 14:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15062026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/23062025.html
it likewise points out that this is from the perspective of the apostles themselves — hence, Christ is here on their side, together with them. 'εἰς μαρτύριον ἐπ’ αὐτούς' — 'eis martyrion ep autous' — for a testimony (on) against them. That is, by this the apostles testify that in the future these people will become even worse, as well as that they are separated from the Church and are effectively perishing spiritually.
