Liturgy:
(1 Thessalonians IV, 1-12)
1 Thessalonians IV, 1 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en kyrio Iesou' — in the Lord Jesus. In Whom. 'παρ’ ἡμῶν' - 'par hemon' — from beside us. The preposition 'para' in its form before a following 'he' takes the genitive case here; a similar construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, the Eternal Council of the Most Holy Trinity, God's entire design, and the human nature of Jesus Christ. See the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/Pryjmennyky.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/vystup-2025-hypo-genitive-Romans-XIII_1.pdf
Here it indicates that through the apostles the Holy Spirit acts, pointing also to how this accords with God's design.
If you wish to know more, please write to me.

1 Thessalonians IV, 3 — 'ἀπὸ τῆς πορνείας' — 'apo tes porneias' — from fornication / from sexual immorality. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that one must completely, entirely abstain from fornication.

1 Thessalonians IV, 4 — 'ἐν ἁγιασμῷ καὶ τιμῇ' — 'en hagiasmo kai time' — in sanctification and honour.

1 Thessalonians IV, 5 — 'ἐν πάθει ἐπιθυμίας' — 'en pathei epithymias' — in the passion of lust. The turn indicates that passion and lust are closely interconnected. One may think that it concerns dependency on lust, when a person is possessed by lust and does not act freely, but by virtue of a prevailing and uncontrollable urge.

1 Thessalonians IV, 6 — 'ἐν τῷ πράγματι' — 'en to pragmati' — in any matter; by the matter. Both translation options are possible: the first indicates that wrong may be in a matter, whilst the second points out that the matter itself may be a wrong.

1 Thessalonians IV, 7 — 'ἐν ἁγιασμῷ' — 'en hagiasmo' — unto holiness / in sanctification. That is, the calling is directed toward sanctification, whilst sanctification also fosters the calling.

1 Thessalonians IV, 8 — 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' — 'eis hymas' — into us; unto us. Both translation options are possible; the first indicates that the manifestation of the Holy Spirit was directed at the faithful, the faithful being the place of action and manifestation of the Holy Spirit, whereas the second shows that the Holy Spirit is given to the faithful themselves.

1 Thessalonians IV, 9 — 'εἰς τὸ ἀγαπᾶν ἀλλήλους' — 'eis to agapan allelous' — to love one another. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action here.

1 Thessalonians IV, 10 — 'εἰς πάντας τοὺς ἀδελφοὺς ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ Μακεδονίᾳ' — 'eis pantas tous adelphous en hole te Makedonia' — toward all the brothers which are in all Macedonia.

The Apostle Paul teaches to guard that which was handed down from the apostles.
He also specifically warns against fornication (vessels being a figurative name for our bodies), against violence and wrong, calling unto love and diligent labour.
The Lord is the avenger — that is, the Lord is the Judge. And whoever rejects God's will, God's commandments and precepts, rejects not human teaching, but God's will, which will carry great weight at God's judgment.
