Matins:
(Luke IX, 28-36)
Luke IX, 28 — 'εἰς τὸ ὄρος' — 'eis to oros' — into the mountain; onto the mountain. Grammatically, the second translation is correct.

Luke IX, 29 — 'ἐν τῷ προσεύχεσθαι αὐτὸν' — 'en to proseukhesthai auton' — in His exalted praying; in His spiritualized prayer; as He prayed exaltedly. When.

Luke IX, 31 — 'οἳ ὀφθέντες ἐν δόξῃ' — 'hoi ophthentes en doxe' — who were seen in glory. 'πληροῦν ἐν Ἱερουσαλήμ' - 'pleroun en Hierousalem' — to accomplish (to bring to fullness, make full) in Jerusalem. Where. It is about the voluntary Sufferings, Death, Resurrection, Ascension, and Sending of the Holy Spirit. One can think that also about the Second Glorious Coming, since Christ is to descend also to Jerusalem. This in particular testifies to the fact that Moses and Elijah knew about Christ, and now they see Him, handed down by writing, words, prototypes, examples also to the faithful, of which they spoke with Jesus now. More is known about Moses on this matter, but also regarding Elijah much can be seen and said. See in particular in English:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Elias.html

Luke IX, 33 — 'ἐν τῷ διαχωρίζεσθαι αὐτοὺς ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'en to diakhorizesthai autous ap autou' — when they departed from Him. That is, Peter's words were also an attempt to stop Moses and Elijah so that they would stay a while longer with them. In general, the idea of three tabernacles is strange, probably a purely human thought of Peter in excitement, in which one does not need to look for theological depth. Moses and Elijah were clothed, were in glory, so from the point of view of completeness of the picture they did not in any way need tabernacles from the apostles. But here one can clearly trace Peter's intuition regarding the desire and need to co-work with God.

Luke IX, 34 — 'ἐν τῷ εἰσελθεῖν αὐτοὺς εἰς τὴν νεφέλην' — 'en to eiselthein autous eis ten nephelen' — as they entered into the cloud. When.

Luke IX, 35 — 'ἐκ τῆς νεφέλης' — 'ek tes nepheles' — out of the cloud. From where. The cloud here is as the place of manifestation of the Father.

Luke IX, 36 — 'ἐν τῷ γενέσθαι τὴν φωνὴν' — 'en to genesthai ten phonen' — when the voice occurred. When. 'ἐν ἐκείναις ταῖς ἡμέραις' - 'en ekeinais tais hemerais' — in those days; during those days. When.

Liturgy:
(2 Peter I, 10-19)
2 Peter I, 11 — 'εἰς τὴν αἰώνιον βασιλείαν τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν καὶ σωτῆρος Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ' — 'eis ten aionion basileian tou kyriou hemon kai soteros Iesou CHristou' — into the eternal Kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Where to.

2 Peter I, 12 — 'ἐν τῇ παρούσῃ ἀληθείᾳ' — 'en te parouse alatheia' — in the present truth; in the near truth. The words are polysemous, indicating in particular that the Second Coming is to be soon. It is necessary to remember that regardless of the duration of time until the Second Coming (about which I wrote in other places, in particular see the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/09022023.html
https://churchandsociety.org.ua/pdf/projects/zbirnyk.pdf
), the main and decisive events before the Second Coming have already occurred (the First Coming of Jesus Christ), God knows when the Second Coming will be (see the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/12042026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/20042025.html
) and from the perspective of eternity any finite duration (and such it is) is short, therefore, as noted, time can be perceived as short.

2 Peter I, 13 — 'ἐν τούτῳ τῷ σκηνώματι' — 'en touto to skenomati' — in this tabernacle; in this dwelling. That is, while in the body (while living the present life). See more about this at the links:
https://www.facebook.com/Oleksandr.S.Zhabenko/posts/
https://www.instagram.com/p/C8AN3CfikTS/
'ἐν ὑπομνήσει' — 'en hypomnesei' — in reminding; by reminding. Rather the second option, as it is simpler to understand. The apostle very humbly sees his mission in particular in stirring up by reminding. One can see here also a memory of the cock crowing, when Peter denied Christ (according to the testimony of many, Peter remembered and repented of those actions of his all his life). Peter sees his weakness, implicitly comparing himself and his role in these words to a cock. Comparison with nature, in particular with animals, was typical of the Semitic culture of that time (we see many other examples in the New Testament, in particular comparing the faithful to sheep, Herod to a cunning fox, people who after cleansing return to old impurities to swine, unremarkable faithful to small birds, and others). Of course, even in such a case the comparison does not exhaust the whole significance of the chief apostle, but here his humility is visible.

2 Peter I, 17 — 'παρὰ θεοῦ πατρὸς' — 'para theou patros' — from beside God the Father; from God the Father. The preposition 'para' here is with the genitive case. Such a construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, regarding the Most Holy Trinity, regarding the whole of God's plan and the human nature of Jesus Christ. See more at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/Pryjmennyky.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/02082025.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/28052026.html
Here this indicates the human deified nature of Jesus Christ, filled with all the gifts of the Holy Spirit, also that in this was God's plan. 'τιμὴν καὶ δόξαν φωνῆς ἐνεχθείσης αὐτῷ τοιᾶσδε ὑπὸ τῆς μεγαλοπρεποῦς δόξης' — 'timen kai doxan phones enekhtheises auto toiasde hypo tes megaloprepous doxes' — honour and glory of a voice brought to Him such as this by the majestic glory. The preposition 'hypo' here is with the genitive case and indicates the passive voice of the participle was brought. 'εἰς ὃν ἐγὼ εὐδόκησα' — 'eis hon ego eudokesa' — into Whom is My delight. The preposition 'eis' here emphasizes the fulfillment of the prophecies and promises of God in Christ.

2 Peter I, 18 — 'ἐξ οὐρανοῦ' — 'ex ouranou' — from heaven. The preposition 'ex' (form of the preposition 'ek' before a following vowel) answers the question From where?. 'ἐν τῷ ὄρει τῷ ἁγίῳ' - 'en to orei to hagio' — on the holy mountain. Where.

2 Peter I, 19 — 'προσεχοντες' — 'prosekhontes' — taking heed; devoting oneself; having additionally or besides. 'εως ου ημερα' - 'heos hou hemera' — until this day — here is meant the Day of the Second Coming of Christ, that is, until Christ comes a second time. Prophecies are valid up to the Second Coming — both those that have been fulfilled and those that are to be fulfilled. 'ἐν αὐχμηρῷ τόπῳ' - 'en aukhmero topo' — in a dark place. Where. 'ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν' - 'en tais kardiais hymon' — in your hearts. The preposition 'en' indicates the place of action.

Entrance into the Kingdom of God — here is also meant the ability to consciously live according to God's will and design already now, even before the onset of the Kingdom of God, but also the Kingdom of God itself after the Second Coming of Jesus Christ.

My present tabernacle (in the original, a word that is cognate with the word tabernacle, tent, and also reminds both of the Old Testament tabernacle and of the Transfiguration, which is discussed further) — Peter's body until his death.

Christ revealed to Peter that he would soon depart to the Lord, which Peter mentions here.

Peter saw as one of the tasks of his ministry to strengthen others in faith and virtues (compare, for example: Luke XXII, 32; John XXI, 15-25), and does so even after bodily death.

Then he refers to the Transfiguration of the Lord, of which he himself was a direct witness and participant. Many pagan myths and legends contain artistic fabrications, the imagination of authors, often quite inventive, while the apostles were witnesses of Christ.

And here Peter testifies to the Transfiguration, the feast of which is today.

Regarding the completion of the reading, it was said above, the richness of meanings in a complex metaphor with several components is very great, so it is difficult to give a fine translation. Prophetic words both need to be had additionally, and one can entrust oneself to God, believing in them, and to them also it is worth taking heed, paying attention. Great is the significance of the Old Testament prophecies for strengthening in faith.

(Matthew XVII, 1-9)
Matthew XVII, 1 — 'εἰς ὄρος ὑψηλὸν' — 'eis oros hypselon' — onto a high mountain. Where to.

Matthew XVII, 5 — 'ἐκ τῆς νεφέλης' — 'ek tes nepheles' — out of the cloud. From where. 'ἐν ᾧ εὐδόκησα' - 'en ho eudokesa' — in Whom is My delight.

Matthew XVII, 9 — 'ἐκ τοῦ ὄρους' — 'ek tou orous' — from the mountain. The preposition 'ek' indicates the descent from the mountain, that is, the mountain as the starting point of movement. The pair 'eis' - 'ek' in verses 1 and 9 indicates movement. 'ἐκ νεκρῶν' - 'ek nekron' — from the dead. Here the preposition 'ek' also indicates from where the exit takes place — from the dead to life.

The reading about the Transfiguration of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ.

As understood, this manifestation was to strengthen faith in the apostles when they would see Christ suffering on the cross, at the time of His Sufferings, Death, and Resurrection. The prohibition to tell could precisely be caused by the fact that the disciples still did not understand the future events with Jesus, although He had already spoken to them about them, so, in order not to spread an incorrect vision of the event, Jesus forbade them to tell then.

Also here we see the manifestation of the Trinity — the Son was transfigured, the Father testified by voice, and the Holy Spirit appeared in the form of a light-bearing cloud. There is a parallel with the manifestation of the Trinity at the time of the Baptism of Jesus Christ in the Jordan.

In the description of the event we see brightly the manifestations of the human spirit (about which I wrote earlier). Most brightly the human spirit manifests itself in three spheres — striving for God (thirst and hunger for righteousness — one of the variants to speak of it in other words), fear of God, and conscience (directly connected with the ability to realize good and evil) (see about this in particular in Ukrainian at the link:
https://parafia.org.ua/biblioteka/hrystyyanstvo-virovchennya-ta-tradytsiji/zakon-bozhyj
).

Here we just see high degrees of manifestation of all three at the same time — It is good for us to be here… and the desire to remain — this is striving for God; great fear in the disciples — the appearance of the fear of God (about which more is written at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/DialogueOnWordsChristFear.html
https://www.facebook.com/Oleksandr.S.Zhabenko/posts/
); while It is good for us to be here… is also a manifestation of conscience testifying that it is good — to see the light of the Transfiguration, the light of God.

Thus, the Lord indeed revealed to the disciples His glory to the extent that they could contain it — in full measure for the spirit, vision with bodily eyes, and all together — significance for souls.

Also regarding what the Taboric light was like in the 13th century there were discussions, and the doctrine of this light was formulated by Saint Gregory Palamas. Without going into details, I will say that the Taboric light is Divine energy, the action of God outside His own nature, action in creation, grace that is uncreated, eternal. One can say that it is God Himself (though not in Himself, but in creation) revealing and opening Himself.
