(Matthew XIX, 3-12)
Matthew XIX, 4, 8 — 'ἀπ’ ἀρχῆς' — 'ap arkhes' — from the beginning. Set expression. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates the starting point of time here.

Matthew XIX, 5 — 'ἔσονται οἱ δύο εἰς σάρκα μίαν' — 'esontai hoi duo eis sarka mian' — the two shall become one flesh. The preposition 'eis' indicates here what those people will become — a single whole. These important words are a Mystery of God.

Matthew XIX, 12 — 'ἐκ κοιλίας μητρὸς ἐγεννήθησαν οὕτως' — 'ek koilias metros egennethesan houtos' — were born so from their mother's womb. The preposition 'ek' indicates here that they were always such, even before birth. See more at the links:

'εὐνουχίσθησαν ὑπὸ τῶν ἀνθρώπων' — 'eunoukhisthesan hypo ton anthropon' — were made eunuchs by men. The preposition 'hypo' here is used with the genitive case and indicates the passive voice of the participle made eunuchs. The word to receive (to make room for, particularly as for a traveler, to move forward, to accept) is polysemous; its use here indicates that a person to a certain extent has the possibility (perhaps not everyone) to accept either another person (by marrying them) or celibacy in the Lord. Such acceptance must give space, move forward, be accepted out of love. In general, the word is very polysemous and to a certain extent characterizes the concept of value. Reflecting on the richness of its meanings, I wrote a poem-wish (using it not in the sense of this specific verse, but taking into account and trying to convey the depth of vision of the word's content, for I wish the young woman to whom it is dedicated either to be married once in the Lord or to preserve purity in Him), available at the link:
https://www.instagram.com/oleksandr_zhabenko/p/C5p7O1WMUqx/
I will dwell on the word receives and its forms and derivatives, which are used several times in this passage. In the original Greek, the word literally means to make room, that is, to give a place, to accept. If we recall how the heart is spoken of in the context of faithfulness, also as a certain space, and also that there is virginity for the sake of the Kingdom of God, then it becomes clear that here Christ speaks in such a form about the degree of a person's dedication to the Kingdom of God. If compared with human relationships, it is natural to desire to be closer to the beloved person. So it is here — if a person loves God, they want to draw closer to His Kingdom, and therefore to give preference and importance to what is connected with it — particularly since virginity is more similar to the state of the age to come, such a person desires to remain unmarried in purity more than to marry.
