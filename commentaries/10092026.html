Liturgy:
(Galatians III, 23-IV, 5)
Galatians III, 23, IV, 4, 5 — 'ὑπὸ νόμον' — 'hypo nomon' — under the Law. Here 'hypo' is with the accusative case, indicating that the Law is as it were raised above a human being, as if placed higher than them. In the Gospel Christ says that the sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath, and that He is Lord of the sabbath (particularly the One Who is awaited on the sabbath, on the day of rest). 'εἰς τὴν μέλλουσαν πίστιν' - 'eis ten mellousan pistin' — unto the faith which should afterwards be revealed; for the faith that was to come. Here it indicates the goal.

Galatians III, 24 — 'εἰς Χριστόν' — 'eis CHriston' — unto Christ; for Christ. Both translation options are possible. The first indicates that the Law teaches until human being becomes conformed to Christ (Christ in human being is, therefore, the final point of movement and the goal), whilst the second — that the Law has true meaning as a preparation for Christ. Galatians III, 25 — 'ὑπὸ παιδαγωγόν' — 'hypo paidagogon' — under a tutor. Here 'hypo' is with the accusative case and indicates that the Law shows certain boundaries, frames.

Galatians III, 26 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ' — 'en CHristo' — in Christ; by Christ. Various translation options are possible here.

Galatians III, 27 — 'εἰς Χριστόν' — 'eis CHriston' — into Christ. Indicates the direction of change and conformity. The ending is better translated have put on Christ.

Galatians III, 28 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ' — 'en CHristo' — in Christ. Here I will add my comment to a Facebook publication:
Nothing of this world will enter into the Kingdom of Heaven in the state in which it is now. Including sex and national identity. But this does not mean that everything that exists now has no meaning. For to live only by the future means for some reason rejecting the past and the present. But is not the Lord the Creator of all ages and times?

End of quote.

Galatians III, 29 — through Christ the faithful have become children of Abraham, and in fact inherited God's promises given to Abraham himself. That is, Christ, figuratively speaking, is the Key to all possible doors of God's promises, He opens and leads to the fulfillment of all God's promises (compare John X). These words were important for the Galatians, whose conscience was under the influence of Christians who wanted the Galatians to keep the whole ceremonial Law of Moses, effectively becoming Jews first, and only then Christians. Those people spoke more… about Abraham than about Christ. It is precisely against such a misunderstanding of the meaning of God's Providence and promises among those people, and hence of faith in general, that Paul contends.

Galatians IV, 2 — 'ὑπὸ ἐπιτρόπους' — 'hypo epitropous' — under tutors / guardians. Here 'hypo' is with the accusative case and indicates (similarly to Galatians III, 25) the frames of the Law.

Galatians IV, 3 — 'ὑπὸ τὰ στοιχεῖα' — 'hypo ta stoikheia' — under the elements / rudiments. Here 'hypo' is with the accusative case and indicates a certain power of the elements over people prior to Christ.

Paul testifies that God gave Abraham the blessing through a promise that was fulfilled in Jesus Christ, not through the Law.

(Mark VI, 30-45)
Mark VI, 31, 32 — 'εἰς ἔρημον τόπον' — 'eis eremon topon' — into a desert place; into a solitary place. Where to.

Mark VI, 33 — 'ἀπὸ πασῶν τῶν πόλεων' — 'apo pason ton poleon' — out of all cities. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that the people left those cities.

Mark VI, 36 — 'εἰς τοὺς κύκλῳ ἀγροὺς καὶ κώμας' — 'eis tous kyklo agrous kai komas' — into the country round about, and into the villages. Where to.

Mark VI, 41 — 'εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν' — 'eis ton ouranon' — up to the heaven. Where to.

Mark VI, 43 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν ἰχθύων' — 'apo ton ikhthyon' — of the fishes. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that those remnants were no longer properly fish (did not have a marketable appearance, in modern terms).

Mark VI, 45 — 'εἰς τὸ πλοῖον' — 'eis to ploion' — into the ship (the same one as before). Where to. 'εἰς τὸ πέραν πρὸς Βηθσαϊδά' - 'eis to peran pros Bethsaida' — unto the other side before unto Bethsaida. Where to.

The reading is dedicated to the rest of the apostles according to Jesus' words (a unique passage in Mark) and the miracle of feeding the 5,000 men (not counting women and children, which is not stated directly).
