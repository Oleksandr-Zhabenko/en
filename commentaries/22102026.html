Liturgy:
(Colossians I, 24-29)
Colossians I, 24 — 'ἐν τοῖς παθήμασιν ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν' - 'en tois pathemasin hyper hymon' - in the sufferings for you. The full verse in translation will be approximately as I rejoice in my sufferings for you (it is not clear whether it is rejoice for you or sufferings for you; in the first case the faithful are in fact a consolation in the Apostle's sufferings, in the second they are an incentive to endurance and patience) and fill up (supplement) in place of someone else the lack (poverty) of persecutions of Christ in my flesh for the sake of His Body, that is, the Church. Here Paul does NOT say that he supplements Christ's Sufferings, but says that he suffers as a supplement to the persecutions that he undergoes for Christ for the sake of the Church and its building up, for the sake of ministry. That is, a supplement to persecutions, and not to the Sufferings of Christ. The latter do not need any supplements. 'ἐν τῇ σαρκί μου ὑπὲρ τοῦ σώματος αὐτοῦ' - 'en te sarki mou hyper tou somatos autou' - in my flesh for the sake of His Body. See above.

Colossians I, 25 — 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' - 'eis hymas' - for you. The preposition 'eis' indicates here the purpose.

Colossians I, 26 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν αἰώνων καὶ ἀπὸ τῶν γενεῶ' - 'apo ton aionon kai apo ton geneo' - from the ages and from the generations. The preposition 'apo' in both cases here indicates the beginning of the count of a certain range (what is meant is from … and right up until recently).

Colossians I, 27 — 'οἷς ἠθέλησεν ὁ θεὸς γνωρίσαι τί τὸ πλοῦτος τῆς δόξης τοῦ μυστηρίου τούτου ἐν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν' - 'hois ethelesen ho theos gnorisai ti to ploutos tes doxes tou mysteriou toutou en tois ethnesin' - to whom God desired to give to know what the riches of the glory of this mystery are among the nations. Where. God gave the former pagans, the nations, to know Christ and His glory. 'Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν' - 'CHristos en hymin' - Christ in you. In whom.

Colossians I, 28 — 'ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ' - 'en pase sophia' - in all wisdom; by all wisdom. That is, following God's Wisdom. 'ἵνα παραστήσωμεν πάντα ἄνθρωπον τέλειον ἐν Χριστῷ' - 'hina parastesomen panta anthropon teleion en CHristo' - to set beside every person perfect, completed, fully mature in Christ. To set beside Christ, near Him, as a brother or sister by grace. Perfection needs to be written about in detail and at length here. If you wish, you can write to me in a private message, and I will give considerably more information. I wrote briefly about similar words yesterday at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/21102026.html

Colossians I, 29 — 'εἰς ὃ' - 'eis ho' - for which. It indicates here the purpose. 'κατὰ τὴν ἐνέργειαν αὐτοῦ τὴν ἐνεργουμένην ἐν ἐμοὶ ἐν δυνάμει' - 'kata ten enegreian autou ten enegrumenen en emoi en dynamei' - according to His action, that which works in me in power. Here the Apostle speaks about Christ's grace and power, about his cooperation with it, and thus with Christ, with God, and about the fact that God's power far exceeds human powers, so that Christ acts more than the Apostle (humble and truthful words).

(Luke IX, 7-11)
Luke IX, 7, 8 — 'ὑπό τινων ' — 'hypo tinon' - by some. The preposition 'hypo' here with the genitive indicates the passive voice of were said and by whom. 'ἐκ νεκρῶν' - 'ek nekron' - from the dead. A set expression; the preposition indicates the place from which the transition took place — from the dead to the living.

Luke IX, 10 — 'εἰς πόλιν' — 'eis polin' - into a city. Where to.

Luke IX, 11 — ‘τους χρειαν εχοντας θεραπειας ιατο’ — ‘tous khreian ekhontas therapeias iato’ — those who had need, caring for (treating, healing) He healed. Here, judging by everything, we have the so-called emphatic syntagm, that is, the use, characteristic of Old Testament texts, of two words in a row with the same root or close in meaning for the maximum or cardinal strengthening of the meaning. But it may also be an emphasis on the fact that Christ did not merely heal people in need while preaching, but also showed His care, attention, concern, mercy, love.
