Luke XI, 35 — 'τὸ φῶς τὸ ἐν σοὶ' — 'to phos to en soi' — the light which is in you. Where. That is, the inner light: it is the capacity to perceive, see, and understand the spiritual, one might say, spiritual perception, vision, mind, as well as that which activates it; one can add that it motivates and prompts one to listen to it, be guided by it, pay heed to it, etc. Quite different approaches to understanding what is said are possible here, including two others that follow.
See regarding Matthew VI, 22-23 at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/Matthew6.html
In view of the above, one can say that by the eye is meant the goal for the sake of which a person does something, and by vision — the process of motivation. Then what is said also carries the sense that if the goal is correct, if the motivation is correct, for God's sake, then what you do will also be directed correctly — toward God and will be right, even righteous. If, however, the goal and motivation are wrong, then everything directed will be wrong. Furthermore, it is easier to direct goal and motivation than the whole activity, which depends on many factors. For it is easier to steer the helm than the whole ship.
End quotation.
Generally, it is common to understand the mind itself as the eye.
If the mind is pure — which is possible when the heart is purified by the action of God's grace — then the person will see everything in God's presence, in God, which is called light here. Even a vision of the light of grace, the Tabor light, the light of Transfiguration is possible, though not necessarily in that manner. But if the mind is unpurified, or worse — darkened by passions, sins, errors, negligence, unrepentance, and the like — then the person does not see clearly and distinctly God's presence in life, in the world, remaining in that state; and this is figuratively called spiritual darkness, which also allows evil spirits to approach the person more easily. Then everything bad can become part of their life.

Luke XI, 37 — 'Ἐν δὲ τῷ λαλῆσαι' — 'en de to lalesai' — and as He spoke / while He was speaking. When. 'ἀριστήσῃ παρ’ αὐτῷ' - 'aristese par auto' — to dine with Him / to dine in the vicinity of Him. Where, at whose place. The preposition 'para' in its form before a following vowel takes the dative case here and indicates where Jesus was to dine if He accepted the invitation — beside the host, at the place of honour. At the same time, this is an expression of honour, but also of the Pharisee's own self-importance.
