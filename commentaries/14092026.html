Liturgy:
Of the Cross of the Lord:
(1 Corinthians I, 18-24)
1 Corinthians I, 21 — 'ἐν τῇ σοφίᾳ τοῦ θεοῦ' — 'en te sophia tou theou' — in the wisdom of God. In what; and it can be added — Which God people knew not — the All-Wise.

The apostle here and in other places of the Epistle uses 'μωρία' — 'moria' — foolishness, madness, absurdity. See a more detailed explanation of this word and its nuances at the link:
https://www.facebook.com/Oleksandr.S.Zhabenko/posts/
Very eloquent and expressive words, in my opinion. Additionally, I will say that to praise — means to acknowledge the dignity, worth, and pre-eminence of something or someone, as well as to recommend it to others as good. That is, the words that the faithful praise with the cross of Jesus Christ do not mean that they are proud of it or consider themselves better than others, but that they see in it immense meaning, value, a source of power and wisdom, a manifestation of great love, an instrument of victory over sins and death, a banner of victory, and other things that are difficult to understand for those who do not accept the truth about Jesus.

(John XIX, 6-11, 13-20, 25-28, 30-35)
John XIX, 6 — 'ἐν αὐτῷ' — 'en auto' — in Him. In Whom.

John XIX, 9 — 'εἰς τὸ πραιτώριον' — 'eis to praitorion' — into the judgment hall / praetorium. Where to.

John XIX, 13 — 'εἰς τόπον' — 'eis topon' — into a place / unto a place. Where to.

John XIX, 17 — 'εἰς τὸν λεγόμενον' — 'eis ton legomenon' — into a [place] called. Where to.

John XIX, 20 — 'Ἑβραϊστί, Ῥωμαϊστί, Ἑλληνιστί' — 'Hebraisti, Rhomaisti, Hellenisti' — in Hebrew, and Greek, and Latin. I draw attention to the order of the languages. It conveys an implicit thought — the Jews and the Romans, in the persons of the enemies of Christ and Pontius Pilate, reached an agreement, but not one that ought to have been on the basis of a shared Greek culture, on the basis of seeing Jesus as a prominent philosopher and teacher; rather, it is as if a certain mockery of this possibility, which Pilate neglected. And the Lord, by His sacrificial love and victory, transformed the instrument of mockery into a sign of the unification of nations and all humanity, of which only three nations are named.

John XIX, 27 — 'ἀπ’ ἐκείνης τῆς ὥρας' — 'ap ekeines tes horas' — from that hour. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel indicates the starting point of reckoning here. 'εἰς τὰ ἴδια' - 'eis ta idia' — unto his own home. Where to. Set expression with the meaning of close personal relationships.

John XIX, 31 — 'ἐν τῷ σαββάτῳ' — 'en to sabbato' — on the sabbath day. When.
