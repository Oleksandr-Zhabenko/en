(Mark VI, 14-30)
Mark VI, 14 — 'ἐγήγερται ἐκ νεκρῶν' — 'egegertai ek nekron' — is risen from the dead. The preposition 'ek' indicates here from where the transition occurred, in Herod's opinion — from the dead to the living. 'ἐν αὐτῷ' - 'en auto' — in Him; by Him. Both translation options are possible. The second means that Herod thought Jesus was an instrument of miracles, that is, He does not perform them Himself, but they happen, using His (in the opinion of Herod himself, as the risen John the Baptist) resurrection. Naturally, an erroneous opinion showing that Herod was not actually interested in Jesus or was satisfied with his own ideas as such.

Mark VI, 17 — 'ἐν φυλακῇ' — 'en phylake' — in prison. Where.

Mark VI, 28 — 'ἐν τῇ φυλακῇ' — 'en te phylake' — in the [same] prison. Where.

Mark VI, 29 — 'ἐν μνημείῳ' — 'en mnemeio' — in a tomb. Where.

The narrative of the martyrdom of Saint John the Baptist. Mark, as is sometimes the case, gives the most details among all the evangelists.

I will add that Jesus was considered to be Elijah, because before the Messiah, according to Old Testament prophecies, Elijah was to come (who was precisely John the Baptist in spirit and power as the Forerunner of Christ's First Coming, and Elijah himself personally as the predecessor of Christ's Second Coming, at least regarding the latter such a tradition is widespread). Similarly, there was a tradition that the prophet Jeremiah hid the ark of the covenant, and before the Coming of Christ he would also appear to point out where the ark was located. Thus, regarding two prophets such thoughts were widespread, which is why regarding others simple people thought that something similar could also happen with them.
The Evangelist Mark emphasizes that Herod even at times acted according to John's words, although he kept him in prison, and ultimately executed him. An example of what can happen to a person who does not try to live according to the voice of their conscience or the demands of morality.
Later, when this same Herod judged Jesus Christ, Jesus, unlike during the trial before Pontius Pilate, answered Herod nothing. It is considered that this was because Herod would not have taken anything good for himself from those answers, being so spiritually fallen.
There is a view that the Lord permitted John to suffer before the Resurrection of Jesus so that he would also preach in Hades about the imminent deliverance of the Old Testament righteous, so powerful were John's words. The Lord Jesus Christ, having risen, also delivered John from Hades with Himself into Paradise.
