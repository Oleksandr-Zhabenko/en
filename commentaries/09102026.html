Liturgy:
(Philippians I, 27-II, 4)
Philippians I, 27 — 'ἐν ἑνὶ πνεύματι' — 'en heni pneumati' — in one Spirit. Points to the Holy Spirit, Who both unites and grants strength.

Philippians I, 28 — 'ἐν μηδενὶ' — 'en medeni' — in nothing. 'ὑπὸ τῶν ἀντικειμένων' - 'hypo ton antikeimenon' — by your adversaries / through those who oppose. The preposition 'hypo' with the genitive case underlines that possible fear might have been caused not by Paul, but by those who oppose, yet Paul precisely does not fear them, which points to freedom in Christ. 'ἔνδειξις ἀπωλείας' - 'endeiksis apoleias' — an evident token of perdition / proof of ruin, destruction, loss. That is, Paul implies that those who withstand the Gospel are moving towards destruction and ruin, and this will become manifest to all, evident, serving as a witness to all. Whereas for those who contend for the faith of the Gospel, this standing serves unto salvation. 'ἀπὸ θεοῦ' - 'apo theou' — from God. The preposition 'apo' here has two distinct meanings regarding the faithful and the unfaithful: that for those who fall away this is proof of their departure from God, whilst for the faithful salvation is a gift of God.

Philippians I, 29 — 'εἰς αὐτὸν πιστεύειν' — 'eis auton pisteuein' — to believe in(to) Him. The preposition 'eis' emphasizes that faith is a movement towards Christ, that He is the Goal and Final Destination of the movement of faith. It touches upon the profound theme of suffering for Christ's sake. I wrote briefly earlier, here I give the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/renewal-of-Peter.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15072023.html

Philippians I, 30 — 'ἐν ἐμοὶ' — 'en emoi' — in me. That is, it refers to meanings, feelings, values, and the like, which the faithful find in Paul's life and in himself.

Philippians II, 1 — 'παράκλησις' — 'paraklesis' — consolation, comfort. 'ἐν Χριστῷ' - 'en CHristo' — in Christ. In Whom. 'παραμύθιον' - 'paramythion' — comfort, solace, consolation. 'κοινωνία' - 'koinonia' — fellowship, communion, participation, joint participation. 'σπλάγχνα καὶ οἰκτιρμοί' - 'splankhna kai oiktirmoi' — bowels and mercies / compassion, sympathy, and pity, empathy. The Apostle lists deeply personal feelings that show that in Christ people have become close to God, significantly closer than they might have thought.

Philippians II, 2 — 'τὸ αὐτὸ φρονῆτε τὴν αὐτὴν ἀγάπην ἔχοντες σύνψυχοι τὸ ἓν φρονοῦντες' — 'to auto phronete ten auten agapen ekhontes sympsykhoi to hen phronountes' — be likeminded, having the same love, being of one accord [unity of souls unto communion in something important], of one mind.

Philippians II, 3 — 'ἐριθείαν' — 'eritheian' — strife, rivalry, ambition.

A very rich and dense description of Christian life, way of thinking, worldview, and conduct — one of the most graphic in all of Scripture. I shall not dwell in detail on each verse, as each is worthy of great attention in particular, as well as all together. I would add that the continuation of this reading is that which is frequently read in honour of the Mother of God, Who is a wonderful exemplar of virtues, including all those listed here.
