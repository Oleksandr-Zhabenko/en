Liturgy:
(Colossians II, 13-20)
Colossians II, 14 — 'ἐκ τοῦ μέσου' — 'ek tou mesou' — out of the midst; out from within. Set expression. From where. Emphasizes here that Christ conquered sins in human nature, as the One Who possesses this midst, that is, the state of existence of human nature. Nailing it to His cross indicates here that the crucifixion manifested this state also with the greatest power.

Colossians II, 15 — 'ἐν παρρησίᾳ' — 'en parresia' — openly; with boldness; in full confidence. Polysemous words. 'ἐν αὐτῷ' - 'en auto' — in it; in Himself. In some translations omitted.

Colossians II, 16 — 'ἐν βρώσει' — 'en brosei' — in eating. 'ἐν πόσει' - 'en posei' — in drink / in drinking. 'ἐν μέρει ἑορτῆς ἢ νουμηνίας ἢ σαββάτων' - 'en merei heortes e noumenias e sabbaton' — in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath day. Part of a set expression meaning in respect of.

Colossians II, 18 — 'ἐν ταπεινοφροσύνῃ' — 'en tapeinophrosyne' — in a humility of the mind / by humility of the mind. Both translation options are possible; the first indicates that (imaginary) humility is constantly manifested, whilst the second points to it as the driving force of such actions. 'ὑπὸ τοῦ νοὸς τῆς σαρκὸς' - 'hypo tou noos tes sarkos' — by his fleshly mind. The preposition 'hypo' here takes the genitive case and indicates the middle-passive voice of the participle puffed up, that is, either the fleshly mind puffs up, or such a person is so strongly captivated by it that they introduce themselves into that state of puffing up.

Colossians II, 19 — 'ἐξ οὗ' — 'ex hou' — from Which; from Whom. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following 'hou' indicates the Source of growth.

Colossians II, 20 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν στοιχείων' — 'apo ton stoikheion' — from the rudiments / elements. The preposition 'apo' emphasizes liberation from the elements of the world through dying with Christ. 'ἐν κόσμῳ' - 'en kosmo' — in the world. Points to living in the world.

The Apostle Paul exposes a heresy that had begun to spread among the Colossians. Nevertheless, he does so in general terms, which is why it remains unclear what specifically that heresy consisted in directly.
Paul says that the heresy spread an excessive belief in the powers of angels, taught a manner of worshipping them, thereby exalting creation above the Creator, above Christ. Therefore Paul here shows that Christ is Superior to the angels. Earlier he testified that Christ is the Son of God, the image of the Father, the Head of the Church. It is Christ Who stands at the center of Revelation, and consequently of the apostles' preaching, and of the life of the faithful.
Paul also points out one of the main reasons why that heresy arose, as well as why many others arise — through being puffed up by the mind, simply put, through pride. Many scholars consider that heresy to have been similar to later Gnosticism, perhaps one of its earliest known (recorded) varieties.
Specific verses of the reading remain not entirely clear, to a certain extent enigmatic as to what specifically is meant.
Principalities and powers — angelic ranks, individual representatives of which became demons. It was these demons who were put to open shame by the sufferings on the cross of the Crucified Son of God, Who humbled Himself in contrast to those demons.
Paul also speaks again of the ceremonial Law of Moses, as well as of Gentiles' beliefs, for Gentiles also celebrated new moons.
Therefore Paul turns again and again to Christ — in Whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom.

(Luke IX, 18-22)
Luke IX, 18 — 'ἐν τῷ εἶναι' — 'en to einai' — as he was; whilst. Set expression with the meaning of the second translation option.

Luke IX, 22 — 'ἀπὸ τῶν πρεσβυτέρων καὶ ἀρχιερέων καὶ γραμματέων' — 'apo ton presbyteron kai arkhiereon kai grammateon' — of the elders and chief priests and scribes. The preposition 'apo' here emphasizes that the elders will reject Him completely, entirely, utterly.

The confession of the apostles of Jesus Christ as the Son of God, expressed by the Apostle Peter. Luke presents it more briefly than Matthew.
