Liturgy:
(1 Corinthians XVI, 13-24)
1 Corinthians XVI, 13 — 'ἐν τῇ πίστει' — 'en te pistei' — in the faith; by the faith. Both translation options are possible, although they differ, yet they complement each other.

1 Corinthians XVI, 14 — 'ἐν ἀγάπῃ' — 'en agape' — in love; by love. Both translation options are possible, although they differ, yet they complement each other.

1 Corinthians XVI, 15 — 'εἰς διακονίαν' — 'eis diakonian' — to the ministry; for ministry. The preposition 'eis' indicates the purpose of the action here.

1 Corinthians XVI, 19 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord.

1 Corinthians XVI, 20 — 'ἐν φιλήματι ἁγίῳ' — 'en philemati hagio' — in a holy kiss; by a holy kiss. Both translation options are possible, although they differ. The first emphasizes that the greeting is not contained in the kiss itself, meaning the kiss is not properly the greeting itself, but accompanies it (a logical explanation as to why there are insincere kisses as such — that is, a true greeting may not be in the kiss itself; recall the kiss of Judas the betrayer). The second option emphasizes that the kiss is properly the greeting, meaning it is always sincere in the words of the apostle. Since a holy kiss is in one way or another sincere, it contains a greeting within itself regardless of which option is intended by the Apostle Paul.

1 Corinthians XVI, 24 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Christ Jesus.

Conclusion of the Epistle.
Paul greets the addressees, encourages demonstrating love and respect, particularly to workers in the Lord's field, and reminds of the Second Coming of Jesus Christ.
A friendly greeting is called a holy kiss. For a deeper look at kisses, see the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Kiss.html
The Epistle was written under dictation, and at the end Paul signed it with his own hand as a sign of the correctness of what was written.
Regarding anathema at the end — this is the only Epistle with such a conclusion. All faithful ought to manifest at least in a small measure heartfelt love for Jesus Christ, the Savior and Deliverer, the Lord. And if there is not even a drop of that sincere love, then they are not Christians; thus, having the appearance of faith, they renounce it, and therefore will not be part of the Church (which is what the word anathema means in the proper sense). Paul in this way reminds that if anyone among Christians does not love Jesus even a little, they will be excommunicated from the Church (will separate themselves by their own will). That is, this is not a curse, but a warning and a reminder.
He concludes the Epistle with a wish for grace and an assurance of love for the addressees of the letter.
