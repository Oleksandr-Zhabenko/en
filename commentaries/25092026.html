(Ephesians IV, 17-25)
Ephesians IV, 17 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. 'ἐν ματαιότητι' - 'en mataioteti' — in vanity.

Ephesians IV, 18 — 'ἐν αὐτοῖς' — 'en autois' — in them. Where.

Ephesians IV, 19 — 'εἰς ἐργασίαν' — 'eis ergasian' — unto lasciviousness / uncleanness; into wantonness. Indicates the direction of falling, decay. 'ἐν πλεονεξίᾳ' - 'en pleonexia' — in greediness / covetousness. In what.

Ephesians IV, 21 — 'ἐν αὐτῷ' — 'en auto' — in Him; by Him. Both translation options are possible; the first emphasizes that Jesus is the Example for learning, whilst the second — that He Himself teaches the faithful. 'ἐν τῷ Ἰησοῦ' - 'en to Iesou' — in Jesus.

Ephesians IV, 24 — 'ἐν δικαιοσύνῃ καὶ ὁσιότητι τῆς ἀληθείας' — 'en dikaiosyne kai hosioteti tes aletheias' — in righteousness and true holiness; by righteousness and holiness of truth. Rather the first option, although the second is also possible. The first speaks of a person being created, developing, living righteously and holily, whereas the second speaks of a person being called to righteousness and holiness, because they were created in such conformity with righteousness and holiness. In both cases, it speaks of human calling to righteousness and holiness, because this leads to creation and life, to the life-giving performed by God.

Paul speaks once again about each Christian individually and about personal piety, utilizing the vivid image of putting on and taking off garments.

The image is vivid, but requires deeper examination to be properly understood. One must take as a foundation that the Incarnation of the Son of God is also compared in Scripture with His being clothed — The Lord reigned, He is clothed with majesty; the Lord is clothed with strength, wherewith He has girded Himself (Psalm XCII, 1). Here under majesty (beauty) and strength is named the human nature of the Incarnate Son of God; one can even compare the soul with beauty, and the body with strength.

On the renewal in the spirit of the mind, it is finely written at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/10092023.html

In view of all this, one can see that Paul implies a constant renewal of human life according to God's grace and what the Lord intended regarding human nature. It does not mean some kind of acting, a mask in the form of piety that hides the real essence of a person's life, but refers to a complete renewal of the entire life of a person according to God's will by the gifts of grace in God.

A person cannot become holy by themselves, solely by their own powers, but neither will God transfigure a person without their active participation; therefore, cooperation, synergy of God and human being is needed in all of this.

Factually, a clear call and vocation to the general holiness of all the faithful is also visible here. Yes, indeed, we are all called to become and be holy and righteous through the Lord Jesus Christ.

(Mark XII, 1-12)
Mark XII, 1 — 'ἐν παραβολαῖς λαλεῖν' — 'en parabolais lalein' — to speak in parables; to speak by parables. Both options are possible, complementing each other.

Mark XII, 2 — 'παρὰ τῶν γεωργῶν' — 'para ton georgon' — from the husbandmen (vinedressers). The preposition 'para' here is with the genitive case; the same construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ, regarding the Pre-Eternal Council of the Holy Trinity and all God's design; here it indicates that these were proper actions of the owner. 'ἀπὸ τῶν καρπῶν τοῦ ἀμπελῶνος' — 'apo ton karpon tou ampelonos' — of the fruit of the vineyard. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that the owner wanted only to take a certain portion of their fruits, that is, he acted entirely as an investor.

Mark XII, 10 — 'ἐγενήθη εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας' — 'egenethe eis kephalen gonias' — is become the head of the corner. The preposition 'eis' indicates transformation (into what) here.

Mark XII, 11 — 'παρὰ κυρίου' — 'para kyriou' — from the Lord; by the Lord. The preposition 'para' here is with the genitive case; the same construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ, regarding the Pre-Eternal Council of the Holy Trinity and all God's design; here it indicates that the suffering of the Son of God was foreseen by God for salvation, similarly to His glorification after the Resurrection. I will add that it is known that this parable is about the elders of Israel and their followers, who persecuted many prophets and ultimately reached deicide — they crucified Jesus Christ, the Son of God. The servants and the son in the parable depict the prophets and Jesus.

Although the parable itself pertains specifically to the elders of the nation of Israel and their followers, it serves as a warning for all, because similar temptations arose in later times as well — for instance, many followers of Jesus Christ were also persecuted. The parable warns everyone against that, and also testifies that the Lord is a Righteous Judge.

It is also visible what prompted people to persecute God's faithful and Christ Himself — the desire to appropriate what belongs to God, which is factually a synonym for pride. From pride originate many other sins, and pride itself became the primary cause of the fall; therefore the Lord teaches us not to be proud, but to emulate the humble.

(1 Corinthians XIV, 20-25)
1 Corinthians XIV, 21 — 'ἐν τῷ νόμῳ' — 'en to nomo' — in the Law. Where. 'ἐν ἑτερογλώσσοις καὶ ἐν χείλεσιν ἑτέρων' - 'en heteroglossis kai en kheilesin heteron' — by other tongues and with other lips; in other languages and with other lips. Both translation options are possible, although the second is more direct and therefore more appropriate, as it refers to God's desire to be heard by people for the sake of their salvation and eternal life.

1 Corinthians XIV, 22 — 'εἰς σημεῖον' — 'eis semeion' — for a sign. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal here: for what.

1 Corinthians XIV, 24 — 'ὑπὸ πάντων' — 'hypo panton' — of all / by all. The preposition 'hypo' here is with the genitive case and signifies that judgment and conviction become a part of a person's life through other people.

1 Corinthians XIV, 25 — 'ἐν ὑμῖν' — 'en hymin' — in you / among you. Where, among whom.

The quotation from the Law is Isaiah XXVIII, 11 (in a distinct recension).

Since experience is sometimes negative and can contribute to sins, many Corinthians thought that it was better to be inexperienced in order not to sin. The Apostle Paul says that it is better not to have experience that leads, prompts, or teaches one to sin; instead, it is better to have experience of a reasonable, good, pious, and devout life — and the more of such experience, the better, without limit.
Diverse good experience, knowledge, and skills contribute to the spread of faith and to salvation; therefore, this is one of the means that the Lord can use to address a person better (an indirect address through this experience, knowledge, skills, etc.). On the other hand, people impenitent in evil reject even this if they remain impenitent.
An example of wisdom in faith is when prophecies are used properly, as is speaking in different languages. It is immediately apparent that God's gifts must be used wisely for the common good and salvation.
Pre-eminence in the Church belongs to what is truly good for many or for all. Paul's example of a person's conversion from hearing prophecies is eloquent. Coming to faith can occur in various ways, and it can happen in such a miraculous way as well.
It must be said that the efficacy of prophesying by all in the Church is explained not by their exposing someone, but by their managing to find a way to the heart, to its secrets, to the movements of the heart, etc. That is, one must understand that in the time of the Apostle Paul, there was no mass culture such as has existed for a long time now; every Gentile man or woman thought in many cases differently, all being human beings. Therefore, they often did not understand one another, often hid the secrets of their heart, and shared only a fraction of them — not as people today discuss tastes and certain details of events, films, music, etc. Therefore, the success of prophesying lay not in passing judgment on someone, but in the faithful, by the action of the Holy Spirit, being able to understand the hearts of other people, being open, and leading others to sincerity. And many Gentiles sought this sincerity, which became the cause of their conversion.
