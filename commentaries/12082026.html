Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians III, 4-11)
2 Corinthians III, 4 — ‘πεποίθησιν’ — ‘pepoithesin’ — confidence, trust, boldness.

2 Corinthians III, 5 — 'ἀφ’ ἑαυτῶν' — 'aph heauton' — from ourselves. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following 'he' indicates here that it refers to the desire to make a true objective statement about oneself. 'ὡς ἐξ ἑαυτῶν' - 'hos ex heauton' — as out of ourselves. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following 'he' indicates the source of origin of a true objective statement — the deep truth about oneself is learned from God. 'ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ' - 'ek tou theou' — from God. The preposition 'ek' indicates here the source of origin.

2 Corinthians III, 7, 8, 11 — 'ἐν γράμμασιν ἐντετυπωμένῃ' — 'en grammasin entetypomene' — engraved in letters; carved in letters. By what, in what form. 'ἐν δόξῃ' - 'en doxe' — in glory; glorious. Set expression with such meaning. 'εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον Μωϋσέως' - 'eis to prosopon Moyseos' — at (into, onto) the face of Moses. The preposition 'eis' indicates here where the children of Israel (Israelites) looked.

2 Corinthians III, 10 — 'ἐν τούτῳ τῷ μέρει' — 'en touto to merei' — in this respect. In what. Resembles a more journalistic style of speech.

The Apostle Paul compares God's gifts in the Old and the New Testament, pointing out that the latter will not end and will not yield to anything else; it is eternal and definitive. Paul testifies that the ability to be a letter of recommendation of Christ is possessed by the apostles and all truly faithful Christians not through thoughts about themselves, but from God as the Source. This implies a stronger statement: that people's true ability to be God's servants is determined not by their own evaluation of whether they are indeed such, but by God through His gift. Further, Paul compares the Old Testament and New Testament ministries through the image of the letter and the Spirit. By the Spirit, one must understand specifically the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the Giver of Life, and this is a citation from there. The image becomes clearer the more we reflect on the properties of letters and the Holy Spirit. Letters are written, material, human, obvious and understandable to some, and incomprehensible to others. But the Holy Spirit gives life (makes alive); He is immaterial and spiritual in the highest degree, whilst matter is created by Him with the Father and the Son; He is the meaning of knowing God (particularly through Scripture), for this knowledge takes place through Him, in Him, and for Him (not only for Him alone, but for the Father and the Son as well); He also bestows other gifts. Since the letter is inanimate, serving the letter does not give life, does not save, and is called to put sin to death without possessing the full power to do so. In this connection it is called the ministry of death. But such ministry of the Old Testament has its significance, on which Paul does not dwell in detail here, but which is discussed more in his other epistles. See in particular at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15062026.html

Paul testifies that the ministry of the Spirit is far more glorious. Indeed, in the New Testament one can see the glory of God much more distinctly than in the Old, and this applies to almost everything.
