Liturgy:
(Ephesians I, 7-17)
Ephesians I, 7, 11, 13 — 'ἐν ᾧ' — 'en ho' — in Whom. In Whom.

Ephesians I, 8 — 'εἰς ἡμᾶς' — 'eis hemas' — toward us; for us. Where to and for whom. The first option signifies that God multiplied wisdom in human nature, in people through Christ, whilst the second — that it is granted to people and for their sake. 'ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ καὶ φρονήσει' - 'en pase sophia kai phronesei' — in all wisdom and prudence; with all wisdom and understanding. Both translation options are possible.

Ephesians I, 9, 10 — 'ἐν αὐτῷ' — 'en auto' — in Him; in Himself. Rather the first translation option, although the second is also possible. Then it refers to the Council of the Most Holy Trinity. See also about the nuances of translating similar turns at the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

Ephesians I, 10, 12 — 'εἰς οἰκονομίαν' — 'eis oikonomian' — for the dispensation; unto the dispensation. Rather the second. The first indicates the weaving of Divine Revelation into the overall picture. 'ἐν τῷ Χριστῷ' - 'en to CHristo' — in Christ. In Whom. See more at the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

Ephesians I, 11 — 'προορισθέντες' — 'prooristhentes' — being foreordained; being limited (in a broad sense) beforehand. Here it does not mean that the Lord chose and appointed beforehand who would be saved and who would not, but means that He designed and established beforehand the inheritance of God's grace and eternal life by humans. That is, He did not appoint beforehand the people themselves, but that wealth of His goodness which He manifests to people in all times, and especially in the Coming of Christ (the First, but also the future Second). God does not replace human freedom with His determination; if someone does not accept God's will, then God does not force them to do so, but such a person deprives themselves of unity with God. 'κατὰ τὴν βουλὴν τοῦ θελήματος αὐτοῦ' — literally: according to the counsel (conscious wisdom) of His will — that is, it refers to the Trinitarian pre-eternal Council concerning the creation of the world and the Incarnation of the Son of God. The natural will in the Trinity is one for the Three Hypostases, but Those Who will are Three. This leads to the fact that although the will is one, it can also be said to be a certain dialogue, a certain Council, whence the name.

Ephesians I, 12 — 'εἰς τὸ εἶναι' — 'eis to einai' — that we should be; for being. For what. 'εἰς ἔπαινον δόξης' - 'eis epainon doxes' — to the praise of His glory; for the praise of glory. For what. See more about praise and glory at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Do-you-see-birds-lilies-divine-beauty-and-care.pdf
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/21062026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/Beauty.html
Here glory is understood as uncreated (God's energy), and praise — creation's participation in it.

Ephesians I, 14 — 'εἰς ἀπολύτρωσιν' — 'eis apolytrosin' — unto the redemption; for redemption. 'εἰς ἔπαινον τῆς δόξης' - 'eis epainon tes doxes' — unto the praise of His glory. Everywhere here 'eis' indicates the goal of the action. See just above regarding verse 12.

Ephesians I, 15 — 'ἐν τῷ κυρίῳ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en to kyrio Iesou' — in the Lord Jesus. Faith not in Whom, but what kind of faith, filled with life in Whom. 'εἰς πάντας' - 'eis pantas' — unto all; directed toward all. Here the preposition 'eis' indicates the direction of love (toward whom).

Ephesians I, 16 — 'προσευχῶν' — 'proseukhon' — in my prayers (prayers; the word signifies elevated, high prayers).

Ephesians I, 17 — 'ἐν ἐπιγνώσει' — 'en epignosei' — in the knowledge; by knowledge. Signifies that the knowledge of God fills a person and unites them with God. See at the link:
https://gemini.google.com/share/901c8daff8ce

(Mark VIII, 1-10)
Mark VIII, 1 — 'Ἐν ἐκείναις ταῖς ἡμέραις' — 'En ekeinais tais hemerais' — In those days. When. Set expression.

Mark VIII, 3 — 'εἰς οἶκον' — 'eis oikon' — to their own house. Where to. Singular stands instead of the expected plural. 'ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ' - 'en te hodo' — by the way; on the way. Where. 'ἀπὸ μακρόθεν' - 'apo makrothen' — from far. How. Set expression, which also emphasizes that they left their places.

Mark VIII, 6 — 'εὐχαριστήσας' — 'eucharistesas' — offered thanksgiving. Here an important parallel is also with the Eucharist, when the Holy Gifts can grant spiritual food to many people.

Mark VIII, 10 — 'εἰς τὸ πλοῖον' — 'eis to ploion' — into the ship. Where to. 'εἰς τὰ μέρη Δαλμανουθά' - 'eis ta mere Dalmanoutha' — into the parts of Dalmanutha. Where to.
