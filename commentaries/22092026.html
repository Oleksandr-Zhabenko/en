Liturgy:
(Ephesians II, 19-III, 7)
Ephesians II, 20 — 'θεμελίῳ' — 'themelio' — foundation.

Ephesians II, 21, 22 — 'ἐν ᾧ' — 'en ho' — in Whom. In Whom. 'εἰς ναὸν ἅγιον ἐν κυρίῳ' - 'eis naon hagion en kyrio' — into an holy temple in the Lord. The preposition 'eis' indicates movement and growth — where to. That is, it speaks of the sanctification and divinisation of the faithful.

Ephesians II, 21 — 'συναρμολογουμένη' — 'synarmologoumene' — fitly framed together; assembled together from separate parts to form a single whole. Here it is emphasized that the faithful are diverse, being distinct members of the Church, but within her they receive what is necessary for growth, sanctification, and divinisation, in a certain measure being assimilated to the Most Holy Trinity.

Ephesians II, 22 — 'συνοικοδομεῖσθε' — 'synoikodomeisthe' — are builded together; built together from various materials. Thus Paul emphasizes that the earthly Church is formed of different people with diverse personal histories. 'εἰς κατοικητήριον τοῦ θεοῦ ἐν πνεύματι' - 'eis katoiketerion tou theou en pneumati' — for an habitation of God through the Spirit / in the Spirit. Analogous to verse 21 above.

Ephesians III, 2 — 'εἰς ὑμᾶς' — 'eis hymas' — to you-ward; for you. Rather the second in sense. Paul received the gift of grace not among the Gentiles, nor among Christians who were formerly Gentiles, but for them, in order to lead them to Christ. 'οἰκονομίαν τῆς χάριτος' - 'oikonomian tes kharitos' — dispensation / administration of grace. One could say disposition of grace. Paul implies that the apostles (and likewise their successors) received grace for application in building up the Church, for the growth of the faithful in God (including the apostles themselves and their successors).

Ephesians III, 3 — 'ἐν ὀλίγῳ' — 'en oligo' — in few words; briefly. Set expression.

Ephesians III, 4 — 'ἐν τῷ μυστηρίῳ τοῦ Χριστοῦ' — 'en to mysterio tou CHristou' — in the mystery of Christ. Paul implies that his understanding is partial relative to the entire mystery of Christ, which is greater than all human comprehension, as if encompassing Paul's understanding, which reveals a part of it; but he also speaks of how the faithful understand Paul also according to this mystery, that is, their participation is likewise a participation in this mystery.

Ephesians III, 5 — 'ἐν πνεύματι' — 'en pneumati' — in the Spirit; by the Spirit. Both translation options are possible, with the first indicating the abiding of the apostles and prophets in the Holy Spirit, whilst the second indicates the mode of Revelation — revealed by the Spirit Himself.

Ephesians III, 6 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Christ Jesus.

A rich passage, hence many words and phrases above. I will comment briefly.

On fellowcitizens / household of God, there is a fine parable on the Trinity and Christ at the link:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/Parable.html

Paul calls the mystery of Christ the joint participation of the faithful from all nations in God's plan for the salvation of humanity through Christ. In fact, the spread of faith in Christ among various nations was the very focus of his apostolic labor. Therefore, a significant portion of Paul's personal stance is present here.
