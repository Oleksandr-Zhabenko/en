Liturgy:
(Philippians IV, 10-23)
Philippians IV, 10 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord; by the Lord. Rather the first translation option applies, since the second is not entirely clear in sense.

Philippians IV, 11 — 'ἐν οἷς' — 'en hois' — in whatsoever state I am / in that which; by that which. Both translation options are possible, with the first emphasizing the acceptance of the situation and labour for the Lord and people in various circumstances, whilst the second emphasizes joy in the good in various, even difficult circumstances.

Philippians IV, 12 — 'ἐν παντὶ καὶ ἐν πᾶσιν' — 'en panti kai en pasin' — everywhere and in all things.

Philippians IV, 13 — 'ἐν τῷ ἐνδυναμοῦντί' — 'en to endynamounti' — through [Christ which] strengthens me / in [Him Who] strengthens.

Philippians IV, 15 — 'ἐν ἀρχῇ' — 'en arkhe' — in the beginning. Set expression. 'ἀπὸ Μακεδονίας' - 'apo Makedonias' — from Macedonia. The preposition 'apo' indicates here that Paul then completely departed Macedonia. 'εἰς λόγον δόσεως καὶ λήμψεως' - 'eis logon doseos kai lempseos' — concerning giving and receiving / regarding the matter of giving and receiving.

Philippians IV, 16 — 'ἐν Θεσσαλονίκῃ' — 'en Thessalonike' — in Thessalonica. Where. 'εἰς τὴν χρείαν' - 'eis ten khreian' — unto my necessity / for my needs. Here the preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action.

Philippians IV, 17 — 'εἰς λόγον ὑμῶν' — 'eis logon hymon' — to your account / for your matter. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal here.

Philippians IV, 18 — 'παρὰ Ἐπαφροδίτου' — 'para Epaphroditou' — from beside Epaphroditus. 'παρ’ ὑμῶν' - 'par hymon' — from beside you. The preposition 'para' here takes the genitive case in both instances. Such a construction is used regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father, regarding the Most Holy Trinity, regarding the entire God's design, as well as regarding the human nature of Jesus Christ. Here it indicates the value of the gifts and the fact that they were from the Holy Spirit, bestowed and delivered by His prompting and will, as well as according to God's design.

Philippians IV, 19 — 'ἐν δόξῃ ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en doxe en CHristo Iesou' — in glory by Christ Jesus / in glory in Christ Jesus.

Philippians IV, 20 — 'εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων· ἀμήν' — 'eis tous aionas ton aionon amen' — for ever and ever. Amen / unto the ages of ages. Amen. Set expression.

Philippians IV, 21 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ' — 'en CHristo Iesou' — in Christ Jesus. In Whom.

Philippians IV, 22 — 'ἐκ τῆς Καίσαρος οἰκίας' — 'ek tes Kaisaros oikias' — they that are of Caesar's household. The preposition 'ek' indicates here from where those people are.

The Apostle Paul writes extensively (in several sentences) that the Philippians became the only Church in Macedonia that sent him gifts to support his mission, that he is grateful to them, and prays for God's blessing upon them. In doing so, Paul writes that he rejoices not in the received gifts, but because this offering of the Philippians is their glory in Christ. Paul himself says that he can live and continue his mission without this valuable assistance, for as an apostle in Christ he is accustomed to living in various conditions — favourable or unfavourable — giving thanks to God for what He bestows through Paul's labour and the help of people, yet he rejoices precisely in that Gospel participation in the work of Christ for the salvation of people and the knowledge of the truth.
I would note that Paul is not afraid to praise the Philippians for their special beneficence to the glory of God, yet he does not attribute it as a special merit. It is good to glorify God, but it can be dangerous to view this glorification as a merit through the temptation of pride.
Here we also see a lesson in being content with what God provides. It is often thought that being content with what one has means not striving for more, not desiring growth and development. This is a misconception. Contentment means the absence of inner (or also outer) protest that this is too little!, this is not enough for me!, etc., whilst remaining open to receiving more and moving forward and upward. It is good that this exists, and we shall move further! Without contentment in this sense, happiness is impossible; it is itself in a certain measure happiness. One must also remember that God in this sense is All-Content; the Most Holy Trinity has no need of anything outside Themselves.

(Luke VII, 36-50)
The parable itself and its explanation by Jesus show that the Lord manifested mercy both to Simon and to that repentant sinful woman, but Simon lived piously and therefore did not feel a profound need for repentance, for the forgiveness of sins. In the parable he is in the image of a debtor of 50 denarii, whilst the sinful woman is in the image of a debtor of 500 denarii. At the same time, although Simon conducted himself well, Christ had remarks concerning him. Christ noted relationships that were not as close, small yet real lapses and sins (which become all the more significant when it comes to deep personal relationships). People are often inclined not to notice small sins, whereas in reality they stand in need of deep love and gratitude.
