Liturgy:
(Philippians I, 12-20)
Philippians I, 12 — 'εἰς προκοπὴν τοῦ εὐαγγελίου' — 'eis prokopen tou euangeliou' — unto the furtherance of the gospel; for the progress of the Gospel. Here too the preposition 'eis' indicates the goal. It refers to the spread, the acceptance of the Gospel for the salvation and sanctification of people.

Philippians I, 13 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ' — 'en CHristo' — in Christ. 'ἐν ὅλῳ τῷ πραιτωρίῳ' - 'en holo to praitorio' — in all the palace [praetorium]; throughout the whole praetorium (that is, in the palace in Jerusalem seized by the Romans, or in the units of the praetorians, the Roman guards, in Rome).

Philippians I, 14 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. It refers to brothers in the Lord. 'περισσοτέρως τολμᾶν ἀφόβως' - 'perissoteros tolman aphobos' — much more bold to speak the word without fear; exceedingly / extraordinarily bold and fearless. These words testify to several things:
1. Paul did not expect such inspiration for preaching among the faithful through his imprisonment for Christ;
2. Paul might have thought of a more moderate way of preaching for the faithful, but they on the contrary displayed zeal and boldness.
One can put it this way from the whole description (today's and further): in a word (or two), the preaching was fervent, passionate.
The example of boldness and courage of Paul himself as an apostle inspired others to similar efforts, especially the situation with Paul's imprisonment, of which he writes. In the history of the Church there were many such cases, particularly very often such mentions occur in descriptions of the sufferings of holy martyrs for Christ.
Also here is an important lesson for all — one must preach with faith, also boldly, but wisdom too, of which Paul speaks implicitly, must be shown by displaying reason in everything.

Philippians I, 16 — 'οἱ μὲν ἐξ ἀγάπης' — 'hoi men ex agapes' — the one preach Christ of love. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel here indicates the true cause of their actions — love, commanded by Christ. 'εἰς ἀπολογίαν τοῦ εὐαγγελίου' - 'eis apologian tou euangeliou' — for the defence of the Gospel. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal.

Philippians I, 17 — 'οἱ δὲ ἐξ ἐριθείας' — 'hoi de ex eritheias' — but the other of contention / ambition. The preposition 'ek' in its form before a following vowel here indicates the true cause of their actions. 'θλίψιν ἐγείρειν τοῖς δεσμοῖς μου' - 'thlipsin egeirein tois desmois mou' — literally to add affliction to my bonds. I think Paul says here that insincere preachers attempt to preach out of personal ambitions, whilst taking no account of the fact that by their insincere preaching they can increase (cause additionally) persecution and intensify Paul's own suffering for Christ.

See also another passage where Paul more deeply unfolds God's design concerning zeal and envy:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/03072025.html

Philippians I, 18 — 'ἐν τούτῳ' — 'en touto' — herein; in this. Both translation options are possible.

Philippians I, 19 — 'εἰς σωτηρίαν' — 'eis soterian' — to my salvation; for salvation. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal here.

Philippians I, 20 — 'ἐν οὐδενὶ αἰσχυνθήσομαι, ἀλλ’ ἐν πάσῃ παρρησίᾳ' — 'en oudeni aiskhynthesomai, all en pase parresia' — in nothing I shall be ashamed, but with all boldness / in full confidence. Various translation options are possible due to the polysemy of the word parresia. 'ἐν τῷ σώματί μου' - 'en to somati mou' — in my body. Here it refers to human service in the body — and the apostle expresses faith that his devotion to Christ and persistent labour will lead to the glorification of Christ in all his life.
The Apostle Paul says that by his example he inspired others boldly to preach Christ, and this will serve Paul himself unto salvation through the prayers of the faithful for him and by the action of Jesus Christ Himself and the Holy Spirit.
Here also an important thought is that if a person acts in the Holy Spirit, properly, then even if someone else attempts to use this for their own interests with a different purpose, this will not nullify the correctness and holiness of the actions of that first person, nor reduce them to nothing, but the correctness and holiness will be preserved by the Holy Spirit Himself.
Here everything depends on the Holy Spirit — whether He truly leads the person to those actions or not. If He leads, then such actions are holy and proper, as was just said above. But if actions look good, yet it is not the Holy Spirit leading to them Himself, then such seemingly good actions are most likely not truly good in reality, and that first person needs to act with great caution, discernment, and wisdom with a view to their possible use by someone else for another purpose — all to avoid becoming simply a victim of their own heedlessness, shortsightedness, and naivety. And main of all — one must seek what is truly God's will.
In the case of Paul's preaching, he testifies that it is the Holy Spirit acting through Him, and therefore he rejoices.

(Luke V, 33-39)
Luke V, 34 — 'ἐν ᾧ' — 'en ho' — while; as long as. Set expression.

Luke V, 35 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτῶν' — 'ap auton' — from them. That is, they shall take away completely. 'ἐν ἐκείναις ταῖς ἡμέραις' - 'en ekeinais tais hemerais' — in those days. Set expression with the meaning then.

Luke V, 36 — 'ἀπὸ ἱματίου' — 'apo himatiou' — of a garment / from a garment. Indicates a complete tear. 'ἀπὸ τοῦ καινοῦ' - 'apo tou kainou' — from the new. Likewise.

Luke V, 37, 38 — 'εἰς ἀσκοὺς' — 'eis askous' — into winebottles / wineskins. Where to.

Regarding the patch and regarding the wineskins — the Lord implies that between the old attitude to piety (ritualistic) and the Gospel there are noticeable differences, so that it is impossible to contain both well. And since the disciples are called to renewal in Christ, in the New Covenant, Christ in no way demands of them that which might even harm them in this main thing. A fine example also for the gradual learning of the new life together with Christ — one must prepare for renewal, rather than reformatting the old. For true virtue is not a reformatted old way of life that was not virtuous.
