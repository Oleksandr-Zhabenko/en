As advice regarding reading what is written — one can read the verse in translation or/and original (whoever has such possibility), and then the corresponding comment regarding prepositions here. Then it is necessary to understand which part of the verse the comment concerns, and also to consider what essential for understanding it affirms — or more rarely — denies. Such thoughtful reading helps to deepen understanding and protects from the mentioned mistakes.

Of Sunday after the Exaltation:
(Galatians II, 16-20)
Galatians II, 16 — 'ἐξ ἔργων' — 'ex ergon' — by the works / out of works. That is, that righteousness is a consequence of works, and not of God's grace. 'εἰς Χριστὸν Ἰησοῦν' - 'eis CHriston Iesoun' — in(to) Jesus Christ. The preposition 'eis' indicates here that true faith leads to Christ, Who is its Goal. Galatians II, 17 — 'ἐν Χριστῷ' — 'en CHristo' — in Christ. Emphasizes that Christ is the Source and Cause of justification.

Galatians II, 20 — 'ἐν ἐμοί' — 'en emoi' — in me. 'ἐν σαρκί' - 'en sarki' — in the flesh. Meaning — with the weaknesses of nature. 'ἐν πίστει' - 'en pistei' — in faith; by faith. Both translation options are possible; the first emphasizes that faith encompasses the whole life of the apostle and the faithful, whilst the second — that it itself is the driving force of life.

Similar words are found in the Epistle to the Romans and 2 Epistles to the Corinthians (see particularly at the links above).
