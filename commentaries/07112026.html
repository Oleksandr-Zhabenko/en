Liturgy:
(2 Corinthians VIII, 1-5)
2 Corinthians VIII, 1 — 'ἐν ταῖς ἐκκλησίαις' — 'en tais ekklesiais' — in the churches. Implies that remaining within the Church, participating in church life, is of crucial importance for receiving God's grace.

2 Corinthians VIII, 2 — 'ἐν πολλῇ δοκιμῇ θλίψεως' — 'en polle dokime thlipseos' — in a great trial of affliction / amidst afflictions full of testing. Various translation options are possible, yet overall the core thought is clear. 'εἰς τὸ πλοῦτος τῆς ἁπλότητος αὐτῶν' - 'eis to ploutos tes haplotetos auton' — into the wealth of their generosity. Speaks of the transformation of deep poverty through grace and human goodwill into the wealth of generosity — generosity becomes rich not by the magnitude of the gifts, but through self-sacrificial devotion in hardship.

2 Corinthians VIII, 3 — 'παρὰ δύναμιν αὐθαίρετοι' — 'para dynamin autharetoi' — not by their own ability to choose that voluntarily. The preposition 'para' here takes the accusative case. It speaks of that which exceeds the desire for good in a person, that is, that this was accomplished not merely by virtue of a person's good desire and strength, but also beyond their strength and desire, as something not fully subject to the person themselves, that is, what goes beyond the limits of the power of freedom, the power of voluntariness, that is, voluntariness to such a degree that it became a power.

2 Corinthians VIII, 4 — 'τῆς εἰς τοὺς ἁγίους' — 'tes eis tous hagious' — that which is for the saints. The preposition 'eis' here indicates the direction of the service, for whom it is intended.

The Apostle Paul here praises the Macedonians to the Corinthians, in particular the Thessalonians, setting them forth as an example of generosity, with full committal of themselves to God and a manifestation of faithfulness to the apostles and other churches.
Paul underlines that such self-sacrificial devotion originates from God's grace, or rather from its abundance and wealth, and he rejoices together with them. It must be remembered that all good things which people, and the faithful in particular, receive come through the action of God's grace; yet not every gift of God is grace itself. Nevertheless, as I wrote earlier, the word grace is at least sometimes used in a broad sense in the New Testament, signifying various divine gifts, not exclusively Divine uncreated energy, which is most often denoted by this word in the New Testament.

(Luke VIII, 16-21)
Luke VIII, 17 — 'εἰς φανερὸν' — 'eis phaneron' — abroad / into manifestation. Where to. Speaks of that which will become revealed and known.

Luke VIII, 18 — 'ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ' — 'ap autou' — from him. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following vowel emphasizes that what is taken away will be removed entirely, completely.
