Liturgy:
(1 Thessalonians II, 20-III, 8 )
1 Thessalonians III, 1 — 'ἐν Ἀθήναις μόνοι' — 'en Athenais monoi' — at Athens alone. Where.

1 Thessalonians III, 2 — 'ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ τοῦ Χριστοῦ' — 'en to euangelio tou CHristou' — in the gospel of Christ; by the gospel of Christ. Both translation options are possible, although the first has the preference — the first underlines participation in spreading the faith in Christ through preaching and life, whereas the second points out that Timothy collaborates with God by this preaching. 'εἰς τὸ στηρίξαι ὑμᾶς καὶ παρακαλέσαι' - 'eis to sterixai hymas kai parakalesai' — to establish you, and to comfort you. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action here.

1 Thessalonians III, 3 — 'ἐν ταῖς θλίψεσιν ταύταις' — 'en tais thlipsesin tautais' — by these afflictions; in these persecutions. In what, in what circumstances. 'εἰς τοῦτο κείμεθα' - 'eis touto keimetha' — we are placed for this; into this we are placed. Here is the passive voice placed. The second translation option appears better, as the first contains within itself the thought of contrasting the activity of the faithful, in particular the apostles and Paul himself — as if God placed the faithful into a state of persecutions, while they themselves took no part in it and did not want it (then it turns out that the desire of God and of the faithful oppose one another, which implies sinfulness, rather than the testimony of the faithfulness of the apostles and Christians in hardships for the faith). Instead, the second option does not carry the sense of God's goal — it speaks only of what God permitted and permits persecutions, but in no way wants the faithful to be persecuted and hunted. This latter best accords with the words of the Lord's Prayer, as well as with the faith of the Church in general. See in particular at the links:
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/21022026.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/01032025.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/en/commentaries/15072023.html
https://oleksandr-zhabenko.github.io/uk/commentaries/vystup-2025-hypo-genitive-Romans-XIII_1.pdf
Also, one needs to understand the whole verse in context, together with the preceding and following ones, and not separately. The subsequent verse 4 shows that the apostles spoke beforehand of how there might be sufferings, that is, they prepared the disciples to be ready if (when) they come. Such readiness allows one to look realistically at the state of affairs.
If you wish to know more, please write to me.

1 Thessalonians III, 5 — 'εἰς τὸ γνῶναι τὴν πίστιν ὑμᾶς' — 'eis to gnonai ten pistin hymon' — to know your faith. The preposition 'eis' indicates the goal of the action here. 'εἰς κενὸν' - 'eis keinon' — in vain. Set expression.

1 Thessalonians III, 6 — 'ἀφ' ὑμῶν' — 'aph hymon' — from you. The preposition 'apo' in its form before a following 'hy' indicates here that Timothy left the Thessalonians, arriving to Paul.

1 Thessalonians III, 8 — 'ἐν κυρίῳ' — 'en kyrio' — in the Lord. In Whom you stand.

(Luke XI, 29-33)
Luke XI, 31 — 'ἐγερθήσεται ἐν τῇ κρίσει μετὰ τῶν ἀνδρῶν τῆς γενεᾶς ταύτης' — 'egerthesetai en te krisei meta ton andron tes geneas tautes' — shall rise up in the judgment with the men of this generation. That is, through comparison with those who rejected Christ and judged Him, that queen, having not known Christ, will receive praise and glory, as if she will rise up as a person (although the bodily resurrection will have already occurred earlier by that time), as one faithful to God. 'ἐκ τῶν περάτων τῆς γῆς' - 'ek ton peraton tes ges' — from the utmost parts of the earth. That is, from distant places that were inhabited by people at that time.

Luke XI, 32 — 'ἀναστήσονται ἐν τῇ κρίσει μετὰ τῆς γενεᾶς ταύτης' — 'anastesontai en te krisei meta tes geneas tautes' — shall rise up in the judgment with this generation. Here this thought is underlined even more, for those Ninevites were sinners before that and repented. The first word in the previous verse means to stand up from a sitting or lying position, that is, to receive glory, having had an insignificant position before. And the second even more clearly means to rise again — the Ninevites, dead through sins, repented through the preaching of Jonah and at the judgment will rise again not simply by standing up, but as if rising again, having become God's children. Christ's salvation extends not only to the faithful, but to all people. 'μετενόησαν εἰς τὸ κήρυγμα Ἰωνᾶ' - 'metenoesan eis to kerygma Iona' — repented at the preaching of Jonah; changed their mind into the preaching of Jonah. That is, the preaching led them into repentance. And they changed their mind towards it, in order to enter into it as repented and renewed. A parallel to the Lord's Prayer, but reversed — an incentive to enter into repentance, hence — righteousness and holiness.

Luke XI, 33 — 'εἰς κρύπτην' — 'eis krypten' — into a secret place. Where to. 'ὑπὸ τὸν μόδιον' - 'hypo ton podion' — under a bushel (by which dry measures, in particular grain, were measured). The preposition 'hypo' here takes the accusative case and indicates under what a candle is not placed (this measuring basket is not at all intended for a candle). This part of the verse is absent in some ancient manuscripts of the Gospel.

One can also say that the Lord will judge people according to their openness to God's will — including gentiles. And in this openness, in this search, many gentiles can serve as an example for an evil generation.
